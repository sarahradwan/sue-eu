# LinkedIn distribution mechanics, and the timezone problem

Everything here is subordinate to `linkedin-content-system.md`, which holds measured data
from her own account. Where this file and that file disagree, that file wins. Nothing below
contradicts it, and several parts of it are confirmed by it.

Each claim is labelled. **ESTABLISHED** means LinkedIn has said it publicly or it follows
from how the product provably works. **BELIEVED** means there is a consistent body of
third-party study and a plausible mechanism, but no confirmation. **FOLKLORE** means it is
repeated constantly and there is no good evidence for it in the form people state it.

---

## What actually drives distribution

**ESTABLISHED. The feed is relevance-ranked per viewer, not chronological.** A post is not
broadcast to her followers. It is scored for each viewer and shown if it wins their feed
slot. This is why follower count matters much less than it appears to.

**ESTABLISHED. Dwell time is a ranking input.** LinkedIn's engineering team has published on
using dwell time to improve feed ranking, precisely because likes are a noisy, biased signal
and time spent is not. This single fact explains most of the good advice on this list and
most of the bad advice fails against it.

**ESTABLISHED. Comments weigh more than reactions.** Consistent with dwell time: writing a
comment takes far longer than tapping a like, and it produces text that pulls other people
into the thread.

**ESTABLISHED. Replies extend distribution.** Every reply she writes is a fresh engagement
event on her own post. Replying to all comments in the first two hours is the cheapest reach
available to her and it costs nothing but attention.

**ESTABLISHED. LinkedIn has publicly shifted toward "knowledge and advice" content and away
from viral engagement bait.** Practical effect: a specific professional observation
outperforms a broadly relatable one. This suits her voice rules exactly, which is
convenient, because her rules were arrived at independently.

**CONFIRMED BY HER OWN DATA. Reposts perform terribly.** Her file already says this. It is
consistent with everything above: a repost generates no original text and almost no dwell.

**CONFIRMED BY HER OWN DATA. No-image posts underperform badly, and tagging works.** The
Busuu reply that pushed a post to 2,475 impressions is the mechanism working as designed:
a reply from a tagged account is a high-value engagement event from an account with its own
audience.

## What suppresses reach

**BELIEVED, with an important correction. External links in the post body.** The universal
advice is that links are penalised. LinkedIn representatives have publicly denied a hard
penalty, and link posts do sometimes perform well. The more defensible explanation is
indirect: a link takes the reader off the platform, which ends dwell time on the post, and
dwell time is an established input. So the effect is real but the mechanism is not a
punishment, it is an absence.

**What she should do is unchanged either way:** link in the first comment, or in a reply, or
not at all. The cost of following this rule is zero, so it does not matter which explanation
is right.

**FOLKLORE. Editing after posting destroys reach.** Stated everywhere, confirmed nowhere.
There is no evidence of an edit penalty. What is true is that an edit does not re-trigger
distribution, so a typo fixed at hour three does not get the post a second run.

**Practical rule anyway:** proofread before posting and do not edit in the first hour. Not
because the penalty is proven, but because the upside of an early edit is tiny and the
downside is unquantified. This is a cheap insurance policy, not a law.

**ESTABLISHED. Low early engagement limits distribution.** Not because of a punishment, but
because relevance ranking has nothing to work with. A post nobody engages with gives the
system no evidence to show it more widely.

**BELIEVED, and probably now true. Hashtags do almost nothing for reach.** LinkedIn has wound
down hashtag-based discovery. Treat hashtags as labels for human readers, not as
distribution. Zero to three, or none at all. They are not worth optimising.

## The first 60 to 90 minutes

**FOLKLORE in its precise form. Directionally right underneath.**

There is no verified 60 or 90 minute window, and the specific numbers people quote are
invented. What is true: early engagement is a strong input to whether the post gets shown
more widely, so the first hour or two disproportionately shapes the ceiling.

The more useful and less-repeated fact: **LinkedIn content has a long tail, days rather than
hours**, because the feed is relevance-ranked rather than chronological. A post can still be
picking up impressions on day three. This is very different from Instagram or X, and it
changes what she should do: being available to reply for the first two hours matters, and
writing off a post that started slow does not.

**What to do with it:** post when she can be present for the following two hours. Presence
means replying, not refreshing.

## Formats, for a visual creative

Ranked for her specifically, combining her measured data with general behaviour.

| Format | Verdict | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| **Single strong image, personal, real moment** | Her best format, keep it as the default | Her own measured data. Not a general claim, a specific one about her account |
| **Document / carousel** | The format bet worth making, and underused by her | BELIEVED. Consistent third-party finding, and the mechanism is sound: swiping generates dwell time, and dwell time is ESTABLISHED. Also the natural home for design work |
| **Native video** | Worth testing, LinkedIn has pushed video hard | BELIEVED |
| **Text only** | Avoid | Her own data. No-image posts underperform badly |
| **Articles** | Credibility, low reach, keep pairing with a teaser post | Her own data. Already her practice |
| **Reposts** | Skip entirely | Her own data |

**The carousel is the gap in her current mix.** Everything about her work is visual, sequential
and systematic. A ten-slide document showing three scripts resolving into one typographic
system is a format that generates dwell time by construction and needs no explanation to
either audience.

## Commenting versus posting, at her follower count

This is the question that actually matters for her goal, and the answer is not the usual one.

Her posts are shown mostly to her own network, which is mostly MENA. **Her posting reach and
her audience geography are the same problem.** No amount of posting fixes the geography,
because the distribution starts from the network she already has.

A comment on a European studio's post is shown to *that studio's* audience. For acquiring an
audience in a country where she has no network, commenting is not slightly better than
posting, it is categorically different.

The working model:

- **Comments acquire.** They put her in front of European audiences she cannot otherwise reach.
- **Posts convert.** Someone who saw a sharp comment clicks through, and the post history is
  what makes them stay.
- **The profile closes.** Which is why `linkedin-profile-optimisation.md` comes first in
  priority order.

All three are needed. Only the first one changes her geography.

**Caveat, stated plainly:** her follower count is not recorded in this repo, so the balance
above is reasoning from structure rather than from her numbers. If her following is large
and already partly European, posting does more than this implies.

## The scoreboard

She should track one thing, and LinkedIn gives it away free. Under her analytics, follower
and profile-viewer demographics include **top locations**. That is the direct measurement of
whether the European audience gap is closing.

Baseline it now, before the commenting programme starts, then check monthly. Not weekly.
Monthly, because the number moves slowly and watching it weekly will make her post badly.

## The timezone problem

She is UTC+4 year round, no daylight saving. Her targets move twice a year.

| | Summer, to 25 Oct 2026 | Winter, from 25 Oct 2026 |
|---|---|---|
| Brussels, Amsterdam | UTC+2, two hours behind her | UTC+1, three hours behind |
| Dublin | UTC+1, three hours behind | UTC+0, four hours behind |

### Her Monday and Thursday cadence is already correct

Worth stating because it removes a question: the UAE weekend has been Saturday and Sunday
since 2022, so Monday and Thursday are ordinary working days in Abu Dhabi, Brussels,
Amsterdam and Dublin alike. The days do not need changing. Only the hour does.

### The windows

The design problem is that she needs her MENA network awake to generate the early engagement
that gets the post distributed at all, and her European targets to arrive while it is still
being distributed. Those are not the same hour, and that is fine, because distribution runs
over hours, not minutes.

**Monday, 11:30 Abu Dhabi.** 09:30 Brussels and Amsterdam, 08:30 Dublin.
MENA is mid-morning and fully engaged, which supplies the early signal. Europe arrives at
start of day, an hour and a half into the post's run, while it is still climbing. Avoids the
08:00 European inbox crush, which on a Monday is brutal.

**Thursday, 12:00 Abu Dhabi.** 10:00 Brussels and Amsterdam, 09:00 Dublin.
The strongest single slot of the week. Both European markets fully live, all three cities
inside working hours, and MENA is at pre-lunch peak rather than the Thursday afternoon
wind-down.

**From 25 October, add one hour to both.** Monday 12:30, Thursday 13:00 Abu Dhabi. European
clocks go back, so she has to move later to hit the same local European time.

**The rule that overrides the clock:** post when she can be present to reply for the next
two hours. A perfectly timed post she cannot answer is worse than a well-answered post
thirty minutes late.
