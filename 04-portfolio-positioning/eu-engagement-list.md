# EU engagement list

The working list for closing the European audience gap. Per `linkedin-content-system.md`,
commenting on European institutional and creative profiles is the thing that actually opens
a European audience, and it is simultaneously the lowest-risk tactic available.

**Verification status is marked on every entry.** VERIFIED means checked against sources in
July 2026. LIGHT means one source only. UNVERIFIED means it came from memory and she should
spend two minutes on it before engaging. Nothing here should be asserted as fact in a public
comment without her own check first.

---

## The one risk this list carries

Her content system says a comment is far less likely to reach her boss than a post. True,
because a comment does not get pushed into his feed.

**But comments are listed on her Activity tab, which anyone can browse.** So the exposure is
not zero, it is just passive rather than pushed. Which sets the standard: every comment must
be defensible on its own as professional interest, with no accumulation effect. Thirty
craft comments on European studios reads as a designer who follows European work. Three
comments on European job posts reads as a job hunt.

**Never comment on:** hiring posts, "we're growing" posts, anything with an apply link,
"life at [studio]" recruitment content, or a founder's post about relocating. Those are the
only comment types that are a tell, and they are the ones LinkedIn will most readily suggest
to her.

## Tier 1: EU institutional. Highest value, lowest risk.

### Europa Component Library (ECL): VERIFIED

The official design system for European Commission and European Union websites. Two
component sets, EC and EU. Built to WCAG AA. Public at `ec.europa.eu/component-library`,
source at `github.com/ec-europa/europa-component-library`.

**Why this is the single best target on the list.** It is a design system, it is public
sector, it is multilingual by mandate, and it is the exact vocabulary she is trying to own.
Anyone in the EU institutional design world either works on it, works around it, or
complains about it. Follow the repo, follow the people who post about it, and be in those
threads.

It is also the safest possible subject. Commenting on a public design system is the most
ordinary professional behaviour imaginable.

### 87seconds: VERIFIED

Brussels, at Avenue du Port 86C, which is the Tour & Taxis site. Works with the European
Commission on policy communication. Offices in several European cities. **Sources disagree
on which office is the headquarters**, so do not call Brussels their HQ in public. "Their
Brussels office" or "based here" is safe. Already tagged in the Aug 10 post.

### haca.studio: LIGHT

Brussels. Designs and delivers events for NGOs, EU institutions and mission-driven
organisations, working remotely across Europe. One source. Close to her experience-design
work. Check the site before engaging.

### EU institutional accounts: UNVERIFIED as engagement targets

European Commission, EU Publications Office, Council of the EU. They exist, obviously. What
is unverified is whether their LinkedIn output has design substance worth commenting on, or
is purely policy announcements. Ten minutes of scrolling answers it. Only bother if there
is craft to talk about.

## Tier 3: Netherlands

### Studio Dumbar, part of Dept: VERIFIED
Rotterdam, founded 1977. Part of Dept since 2016. Redesigned the Dutch Police visual
system, and has done other Dutch government work. Motion-led identity. Large, active
audience. The single biggest reach available on this list.

### Fabrique: VERIFIED
Amsterdam and Rotterdam, operating since 1992. Strategic and service design. Rijksmuseum
and Nederlandse Spoorwegen among their clients. The best service-design target on the list
and the closest match to her RTA work.

### Khatt Foundation: VERIFIED
Amsterdam. Non-profit for the advancement of Arabic typography and design research across
the Middle East, North Africa and their diasporas. Founded 2004 by Huda Smitshuijzen
AbiFarès. Known for the Typographic Matchmaking projects.

**Her single strongest personal fit in Europe.** Native Arabic, multi-script systems at
national scale, and a European institution whose entire subject is that intersection. This
is not a comment target so much as a community to be visible in.

### TPTQ Arabic and Kristyan Sarkis: VERIFIED
Amsterdam. Arabic type foundry co-founded 2015 by Kristyan Sarkis with Peter Biľak of
Typotheque. Sarkis is Beirut-born, Amsterdam-based, and teaches on the Type & Media master
at the Royal Academy of Art in The Hague. Also relevant: Typotheque itself, The Hague,
multi-script type.

The Arabic typography world in the Netherlands is small, senior and reachable. She has
production credibility in it that almost nobody there has, because she has shipped a
three-script system at national volume rather than designing the typefaces for one.

## The remote route

She is running two routes, and the second one, remote work for a non-EU employer leading to
a Spanish digital nomad visa, needs different targets. Distributed employers do not hire
through agency networks, they hire through practice communities.

So for that route the targets are not studios, they are the **design systems and design ops
communities**: the ECL threads above, design system conference speakers, and the people who
publish about governance and multi-brand systems. Same comments, same craft, different room.

This is convenient. Design systems commenting serves both routes at once and needs no
separate effort.

## What to actually say

"Great post" is worse than silence, because it appears on her Activity tab as evidence of
effort without any return. Four archetypes, all of which work because she has something
almost nobody in those threads has.

### 1. The constraint she has already solved

The strongest one. European studios mostly have not had to solve bidirectional type, three
scripts, or national volume. She has. Comment on the thing they did not have to handle.

> On a system this size I always end up watching the numerals rather than the letterforms.
> We ran a curriculum system across three scripts and the thing that broke it was never the
> type, it was numerals and punctuation drifting between scripts in the same paragraph. Did
> that come up here, or did the single-script scope spare you it?

Expert, specific, nobody else in the thread can write it, and it says nothing about
anything except craft.

### 2. The governance question

> How many people had to apply this without you in the room? That number is usually the
> real design brief and it almost never appears in the case study.

Designers answer this one, at length. It also plants the word governance next to her name.

### 3. The polite disagreement

Highest reach, needs the most care. Only ever on a craft point, never about a person, and
never about a competitor.

> I would push back gently on one thing. Consistency at this scale usually fails at the
> edges rather than the centre, so the interesting question is not whether the core
> components hold, it is what happens on the day a regional team needs a component that
> does not exist yet. Is there a route for that here?

### 4. The additive reference

Bring one thing the thread does not have. A reference, a precedent, a number.

> There is a useful precedent for this in the Arabic type world, where the Typographic
> Matchmaking projects treated the second script as a design problem in its own right
> rather than a matching exercise. Different field, same argument you are making here.

### The four rules

1. **Comment on the artefact in front of her, never on the company's history.** The moment
   she asserts a fact about a studio's past or status, she is in LEGO territory in front of
   the exact audience she is trying to win. The work on the screen is always safe.
2. **One idea per comment.** Same rule as her posts.
3. **Ask a real question at the end** where it fits. It doubles reply rate and replies are
   what pull her into that studio's audience.
4. **No em dashes.** Same voice rules as posts. Comments are public writing.

## Cadence, and the rate-of-change problem

Her file warns about pattern, not incident. A gradual shift is invisible, a pivot is not.

| Weeks | European comments per week | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| 1 to 4 | 3 | Keep normal MENA engagement fully intact alongside it |
| 5 to 8 | 4 to 5 | Spread across at least three different days |
| 9 onward | 5 to 6 | European commenting should never be the only activity visible |

Never more than two comments on the same organisation in a week. Never a burst of eight in
one evening. The Activity tab shows sequence, and sequence is what reads as intent.

## Commenting windows, UTC+4

Comments need to land while the European post is fresh and its audience is live. That is a
different clock from her posting schedule.

| Window, Abu Dhabi | Brussels and Amsterdam | Dublin | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **11:00 to 14:00** | 09:00 to 12:00 | 08:00 to 11:00 | Primary. European morning, when studio posts go up |
| **19:00 to 20:30** | 17:00 to 18:30 | 16:00 to 17:30 | Strong second. End of European day |

The evening window has a quiet advantage worth naming: it is outside UAE working hours, so
the people most likely to notice her activity are least likely to be looking.

Summer times above. From the last Sunday in October, when European clocks go back, add one
hour to both windows.
