# The dual-audience content engine

The overlap she asked for: content that reads to her employer as ordinary professional pride
and lands hardest with EU recruiters. Her public-sector and institutional work is the whole
answer, because large-scale design systems and government brand work are normal things to be
proud of in Abu Dhabi and are exactly what European institutional employers are hiring for.

Voice rules, cadence and format all come from `linkedin-content-system.md`.

## The template, extracted from her own two best posts

"Arabic first" and "Tour & Taxis" are already flagged in her content system as the two most
valuable posts in the schedule. They are valuable for the same structural reason, and once
that reason is named she can generate more of them.

**Call it the constraint post.**

1. **A concrete moment, in first person and physical.** Standing in a building. Looking at a
   spread. Not an observation about the industry.
2. **The constraint or the history, stated plainly, with real numbers.** This is where the
   scale gets in, and it gets in as fact rather than as boast.
3. **What it forced.** The craft consequence. This is the part only she can write.
4. **One line that generalises**, without becoming advice.
5. **No call to action.** Nothing asking to be shared, nothing asking what people think.

**Why it is dual-audience safe:** the subject is always the work or the world, never her
position in it. There is no version of this shape that can be read as looking, because
looking is a statement about her and this shape never makes one.

**Why it lands with EU recruiters:** every constraint post is a demonstration of systems
thinking at institutional scale, in their vocabulary, without a single claim about her
seniority. Recruiters discount claims and believe demonstrations.

**Format note:** most constraint posts are natural carousels, and the carousel is the gap in
her current mix. See `linkedin-distribution-mechanics.md`.

## Verification pass: Tour & Taxis, 10 Aug

She asked for verification before posting. Three claims in the draft are the same shape as
the LEGO claim. Two need fixing, one is fine but must not be upgraded.

### 1. Must change. "The Thurn und Taxis family ran Europe's first international postal service from this site."

**Not supported.** What is verified: Francesco de Tassi was appointed postmaster of the Low
Countries by Philip the Fair in 1501, and the family built the European postal system for
the Habsburgs across the 16th and 17th centuries, **starting from Brussels**. But the site
itself was meadow. The horses for the postal coaches grazed on these fields north of the
city centre, across the canal dug in 1550. That is why the place carries the family name.

The service was run from Brussels. It was not run from this ground.

This is exactly the LEGO shape: a claim that is 90% right, stated 10% too strongly, on a
subject where someone in the audience is a specialist. And this time the audience is
Belgian.

**The correction is also a better sentence, because it is more concrete:**

> Tour & Taxis carries the name of the Thurn und Taxis family, who pioneered the European
> postal system for the Habsburgs from Brussels, from 1501 onward. This ground was where
> their post horses grazed. Fields, across a canal, north of the city.

Suggest also softening "Europe's first international postal service" to "pioneered the
European postal system". Belgium's own tourism material uses "pioneers", and superlatives
are what get corrected.

### 2. Must tighten. "a station that was once the largest covered goods hall in Europe"

**Supported, but not in that wording.** The verified claim is that the Gare Maritime was the
**largest freight station in Europe** when it was built, around 45,000 square metres,
140 by 280 metres, seven naves on rows of cast-iron columns. Built 1902 to 1907, with
construction on the wider complex beginning 22 July 1900 in the presence of Leopold II.

Her "once" already does the qualifying work, which is good instinct. Use the sourced
phrasing and the sourced numbers:

> the largest freight station in Europe when it was built, 140 by 280 metres under seven
> naves of iron and glass

### 3. Fine as written, do not upgrade. 87seconds

"87seconds, a creative agency whose work I follow and admire, is based here too" is
**accurate**. Their Brussels office is at Avenue du Port 86C, which is the Tour & Taxis
address, and they work with the European Commission.

**Do not change "based here" to "headquartered here."** Sources disagree about which of
their offices is the headquarters, some say Brussels and some say Paris. "Based here" is
true under either. This matters because she is tagging them, and a tagged account is the
one most likely to correct her.

### 4. Verified as written, no change needed

- Multi-modal freight terminal, goods arriving by rail, road and canal. Verified, and it was
  one of the world's first multi-modal freight platforms, which is a stronger fact than the
  one she used.
- Customs became irrelevant with the EU Customs Union and later Schengen. Verified.
- Disused for around twenty years. Verified. Bought by developer Extensa in 2001, Gare
  Maritime reopened in 2020 as a covered neighbourhood of workspaces, food and gardens.
- Spaces coworking is in the Gare Maritime. Verified.
- La Fabrica. Not found in sources, but she ate there, and first-hand experience is not a
  claim about a brand's status. Low risk. Worth double-checking the spelling on the sign.
- "The train rails are gone" is her own observation from the visit. Fine.

### 5. One addition worth making

The beehive paragraph is currently imagined. There is a real number available: **in the
1960s, its peak, Tour & Taxis employed around 3,000 people.** Verified.

That number does the work her imagination is currently doing, and numbers travel across
markets intact. Consider replacing "I kept trying to picture it at full noise" with the
picture plus the figure.

## Aug 6, Arabic first: full draft

Her raw notes, written to the template. This is the highest-value post in the schedule for
the EU goal, because it speaks directly to the one capability that is scarce and commercially
valuable in European institutional markets, and it is completely ordinary content for someone
working in Abu Dhabi.

> Most bilingual work in this region is made in English and then moved into Arabic. You can
> see it immediately. The Arabic sits in a layout that was never built for it, at a size
> chosen for Latin letterforms, with line spacing that ignores how much of Arabic lives
> above and below the line.
>
> I design Arabic first now. Not out of principle. Because the layouts come out better.
>
> Arabic does proximity where English does distance. A line that takes three words in
> English arrives compressed and close, so the white space around it has to do a different
> job. The rhythm is different, the vertical logic is different, and the eye enters from the
> other side. Build the grid for English and pour Arabic into it, and every one of those
> differences turns into a defect you then spend a week repairing. Build it the other way
> round and English adapts. English almost always adapts. That is the whole trick, and it
> took me years to see it.
>
> So the question I ask when someone commissions bilingual work is not "does this translate
> well."
>
> It is: was this made in Arabic, or was it made in English and then repaired.

**Image:** a bilingual spread, Arabic side up. Real work, not a mockup, per her measured
data on genuine images.

**Tag:** Dubai Municipality, per her note, if the example is natural to them.

**Two-reader test.** *Boss:* a senior creative making a craft argument about Arabic, in a
market where that argument is professionally respected. Nothing about anywhere else.
Passes. *EU recruiter:* this person understands multilingual design systems, bidirectional
typography and localisation at a depth almost nobody in European practice has, and thinks
about it at grid and governance level rather than as translation. Passes, hard.

**Aug 3, one line to add.** The proposal post is currently pure craft. Naming the arena
lifts it into EU search vocabulary at no risk, because it is simply what the job is: these
are government tenders, evaluated by committee, on public-sector procurement terms. "Public
sector tender" and "institutional stakeholders" are phrases EU employers use. Add the
context, keep the recipe personal.

**Aug 13, accuracy caution.** The anecdote is fine and the take is good. Keep the gathering
and the people non-identifying, and keep the claim about who else is secretly using AI as an
opinion rather than a statistic. Do not attach a percentage to it. "Roughly a third did not
raise their hands" is her own observation of a room and is safe. "A third of designers use
AI secretly" is a statistic she cannot support, and it is the LEGO shape again.

**Aug 17, the real risk.** "Ugly design era" is her highest-engagement idea and her highest
accuracy risk, because the natural way to write it is to point at examples. **Never name a
brand, a studio or a specific piece of work as the ugly one.** That is a public claim about
a named party, in front of an audience that includes people who worked on it, and it is the
one post here that could cost her something in both markets at once. Argue about the field
and the tools, use her own work as the only named example, and it stays safe and stays
sharp.

## Making MENA work legible, per `case-study-translator`

Never lead with the client name. Lead with what the organisation is and the scale of the
problem. Drop-in phrasings for posts and comments:

| Instead of | Say |
|---|---|
| RTA | Dubai's roads and transport authority |
| The Ministry of Education curriculum | A national school curriculum, 100+ titles across five subjects and three scripts |
| CPI Media Group | A regional media group publishing 20+ consumer and B2B titles |
| Act Air | MENA's first interactive hologram company |
| DAIS 2019 | A government-backed international conference on AI in sport, the first of its kind in the region |
| WeDo government clients | UAE government and institutional clients |

The numbers travel without any cultural context and should carry the weight the client name
carries at home: €500,000 · 100+ books · 5 subjects · 3 scripts · 4 countries · 20+ brands ·
+38% · 12% tender win rate · 250+ vacancies · 6 activations · 22 years.

**One rule from the skill worth repeating here:** never denigrate the region to make the work
sound bigger. It reads badly and it is false. The work was demanding on its own terms.
