# LinkedIn content system

Carried over from Sara's separate LinkedIn chat, July 2026. **This is observed performance
data from her own account, not theory.** It outranks any generic LinkedIn advice — if a
strategy suggestion conflicts with something in this file, this file wins.

## Cadence

**Twice weekly, Mondays and Thursdays.**

## Voice rules — non-negotiable

- **No em dashes. Ever.** Use commas, full stops or a colon instead.
- **No corporate language. No tip lists.** Written like a person, not a content creator.
- **One idea per post**, taken seriously, rather than several observations stacked.
- **Never fabricate experience or credentials.**

## What performs — measured, not assumed

| Works | Evidence |
|---|---|
| **Personal posts with a genuine photo and a real moment** | Consistently her strongest format |
| **Tagging relevant brands or people, when it is natural** | Busuu replied to one post and pushed it to **2,475 impressions** |
| **Articles** | Build credibility but reach is low, so pair each with a teaser post |

| Fails | Evidence |
|---|---|
| **No-image posts** | Underperform badly |
| **Reposts** | Perform terribly. Skip them. |

## Accuracy — a rule learned the hard way

**Verify any brand or news claim before posting.** She was publicly corrected on LEGO's
World Cup status. On a profile being read by her employer and by future recruiters, a
public correction is expensive.

This is the same standard the rest of the repo runs on: verify before publishing, and say
when something is unverified.

## ⚠️ The strategic gap — and the safest way to close it

**Her audience is currently mostly MENA. European reach requires commenting on European
institutional and creative profiles, not just posting.**

That is the single most important line in this file, and it is fortunate, because
**commenting is also the lowest-risk tactic available to her.**

A post is pushed into her employer's feed. A comment on a Belgian design studio's post is
seen by that studio's audience and is far less likely to surface in front of her boss.
So the tactic that actually opens the European audience happens to be the one that
attracts least attention at home. Lean on it.

**One caution:** watch the *rate of change*, not just individual posts. Occasional European
content reads as professional interest. A sudden pivot to majority-European content and
commenting is a pattern, and patterns are what get noticed. Shift gradually.
