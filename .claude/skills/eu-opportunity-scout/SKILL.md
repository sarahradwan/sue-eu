# EU opportunity scout

Finds and **verifies** routes to the EU. The job is not to produce a long list — it is to
produce a short list where every entry is real.

## Before anything else

Read `01-relocation/eligibility-filter.md`. It is the definition of "qualifies". Do not
work from memory of it — the criteria can change and the file is the authority.

## The two routes

**Route A — employment with visa sponsorship.** The employer sponsors, or the country
offers a permit route she can access without a sponsor.

**Route B — funded study.** Fully funded, or first year covered, or housing + some food,
or tuition under €2,000/year.

## The verification rule

This is the core discipline of this skill.

**Never report an opportunity as qualifying unless sponsorship or funding is explicitly
stated at the source.**

These do **not** mean sponsorship:
- "We're an international team"
- "Applicants from all over the world"
- "Relocation support available" (may mean flights and a hotel, not a visa)
- "We welcome diverse candidates"

These do:
- "We sponsor work visas / highly skilled migrant permits"
- The employer appears on a national register of recognised sponsors
- "Visa sponsorship provided"

If it is unstated: record the lead as **unverified**, note exactly what is missing, and
say so plainly. An unverified lead is still worth recording — it is not worth
presenting as a match.

The same applies to funding. "Scholarships may be available" is not a funded place.
Find the scheme, the amount, and the deadline.

## Search strategy

Work in this order — highest-yield first.

**1. Sponsor registers, not job boards.** Several countries publish official lists of
employers licensed to sponsor non-EU workers. Searching those lists for agencies, brand
consultancies and in-house creative teams is far more efficient than filtering job boards
and hoping. Find whether the target country maintains one.

**2. Non-sponsor permit routes.** Job-seeker visas, orientation-year permits, talent and
self-employment routes remove the sponsorship bottleneck entirely. These are the highest-
leverage thing to research because they change what she can even apply to. Verify current
rules at the official immigration source — not a blog.

**3. Scholarship bodies for mid-career professionals.** Most schemes target recent
graduates. Her 22 years is unusual — it disqualifies her from some schemes and makes her
competitive in others (executive, professional development, and mid-career leadership
schemes). Check the age and experience criteria explicitly every time; this is the most
common reason a promising lead collapses.

**4. Low-tuition public universities.** Where public tuition is at or under €2,000/year,
Route B works without a full scholarship. Check the non-EU fee, not the EU fee — they are
frequently very different, and the EU number is the one that appears first.

**5. English-taught programmes and English-language employers.** German A1 and French A2
are below working level. English is where the real field is.

## Fit assessment

Against `05-career-context/cv-source-of-truth.md`. What genuinely maps:

- 22 years, director level, multidisciplinary team leadership
- Government and institutional clients, minister-level stakeholders
- €500k national curriculum programme — 100+ books, 5 subjects, 3 scripts
- Brand architecture across four countries
- AI governance written into client brand systems — genuinely differentiating, and
  currently in demand
- Native Arabic + C2 English — real value for any agency with MENA business
- Multi-script typography — a rare specialism

Be honest about what does not:

- No EU work experience, no current right to work
- MENA client names may not be recognised by EU recruiters — case studies must carry
  scale and complexity themselves
- Figma still developing
- German A1, French A2

## Recording a lead

Route A leads → `01-relocation/pipeline-jobs.md`.
Route B leads → `01-relocation/pipeline-scholarships.md`.

Split by route so parallel sessions never edit the same file. `country-notes.md` is shared
by both — pull before editing it. Record all eight fields from the filter. Deadline inside
60 days → top section, and flag it at the check-in.

Capture the **deadline** above all. Scholarship rounds routinely close six to nine months
before intake. A perfect opportunity found a week late is worth nothing.

## Reporting to Sara

Short. Ranked by route strength, then country preference. For each:

- What it is, where, deadline
- Route A or B, and **confirmed or unverified**
- Why it fits her specifically
- The single next action

Separate **confirmed** from **unverified** visually. Never blur them. If a week's search
found nothing that qualifies, say that — a null result honestly reported is more useful
than five padded leads.
