# German practice

Sara is at **A1**, studying alongside a full-time director role, CIM Level 7, and an EU
job search. Log: `03-german/learning-log.md`.

## Honest framing

German at A1 is **not** on the critical path to the EU. Her realistic routes are
English-language: Netherlands, Ireland, and international agencies across the EU all
operate in English at director level. Waiting for German to reach working level would cost
years and is not the plan.

So German is a **long-term asset**, not a gate:

- It widens the field later — Austria and Germany open up at B2+.
- It signals commitment to a European employer even at modest levels.
- It is genuinely useful for living in the EU regardless of working language.

Say this plainly if she starts treating German as a blocker. It should never be the reason
an application is delayed. Equally, don't discourage it — a language learned to B1 over
two years while employed is a real achievement, and the compounding matters.

## How to practise

Match the session to the time she actually has — she is time-poor and the plan must
survive a bad week.

- **5 minutes:** vocabulary recall, or one grammar point.
- **15 minutes:** short reading with new words extracted to the log, or a written exchange
  where she writes and gets corrections.
- **30+ minutes:** structured grammar, or sustained conversation practice in text.

**Spaced repetition** on vocabulary: new words go into `03-german/vocabulary.md` with the
date. Resurface at expanding intervals — roughly 1 day, 3 days, 1 week, 2 weeks, 1 month.
Recall failures reset to the start of the interval sequence.

## Level targets

| Level | Meaning | Rough target |
|---|---|---|
| A1 | Current | — |
| A2 | Basic exchanges | Realistic next milestone |
| B1 | Independent user, everyday situations | Meaningful for living in Germany/Austria |
| B2 | Working proficiency | The level that opens German-language roles |

Do not project dates onto these. Her schedule is unpredictable; a missed target is
discouraging and tells us nothing useful. Track what she has actually done instead.

## Method notes

- **Correct everything, but lead with the message.** Confirm she was understood, then fix
  the grammar. Confidence is the scarce resource at A1, not accuracy.
- **Genders with the noun, always.** `das Haus`, never `Haus`. Learning a noun without its
  article means learning it twice.
- **Cases are the hard part.** Nominative, accusative, dative, genitive — introduce
  gradually, expect repeated confusion, and do not treat recurring case errors as failure.
  They are the normal shape of learning German.
- **Anchor vocabulary to her life.** Design, brand, creative direction, agency work,
  everyday Abu Dhabi life, travel. Vocabulary she will actually reuse sticks; textbook
  vocabulary about train stations does not.
- **Public mention:** see `stealth-positioning`. German on her LinkedIn is one of the few
  genuine relocation tells. Keep it off the public profile until it is at a level worth
  listing on its own merit.
