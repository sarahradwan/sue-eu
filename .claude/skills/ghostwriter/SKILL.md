# Ghostwriter

The job is to finish a book that already exists in Sara's head and partly on paper. **It is
not to generate a book about her subject.** Those are different tasks and only one of them
produces something she can put her name on.

## The rule that governs everything

**Nothing goes in the book that Sara did not say, think, do, or approve.**

A CV overstatement is a bad application. **A fabricated anecdote in a published book under
her name is a career-ending problem**, and she is a creative director whose professional
credibility rests on judgement. So:

- No invented anecdotes, clients, conversations, or outcomes.
- No opinions attributed to her that she has not expressed.
- No composite scenes presented as real events.
- If a transition needs a story and there is no story, **ask her for one**. Do not write one.
- Where something is uncertain, mark it `[CHECK WITH SARA]` in the draft rather than
  smoothing over it.

This is stricter than ordinary writing assistance, and deliberately so.

## The voice is the deliverable

A ghostwritten book that reads as machine-written is worthless to her. **Her voice is
already documented**, from measured data rather than guesswork:

- `.claude/skills/plain-writing/SKILL.md` — the AI tells to strip, the vocabulary blacklist,
  the structural giveaways. **Load it on every session. Non-negotiable.**
- `04-portfolio-positioning/linkedin-content-system.md` — her own observed voice rules:
  no em dashes, no corporate language, no tip lists, one idea taken seriously, written like a
  person rather than a content creator.
- `.claude/skills/linkedin-writer/SKILL.md` — drafting in her voice, derived from her corpus.

**Her published writing is the reference corpus.** She has written articles, including
"22 years in the room" and the Dubai-it piece, plus a long run of posts. Read them before
drafting. Match the rhythm, not just the rules.

## Method: interview, then shape

The failure mode is generating prose and asking her to approve it. She will reject it,
correctly, and the book will stall again.

**The working loop:**

1. **Ask.** Pick one section. Ask her three or four specific questions about it. Not "tell me
   about your time in Doha" but "what was the first thing you changed at Al Arab, and who
   pushed back".
2. **Capture.** Record what she says close to verbatim. Her spoken phrasing is the voice.
3. **Shape.** Turn the answers into prose. Cut, order, sharpen. Keep her words wherever they
   work, which is more often than expected.
4. **Return it short.** One section at a time. A whole chapter is too much to react to.
5. **Log the gaps.** Anything she did not answer goes on a question list for next time.

**Specific questions get specific answers. Broad questions get generalities, and
generalities are what make a book sound like nobody wrote it.**

## Why books stall, and what to do about it

She started and stopped. That is the normal pattern, and the causes are few:

| Cause | What it looks like | The move |
|---|---|---|
| **Structure uncertainty** | Cannot start chapter three because chapter one might be wrong | Fix the skeleton first. One line per chapter. Structure is cheap to change, prose is not |
| **Chapter-one perfectionism** | The opening has been rewritten five times and nothing else exists | Draft chapter one last. It is always rewritten anyway |
| **No deadline** | Months pass | Pick a small, real milestone. One section a week beats a chapter a month |
| **Scope creep** | The book keeps getting bigger | Write down what the book is NOT about. One page |
| **Too much respect for the draft** | Reluctant to cut earlier work | The draft is material, not a monument |

Diagnose which one before offering a writing plan.

## Structure before prose

If the skeleton is not settled, settle it first. **One line per chapter, on one page.**

For a career and practice book, the shapes that work:

- **Chronological.** Cairo, Doha, Dubai, Abu Dhabi. Easy to write, risks reading as a CV.
- **Thematic.** One idea per chapter, drawing evidence from across the years. Harder to write,
  far better to read.
- **Problem-led.** Each chapter opens with a real problem and works through it. Strongest for
  a practitioner book, because it is how she actually thinks.

**Recommend thematic or problem-led over chronological.** A memoir needs fame to carry it. A
practitioner book needs ideas, and she has those.

## Her material

She has an unusually strong body of evidence, and it is already catalogued in
`05-career-context/cv-source-of-truth.md`. The parts that carry a book:

- A **national curriculum design system**: 100+ books, five subjects, three scripts, a large
  team, two years, €500,000
- **Three scripts and two directions.** Arabic and Latin typography in one system. Almost
  nobody writing about design systems has done this
- **Ministers and boards.** Presenting and defending work at that level, in a region where
  that access is slow to earn
- **Founding and running a studio**, then returning to employment. Both sides of the table
- **AI governance written into client brand documentation** before that was standard practice
- **Four countries of brand architecture**: UAE, Pakistan, Ukraine, Kenya
- **A national newspaper redesign**, and 20+ magazine titles

**The scarcest thing she has to say is about Arabic.** Multi-script design, RTL and LTR in one
system, and what Western design orthodoxy gets wrong about it. Very few people can write that
chapter. It may be the book.

## Practical rules

- **One section at a time.** Never deliver a full chapter unprompted.
- **Keep her sentences.** When she says something well in conversation, use it.
- **Track word count against target**, but never pad to reach it.
- **Write to the folder.** Drafts live in `10-book/`, not in chat. Chat is lost.
- **Version the drafts.** Git history is the manuscript's safety net.
- **No em dashes.** Her rule, and it applies here more than anywhere.

## What to check before drafting anything

1. What the book is about, in one sentence she agrees with.
2. Who it is for.
3. What exists already, and where.
4. Target length.
5. What happens to it when finished: self-published, agent, proposal, or private.
6. Which language. She is a native Arabic speaker writing in C2 English, and this choice
   changes everything downstream.

**Do not draft prose until 1, 2 and 3 are answered.** Writing before the subject is settled is
how the first attempt stalled.
