# Stealth positioning

Sara's employer monitors her LinkedIn. Everything public must attract EU recruiters while
reading, to her boss, as ordinary professional pride in her current work.

That tension is the entire craft. Both halves are required — invisible to recruiters is
as much a failure as visible to her boss.

## Where this applies

**Public — apply these rules:** LinkedIn (profile, headline, about, posts, comments,
skills, groups), the portfolio website, any public bio, conference or speaker listings,
public CV variants.

**Private — no restriction, be direct:** this repo, applications sent directly to an
employer or university, cover letters, chats with Sara.

## Banned in public content

Never, in any form:

- "Open to opportunities", "open to work", `#OpenToWork`, "seeking new challenges",
  "next chapter", "exploring what's next"
- Any statement about relocating, moving, or wanting to work abroad
- An EU city or country in her location field — location stays **Abu Dhabi, UAE**
- Following or engaging in a way that reads as a job hunt: joining "jobs in X" groups,
  publicly following EU recruiters en masse, commenting on job posts
- Language implying dissatisfaction with her current role
- Anything about visas, sponsorship, permits, or scholarships

If asked for something that breaches this, say so and offer the safe version. Do not
quietly comply.

## What works instead

The goal: **international legibility without stated intent.** A recruiter in Amsterdam
should read her profile and think "this person operates at our level." Her boss should
read the same profile and think "she's proud of her work here."

**1. Let the work carry the internationalism.** She already has it — brand architecture
across UAE, Pakistan, Ukraine and Kenya; a €500k national programme; three scripts;
minister-level stakeholders. Stating the scale of real projects is not a signal of intent
to leave. It is simply an accurate CV. This is the single most effective lever and it
carries zero risk.

**2. Lead with AI governance in brand systems.** This is her sharpest differentiator and
it is in demand in the EU right now. Writing about AI usage guidelines inside client brand
documentation reads at home as thought leadership in her current role — because it is —
and reads to an EU recruiter as exactly the capability agencies are hiring for.

**3. Use CIM Level 7 openly.** A UK-accredited qualification is an unambiguous credential
that signals professional development, not exit. It is completely safe to post about and
it puts a European-recognised qualification on her profile. Use it freely.

**4. Speak the register of European creative practice.** Design systems, brand
architecture, experience design, multi-script typography, governance. She already does
this work; naming it in the vocabulary EU recruiters search on makes her findable without
a word about relocation.

**5. Keyword surface, quietly.** Recruiters search by skill and title. "Brand strategy",
"design systems", "creative direction", "experience design" belong in her profile as
descriptions of what she does. No geography attached.

**6. German — frame as culture, not relocation.** If mentioned at all: a personal interest
in language and culture. Safest handling is to leave German off the public profile until
it reaches a level worth listing. There is no upside to flagging it early and it is one of
the few genuine tells.

## The two-reader test

Before publishing anything public, read it twice:

**As her boss.** Does anything suggest she is looking? Any hint of leaving, dissatisfaction,
or elsewhere? If yes — rewrite.

**As an EU recruiter.** Does this read as someone operating at international director level,
in vocabulary they search on? If no — it is safe but useless. Rewrite.

Only publish when it passes both. State explicitly that both were checked.

## Portfolio website — different rules

`sarahradwan.me` is less monitored than LinkedIn but still public and still discoverable.
Treat it as public, with more room:

- Case studies can carry full depth — process, scale, outcomes, stakeholder complexity.
- **Translate MENA context for EU readers.** "RTA" means nothing in Rotterdam. "Dubai's
  government transport authority — a two-day brand experience inside a government building,
  six bilingual activations, 250+ vacancy registration platform" means a great deal.
  Explain scale and complexity; never assume brand recognition.
- Metrics travel across markets: €500k, 100+ books, 3 scripts, +38%, 4 countries, 12%.
- Contact details and availability are fine. A stated *intent to relocate* is not.
- Serve both markets: credible to a UAE client, legible to an EU recruiter. Same
  requirement as LinkedIn, more room to work in.

## When she asks for something risky

Name the risk, then give her the safe alternative that achieves the same goal. She is
senior and makes her own calls — but she should be making them with the tell pointed out,
not discovering it after her boss has seen it.
