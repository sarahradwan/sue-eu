# Case study translator

Solves the single biggest weakness in her EU applications — and it is not the work, it is
the **legibility** of the work.

## The problem

Her portfolio is MENA-institutional. In Abu Dhabi and Dubai, "RTA", "the Ministry of
Education curriculum", "CPI Media Group" and "DAIS" carry immediate weight. In Rotterdam,
Dublin or Lisbon they carry none. A hiring director there is not being dismissive — they
genuinely cannot size the work.

So a portfolio that reads as extremely senior in the Gulf can read as *unplaceable* in
Europe. The work does not need improving. It needs translating.

## The method

**Never lead with the client name. Lead with what the organisation is and the scale of
the problem.**

Weak — assumes recognition:
> Brand experience for RTA.

Strong — carries its own scale:
> A two-day brand experience inside a government building for Dubai's roads and transport
> authority — concept, identity, six bilingual Arabic/English activations, touchscreen
> kiosks, a participatory installation, a secure registration platform handling 250+
> vacancies, environmental graphics, and on-site direction across both days.

Same project, same truth. The second is legible to someone who has never heard of RTA,
and it is more accurate, not less.

## The four moves

**1. Name the organisation type, not just the organisation.**
"Dubai's roads and transport authority." "The UAE Ministry of Education." "A regional media
group publishing 20+ consumer and B2B titles." "MENA's first interactive hologram company."
One clause. It costs nothing and it is the difference between recognition and confusion.

**2. Quantify everything quantifiable.** Numbers cross borders intact and need no cultural
context. Her real ones:

€500,000 · 100+ books · 5 subjects · 3 scripts · 4 countries · 20+ brands · +38% ·
12% tender win rate · 250+ vacancies · 22 years · 6 activations · 2 days

**3. State the scope of her role.** She led large multidisciplinary teams. European
readers need to know whether she directed or executed — the seniority is the point, and
being precise about it builds trust rather than diminishing it.

**4. Name the difficulty.** What made it hard is what makes it impressive. Three scripts in
one typographic system. Bilingual RTL/LTR across physical and digital. Ministerial
stakeholders. A live government building. Four countries on one brand architecture. Say the
constraint, then say how it was solved.

## Translating each project

| Project | Lead with | Difficulty to name |

| **National curriculum** | National education system design, €500k, 100+ books, 5 subjects, 3 scripts, large team led | Three scripts in one system; national consistency at volume |
| **RTA** | Two-day brand experience in a government building, six bilingual activations, physical + digital + platform | Live government environment; bilingual across every touchpoint |

| **MBC rebrand** | Consumer magazine rebrand, +38% subscriber growth | Rare clean commercial proof of design impact |

## Reframe the MENA context as an asset

It is easy to treat "my experience is regional" as a weakness. For the right EU employer it
is the opposite, and it should be said plainly:

- **Native Arabic + C2 English** — any agency with Gulf, Middle Eastern or North African
  business needs this and almost never has it in-house.
- **Multi-script typography** — Arabic, Latin and a third script in one working system is a
  genuine specialism. Most European design systems people have never had to solve RTL/LTR
  properly.
- **Government and institutional delivery** — regulated, high-stakes, slow-approval work.
  Directly transferable to European public sector, EU institutions and regulated industries.
- **Volume at national scale** — 100+ books in one system is a systems-thinking credential
  regardless of geography.

Belgium is worth noting here: Brussels runs on EU institutional and multilingual
communication work, which is close to what she has been doing for a decade.

## Rules

- **Never inflate.** Translation clarifies scale; it does not add any. No new numbers, no
  upgraded titles, no borrowed credit.
- **Never denigrate the region** to make the work sound bigger. It reads badly and it is
  false — the work was demanding on its own terms.
- **Keep public versions safe.** Case studies on her website and LinkedIn still follow
  `stealth-positioning`. Depth is fine; a stated intent to relocate is not.
- **Check client confidentiality** before publishing detail on government work. Some tender
  and ministry material is restricted. When unsure, describe the organisation type and the
  scope without naming the client.
