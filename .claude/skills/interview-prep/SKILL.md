# Interview prep

For EU employers and scholarship panels. Her material is in
`05-career-context/cv-source-of-truth.md`.

Adapted from public interview-prep skills, with EU relocation realities added — that is
where the actual difficulty sits for her, and generic skills say nothing about it.

## Her STAR bank

Level 7 and director interviews reward structured evidence. She has unusually strong
material; the risk is telling it as a story rather than as **Situation → Task → Action →
Result**, and never landing the result.

Build and reuse these. Each should run 90 seconds to two minutes.

| Story | Demonstrates | Result to land |
|---|---|---|
| **National curriculum system** | Design systems at scale, leading a large multidisciplinary team | €500k, 100+ books, 5 subjects, 3 scripts |
| **RTA brand experience** | End-to-end experience design, bilingual delivery, physical + digital | Two days, six activations, 250+ vacancy platform |
| **Act Air** | Brand + investor narrative, making unfamiliar technology legible | Full brand and proposition from zero |
| **AI usage guidelines in brand systems** | Foresight, governance, turning a hype topic into practice | Codified into client brand documentation |
| **Group brand architecture** | Multi-market consistency, long-client relationship | 4 countries, 4-year retainer |
| **MBC rebrand** | Commercial impact of design | +38% subscriber growth |
| **Co-founding Social Dar** | Ownership, new business, commercial risk | Built and ran a studio, 2021–2024 |
| **Defending work to ministers** | Senior stakeholder nerve | Government tender work, 12% win rate contribution |

**The gap to close:** she is used to MENA audiences who know these clients. EU panels do
not. Every story needs one sentence of context first — *what the organisation is* — before
the story starts. See `case-study-translator`.

## The hard questions

These decide EU interviews for a non-EU candidate. Rehearse them properly.

**"Why do you want to leave the UAE?"**
The trap is answering with what she is leaving. Answer with what she is moving *toward* —
European brand practice, design systems culture, a market where the work she wants to do
is the norm. Never criticise the UAE, her employer, or the region. It reads as someone who
will criticise them next.

**"Do you need visa sponsorship?"**
Yes, and say so plainly. Then immediately show she understands the route — which permit,
what the employer actually has to do, roughly how long. Employers fear unknown
administrative burden more than the burden itself. Competence here is reassuring and rare.

**"You have no European experience."**
True, and do not pretend otherwise. Pivot to what transfers: multi-market brand
architecture across four countries, three-script typography, institutional stakeholders,
regulatory-heavy government work. And name the genuine asset — MENA fluency and native
Arabic is something almost nobody in their team has, and it is commercially useful to any
agency with Gulf clients.

**"Why a scholarship at this stage of your career?"** *(Route B)*
Panels expect early-career candidates. Her 22 years is either a red flag or her strongest
differentiator depending entirely on the framing. The answer: formalising two decades of
practice, and a specific thing she intends to do with it. Vague self-improvement loses.

**"Are you overqualified?"**
Address the real fear underneath — that she will leave when something better appears.
Answer that directly with the reason she wants *this*.

**"What are your salary expectations?"**
Research the market band for the city first, never quote a UAE number. UAE salaries are
tax-free; European gross figures are not comparable and quoting one either underprices her
badly or prices her out. Give a researched range for that market, and say it is based on
the local market.

## Portfolio presentation

Creative director interviews mean presenting work. Almost always the deciding round.

- **Three to five projects.** Depth beats breadth. Curate to their brief.
- **Context first, always.** One sentence on what the organisation is and why the work
  mattered, before any visuals. Assume zero recognition.
- **Her role, precisely.** She led large teams — be exact about what she directed versus
  what she made. Senior interviewers probe this, and precision builds trust.
- **The thinking, not the artefact.** At director level they are buying judgement. Why this
  direction, what was rejected, what constraint shaped it.
- **Rehearse in English to a European ear.** Her English is C2, so this is not about
  language — it is about reference points. Explain regional context that she would normally
  assume.

## Panel-specific notes

**Scholarship panels** weight motivation, trajectory and what she will do afterwards. They
are assessing whether funding her produces something. Have a clear, specific answer for
"and then what?"

**Agency interviews** weight craft, taste and team fit. Expect a portfolio deep-dive.

**In-house brand teams** weight stakeholder management and systems thinking — where her
government and institutional experience is genuinely differentiating, and often stronger
than an agency-only candidate's.

## Her questions for them

Never none. For Route A, the sponsorship timeline and who has done it before are entirely
reasonable and signal seriousness. Also: how creative decisions get made, what the team
looks like, what success is at six months.

## Practice

Offer to run mock rounds — one question at a time, honest feedback, then again. Watch for
the two recurring failure modes: **assuming client recognition**, and **describing work
rather than landing the result**.
