Owned by the **UAE jobs session**. Researched 31 July 2026.

## The headline finding, before any numbers

**Public salary data for UAE creative roles is not merely noisy — it disagrees with itself by
a factor of three for the same job title.** Any single figure quoted from it is close to
meaningless.

| Source | Role | Figure | What it implies monthly |
|---|---|---|---|
| Payscale | Marketing Director, branding skills, UAE | AED 255,000/yr base | ~AED 21,250 |
| Michael Page 2026 guide, *via* What's On / Arabian Business | Sales & marketing director | **AED 70,000/month** | ~AED 840,000/yr |

Same seniority band. **A 3.3× gap.** That is not sampling noise; the two sources are
measuring different populations. And the reason they differ is the single most useful thing
in this document:

> **In the UAE, employer type sets her pay far more than her title or her 22 years do.**

Self-reported aggregators (Payscale, ERI, Indeed) are filled by people at small studios and
mid-size agencies reporting base salary. Recruiter guides (Michael Page, Cooper Fitch) are
built from their own placements, which are overwhelmingly large corporates and in-house
teams, quoted as total package.

**Consequence for the search: moving between agencies is a 10–20% step. Moving from a
mid-size agency into a large multinational's in-house brand team can be a 2× step.** That
single sentence should shape the target list — and it does, in `target-employers.md`.

### Market direction

UAE salaries are forecast to rise **4.1% on average in 2026**, with the economy growing 4.5%
led by non-oil sectors. **AI, analytics and performance-marketing capability are reported as
driving the sharpest salary premiums at every seniority level**
([Arabian Business](https://www.arabianbusiness.com/jobs/uae-salary-guide-2026-the-fastest-growing-sectors-top-paying-jobs-and-skills-employers-want-most)).

That last clause is worth pausing on. **Her AI positioning is not only her EU differentiator
— it is a named salary premium in her current market, right now.** The vault has been
treating AI as an argument for European employers. It is also a live argument for a raise at
WeDo, in a conversation that carries none of the risk of a job search.

## 🎯 The finding that repeats across both markets

In the same Payscale dataset, on the same underlying skill:

| Title | UAE average base |
|---|---|
| Creative Director *with branding skills* | AED 215,000 |
| **Marketing Director *with branding skills*** | **AED 255,000** |

**An ~18% premium for carrying the marketing title rather than the creative title.**

`pipeline-jobs.md` reached exactly this conclusion for Europe on 26 July — that her CIM
Level 7 has been sitting unused while she applies only to design and creative roles. **The
UAE data says the same thing about her home market.** Two independent datasets, two
continents, one conclusion:

> **The title she should be negotiating and searching for is brand or marketing-side, not
> creative-side.** `Head of Brand` · `Brand Director` · `Marketing Director` ·
> `Senior Brand Manager` · `Head of Marketing Communications`.

And the `brand-strategy-cim` CV variant already exists for exactly this. It was built for
Karsten in Amsterdam. **It is equally the right document for a UAE in-house brand role**, and

### The self-assessment, so she can answer it in two minutes

Against a **Senior Art Director / Brand and Creative Lead at a mid-size Abu Dhabi agency,
22 years, government and institutional clients**:

| If her total package is | Verdict | Action |
|---|---|---|
| **Below AED 15,000/mo** | **Underpaid, clearly.** Below the Cooper Fitch all-roles Dubai median of AED 20,000 despite 22 years and a director-level remit | Negotiate hard, and search. Both |
| **AED 15,000–18,000/mo** | **Underpaid modestly**, and short of the brief in `savings-target.md` | Negotiate first — the gap is a raise, not a job change |
| **AED 18,000–22,000/mo** | **Market-appropriate for agency-side.** Meets the brief at a mid lifestyle | Do not move for money. Move only for an EU-office employer |
| **Above AED 22,000/mo** | **Paid well for agency-side.** Above the ERI senior-CD model | Stay. The target is reachable on this salary; protect it |

Two things she should weigh when she places herself in that table:

- **Abu Dhabi agency-side is the lower-paying corner of the UAE market.** Dubai pays more,
  and in-house pays more than agency. She is in the corner where the public data is weakest
  and the pay is likely lowest.
- **Her title understates her role.** She is titled *Senior Art Director* while doing
  creative-director work — leading creative vision for government clients, presenting to
  ministers, contributing to a 12% tender win rate. **That gap between title and remit is
  itself a negotiating asset**, and it is the cleanest raise argument available: not "pay me
  more", but "the title does not describe the job".

## ⚠️ Do not compare these figures to European gross salaries

UAE income is untaxed. European figures in the rest of this vault — €71,304 Dutch,
€50,700 German, €36,605 Irish, €95,000 as her Amsterdam ask — are **gross, pre-tax**.

| | Amount | What lands |
|---|---|---|
| AED 20,000/month UAE | AED 240,000/yr ≈ **€56,500** | **€56,500** — no income tax |
| €95,000 Amsterdam, with the 27% ruling | €95,000 gross | roughly €65,000–70,000 net **[ESTIMATE]** |
| €95,000 Amsterdam, no ruling | €95,000 gross | roughly €55,000–58,000 net **[ESTIMATE]** |

Two consequences, both uncomfortable and both worth knowing now:

1. **Her €95,000 Amsterdam ask is not a pay rise over AED 20,000/month.** It is roughly a
   wash, and without the expatriate ruling it may be slightly worse. **The move is not a
   financial upgrade and should never be evaluated as one.** It buys residence, an EU labour
   market, and a passport pathway. Those are the returns. Not money.
2. **Which is exactly why the savings must be banked here.** She will not out-earn the UAE in
   Europe at this seniority once tax is applied. The ten months remaining are the highest
   net-saving months of her working life, and they do not come back.
