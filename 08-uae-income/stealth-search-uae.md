# Searching the UAE without being caught

Owned by the **UAE jobs session**. Written 31 July 2026, following
`.claude/skills/stealth-positioning/SKILL.md`.

---

## The rule that makes this simple

> **This search requires zero public activity. Change nothing on LinkedIn.**

The EU search benefits from an internationally legible public profile — that is what
`stealth-positioning` is for. **The UAE search does not benefit from public activity at all.**
UAE recruiters will find her through the profile she already has; every additional public
signal is pure downside risk with no upside.

So the entire protocol below is about **private channels and defensive settings**. Nothing she
posts, follows, likes or updates helps her here.

---

## Two things make the UAE harder than the EU

**1. UAE recruitment genuinely runs through LinkedIn**, more so than in Europe, where
specialist agencies, Xing and StepStone carry more weight. She cannot simply abandon the
platform. She can, however, make herself **passively findable and actively silent** — visible
in search results, invisible in the activity feed.

**2. Abu Dhabi's creative community is small.** Small enough that a direct approach to an Abu
Dhabi agency can reach her boss socially before it reaches an offer. Nothing in the LinkedIn
settings defends against a conversation at an industry event.

**Both point the same way, and it happens to match the money and the European route:
bias the search to Dubai employers and to in-house teams.** `target-employers.md` reaches the
same conclusion from pay and EU-office data; `move-or-stay.md` reaches it from non-compete
exposure. Three independent reasons, one answer — **do not approach Abu Dhabi agencies.**

## ✅ Safe channels, ranked

| # | Channel | Why it is safe |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Specialist recruiters — email, phone, WhatsApp** | Completely private. No platform activity, no observable trace. `pipeline-jobs.md` already established this for the EU: *"a recruiter conversation is completely private"* |
| **2** | **Direct approach to a named person by personal email** | Private. A note to a Creative Director or Head of Brand about their work is normal professional contact |
| **3** | **Company career portals directly** (Workday, SuccessFactors, Taleo, Greenhouse) | Leaves no LinkedIn trace whatsoever. Already the vault's standing rule — Adyen was flagged *"Greenhouse only"* |
| **4** | **Referrals from pre-WeDo colleagues** — Social Dar, CPI, the Ministry programme, DAIS | All past employers, all safe. Same list `application-history.md` uses for referees |
| **5** | **Gated salary-guide downloads** — MCG Talent, Sapio, Cooper Fitch, Michael Page | A form submission, not a public action |
| **6** | **LinkedIn: receiving and replying to recruiter DMs** | Messages are private. Replying to an inbound approach is invisible |

**WhatsApp is how UAE recruiting actually runs**, and it is private. From her personal number,
never a work device.

## ⛔ Unsafe — do not do any of these

| Action | Why |
|---|---|
| **`#OpenToWork`, in any setting including recruiters-only** | LinkedIn blocks recruiter seats registered to her employer — **but explicitly does not guarantee privacy**, and it fails against connected recruiters and personal accounts ([LinkedIn Help](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a507508/let-recruiters-know-you-re-open-to-work?lang=en)). **Her threat is not a recruiter seat. It is her boss reading her profile**, which no setting defends against. `stealth-positioning` bans it outright and that ban stands |
| **Following target companies** | Companies she follows are visible on her profile. Already flagged in the vault for PVH |
| **"I'm interested" / saving jobs** | Same exposure |
| **Applying to jobs through LinkedIn** | ⚠️ **Applying can auto-follow the employer.** Check and untick it — or better, apply on the company's own portal instead. This is the quiet one that catches people |
| **Commenting on recruiter or hiring posts** | Appears in her network's feed |
| **Joining "jobs in Dubai" groups** | Group membership is public |
| **A sudden profile overhaul** | Triggers "updated their profile" notifications to her whole network — including her boss |
| **Connecting with recruiters at target companies** | Connections are visible by default. See the settings fix below |
| **Approaching Abu Dhabi agencies** | Social overlap with WeDo. No setting protects against this |

## 🔒 The five LinkedIn settings — do these first, before anything else

| Setting | Where | Set to |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Share profile updates with your network** | Settings → Visibility → Visibility of your LinkedIn activity | **OFF.** Do this *before* editing anything. It is the single highest-value setting and it is off by default for nobody |
| **2. Profile viewing options** | Settings → Visibility → Profile viewing options | **Private mode** whenever browsing companies or recruiters |
| **3. Connections** | Settings → Visibility → Who can see your connections | **Only you** |
| **4. Open to Work** | — | **Leave off entirely** |
| **5. Contact info** | Profile → Contact info | **Personal email and personal mobile only.** Never the WeDo address |

Setting 1 is the one that matters most. Every other precaution is undone by a notification
saying she updated her headline.

## 🚨 The UAE-specific exposure almost nobody thinks about

**Gulf job boards run employer-searchable CV databases.** Bayt, GulfTalent and NaukriGulf let
employers with a recruiter account **search the CV pool directly** — a completely different
model from applying to a posting.

> **If WeDo or any of its clients holds a recruiter account on one of those boards, her CV
> appears in their search results the moment she uploads it.** She has not applied to them. She
> has not contacted them. Her CV is simply there, dated, with her current employer on it.

This is the UAE equivalent of `#OpenToWork`, it is far less well known, and it is worse —
because it is not a badge she can toggle off, it is a document sitting in a searchable index.

**If she uses those boards at all:**
- Set the profile to **confidential / not searchable by employers**, if the board offers it
- Apply **only to specific postings**, never leave a CV in the open pool
- **Do not upload a CV naming WeDo** to any open database

**Better: skip them.** Every channel in the safe list above works without them.

## 📩 The single most important instruction — brief every recruiter, in writing, first

**UAE recruiters routinely submit CVs to clients without asking first.** It is normal practice
here, not misconduct. And in the UAE agency market, WeDo's clients and competitors overlap
heavily with any target list.

**The exposure route nobody plans for is her CV landing on the desk of a WeDo client, sent by a
recruiter who was being helpful.**

So the confidentiality instruction is not a footnote at the end of a conversation. **It is the
first line of the first message, in writing, before the CV is attached:**

> *Before we go further, one condition on my side: my search is strictly confidential and my
> current employer must not learn of it. Please do not submit my CV, or name me, to any company
> without my written approval for that specific company first. If that works for you, I'll send
> my CV across.*

Three things that sentence does: it is unambiguous, it is dated and in writing, and **a
recruiter who ignores it has told her something useful about themselves early**, at no cost.

**Additional rules for recruiters:**
- Personal email and personal mobile only.
- Never name WeDo's clients when describing her work — *"a federal transport authority"*,
  *"a municipal government"*. `case-study-translator` already does this for the EU; the same
  discipline applies harder in a market where those clients are three phone calls away.
- Ask each recruiter which of their clients she should be excluded from, before they submit
  anything.

## Interviews and logistics

Carried forward from `application-history.md`, and all of it applies to UAE interviews too:

- **Never take a call from the WeDo office or on WeDo wifi.**
- Personal laptop, personal phone, personal email.
- Dubai interviews mean travel from Abu Dhabi — **do not take a half-day at short notice
  repeatedly.** Pattern is visible even when individual absences are not. Batch them, or use
  early mornings and Saturdays.
- **Never offer a WeDo referee.** Social Dar, the Ministry programme, DAIS, CPI.
- Nothing about the search on a work device or work account, ever.

## The two-reader test — applied

`stealth-positioning` requires both readings before anything public. **Applied to this whole
protocol: there is nothing public in it.**

- **As her boss:** he sees no profile changes, no new follows, no posts, no comments, no badge,
  no notifications. Nothing has happened.
- **As a UAE recruiter:** her existing profile is already strong — 22 years, government and
  institutional clients, brand and experience, CIM Level 7. She remains findable in search
  exactly as she is today, and reachable by DM.

**Both checked. The protocol passes because it adds no public surface at all.**

## If she wants to strengthen the profile anyway

There is a version that helps recruiters find her and stays safe, but the sequence matters:

1. **Turn off "share profile updates" first.** Wait a day.
2. Make edits in **one sitting**, not as a trickle over weeks.
3. Change **only vocabulary, never intent** — the register `stealth-positioning` calls for:
   *brand strategy, design systems, brand architecture, experience design, creative direction.*
   These describe what she already does.
4. **CIM Level 7 is completely safe to add**, and it is the single most valuable addition —
   `market-rate.md` finds an ~18% premium on the marketing title over the creative title in the
   same UAE dataset.
5. **Location stays Abu Dhabi, UAE.** Non-negotiable.
6. **Leave German off.** `stealth-positioning`: it is one of the few genuine tells and there is
   no upside to flagging it early.

**But the honest recommendation is to do nothing.** She has ten months, four private channels
and a strong existing profile. The marginal recruiter reached by a better headline does not
justify any risk of the conversation she cannot take back.
