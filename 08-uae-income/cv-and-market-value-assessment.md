# Honest assessment — CV, level and market value

Written 31 July 2026 at Sara's request: an unflattering read, not a pitch.
Source: `05-career-context/cv-source-of-truth.md`.

**Salary bands below are reasoned from market structure, not from a dataset.** Public UAE
salary aggregates are dominated by mid-level roles at local agencies and are close to
useless at her level. Treat every figure as an estimate and test it against live offers.

---

## 1. The title problem — the most important thing in this file

**Her CV header says Creative Director. Her current job title is Senior Art Director.**

She has genuinely held Creative Director roles: co-founder and CD of Social Dar for three
years, CD on the national curriculum programme, CD on DAIS 2019. That is not a false claim.
But her *current* employer titles her **Senior Art Director, Brand and Creative Lead**, and
that is the title a recruiter will verify and benchmark against.

This matters more than anything else here, because **titles set pay bands in the Gulf
market.** Going to market as a Senior Art Director gets Senior Art Director money, which is
roughly AED 10,000–15,000 a month below Creative Director money for the same person.

**The trajectory also reads non-linearly:**

| Period | Title | Direction |
|---|---|---|
| 2010–2015 | Head of Creative, Al Arab | Senior |
| 2015–2020 | Art Director, CPI Media Group | Reads as a step down |
| 2019–2021 | Creative Director, curriculum programme | Up (contract) |
| 2021–2024 | Co-Founder and Creative Director | Senior |
| 2025– | **Senior Art Director**, WeDo | Reads as a step down |

There are good explanations for every move: countries changed, sectors changed, she founded
a studio and then returned to employment, and Gulf titles are notoriously inconsistent
between a newspaper, a media group and an agency. But a recruiter reading cold sees a
zig-zag, and she should have a one-sentence answer ready rather than being surprised by the
question.

**Two other things a careful reader will notice:**

- **An eight-month gap**, June 2024 to February 2025, between Social Dar closing and WeDo
  starting. Needs a prepared, unembarrassed answer.
- **Overlapping dates** — CPI 2015–2020 runs across DAIS (2019) and the curriculum
  programme (2019–2021). The CV labels these as independent contracts, which handles it,
  but it will be asked about.

## 2. What is genuinely scarce

Separating the rare from the merely good, because only the rare commands a premium.

**Rare, and hard to hire for:**

1. **Native Arabic plus genuine Arabic typographic craft.** Most senior creatives in Dubai
   are expatriates who cannot art-direct Arabic. She built a **national typographic system
   across three scripts**. In a bilingual market this is the single most defensible thing
   she has, and it is almost certainly underpriced in her current salary.
2. **National-scale system delivery.** A €500,000 programme, 100+ books, five subjects,
   large multidisciplinary team. Very few creative directors anywhere have run something of
   that scale and consistency. This is the strongest evidence of seniority in the file.
3. **Ministerial and government stakeholder experience.** Presenting and defending work to
   ministers is high-trust, slow-earned and impossible to fake.
4. **Trilingual publishing including French at A2.** Layout and typographic craft in a
   language she does not speak fluently is a real, demonstrable credential.
5. **Commercial ownership.** Three years co-founding and running a studio: new business,
   pitching, client relationships, and the P&L consequences of losing them. Most
   agency-employed creative directors have never carried this.

**Good, but common at her level:**

Brand identity systems, campaign work, editorial design, team leadership, AI usage in
production. All real, all expected of a senior creative in 2026, none of them a premium on
their own. **AI in particular is now table stakes**, not a differentiator, though the
governance angle — codifying AI usage into client brand documentation — is genuinely ahead
of the market and worth keeping.

## 3. What is genuinely weak

- **Current title sits below her demonstrated scope.** See section 1.
- **No EU or Western-market experience.** Entirely MENA. For the UAE market this is fine.
  For the EU goal it is the central hurdle, and it is already recorded as such.
- **Figma.** Her own vault records it as a stated requirement in four separate postings
  screened in July. Depth is Adobe. This is a live, recurring gap.
- **Motion.** She directs it, she does not build it. Defensible, but it closes some doors.
- **Line management is unclear.** She has led project teams and directed vendors, but the
  CV does not establish whether she currently manages permanent direct reports. **Creative
  Director roles at larger organisations are often assessed on team leadership as much as
  craft.** If she does manage people, it should be explicit with a number. If she does not,
  that is a real constraint on the roles she can win.
- **Some metrics are soft.** "Contributed to a 12 percent government tender win rate" is
  weak phrasing: contributed how, and against what baseline? The +38 percent subscriber
  growth is strong but dates from 2015–2020. Recent quantified outcomes are thin.

**Where she realistically sits: Creative Director band.** Her scope of work, years and
ownership history support it; her current title does not, and that mismatch is the thing to
fix rather than a reason to lower the ask.

**Target: AED 40,000–55,000 a month at an international firm.** At the top of that range she
would need to win the argument on scarcity, which she can.

### The route most likely to pay best

**In-house at a large UAE corporate or semi-government entity** — the Etihads, Aldars,
Mubadalas, DP Worlds, major banks and developers. Reasons:

- They pay above agencies for senior brand people, consistently.
- Her government and institutional experience transfers directly.
- Bilingual Arabic and English capability is a requirement there, not a nice-to-have.
- The work is closer to what she already does than a consumer agency role would be.

**One caution:** federal government entities often have Emiratisation requirements for
senior positions. Semi-government, corporates and banks are considerably more open. This
needs verifying per employer rather than assuming.

## 5. The three levers, in order of value

**1. Fix the title before going to market.** The single highest-value action in this file.
Either negotiate a title change at WeDo, which costs them nothing, or apply exclusively for
Creative Director roles with the evidence to back it. Her record supports the title. Being
titled below her scope is quietly costing her five figures a month.

**2. Price the Arabic.** It is the scarcest thing she has and almost certainly not reflected
in her current pay. In any negotiation this is the argument: not "22 years of experience",
which everyone claims, but "I can art-direct Arabic typography at national-system scale,
and your alternative candidates cannot."

**3. Never anchor on her current salary.** If she is underpaid relative to these bands, that
number becomes the reference point for every subsequent offer. Ask for their range first.

---

## Bottom line

She is **operating at Creative Director level and titled below it.** The work is genuinely
senior and two elements of it are scarce enough to command a premium. The record does not
need improving before she goes to market; **the framing does.**

The gaps that matter for the EU goal — no European experience, Figma, motion — are already
recorded honestly elsewhere in the vault and are not what is holding her UAE salary down.
The title is.
