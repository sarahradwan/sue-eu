# Target employers — ranked on money AND on a European route

Owned by the **UAE jobs session**. Built 31 July 2026.

Per `README.md`: *"a slightly lower offer from a company with EU offices can be worth more
than the highest bidder locally."* This list scores both, and never collapses them into one
number without showing the parts.

---

## ⚠️ Read this before the table — the transfer thesis has a timing problem

The premise behind this whole list is that a multinational with EU offices gives her an
intra-company transfer route. **That premise is sound, and it does not work on her stated
timeline.** Better to say so now than to build a search around it.

**Internal mobility programmes almost universally require 12–24 months in the role before an
employee is eligible to apply for an international move.** If she joins a tier-1 employer in
November 2026 and intends to leave by mid-2027, she would be asking at month seven — below
the bar at most of these companies, and with no track record inside the business to make the
case with.

So the honest value of an EU-office employer **on her timeline** is not the transfer. It is
these four things, and every one of them is available in month one:

| What she actually gets | Why it matters | Available from |
|---|---|---|
| **The internal job board** | Group intranets list EU vacancies before or instead of public posting, and internal applicants are usually screened separately from the external pile. **It is also completely invisible to her employer** — no LinkedIn trace, no public application | Day 1 |
| **A European-legible employer name** | An Amsterdam recruiter recognises *Serviceplan*, *Accenture Song*, *PepsiCo*. Nobody in Rotterdam has heard of WeDo. This is CV currency, and `pipeline-jobs.md` already identifies unrecognised MENA employers as a screening problem | Day 1 |
| **European colleagues who become referees** | A reference from a Munich or Brussels colleague carries more with an EU employer than any UAE reference. `application-history.md` already rules out WeDo referees | Month 3–6 |
| **Internal recruiters in EU offices who take her call** | An internal enquiry is a conversation. An external application is a form | Month 3–6 |

**And there is a fifth option she should at least see, even though it is not what she asked
for.** If she joined a tier-1 employer in late 2026 and treated **mid-2028** as the transfer
date rather than mid-2027, the intra-company route becomes genuinely available — 18 months of
tenure, a track record, and an employer for whom her paperwork is routine. That is a real
alternative plan with a real cost of one extra year. **Her call entirely; recorded so she is
choosing rather than defaulting.**

## ⚖️ Weighting corrected, 2 Aug 2026 — after the ADNEC screen

The 1.5× weight on the European axis below **is too high for a mid-2027 departure**, and the
reason is stated in the box above: **an intra-company transfer needs 12–24 months of tenure, so
on this timeline it never had time to happen.** The weight was pricing an asset she cannot use.

What an EU-office employer actually delivers by mid-2027 is an internal job board, a
European-legible employer name and European referees. Real, but not worth 1.5× the money — and
the money now buys something the earlier ranking did not account for:

> **`savings-target.md` (31 Jul): at a large enough salary, savings reopen Germany's
> Opportunity Card, Austria's proof of funds and possibly Spain's savings limb. A job with zero
> European route can be the thing that opens three European routes, by funding them.**

**So: weight the European axis at 1.5× for a mid-2028 departure, and at 1.0× for mid-2027.**
The table below is unchanged and its ordering still holds on the longer timeline. On the
current timeline, read the money column first — and see
[`leads-screened.md`](leads-screened.md) for ADNEC Group, which scores 0 on Europe and is
nonetheless the strongest lead found.

## Scoring

**Money** 1–5 · **EU route** 1–5 · **Total = Money + (EU route × 1.5)**, max 12.5.

EU route is weighted at 1.5 because the README says to weight it heavily, and because money
here is only ever worth ten months of it while a European foothold is worth the rest of her
career. **See the correction above before using this on the mid-2027 timeline.**

**EU route scores what the same legal group can actually do for her**, not merely whether it
owns a building in Europe:
- **5** — same employer, formal international mobility, brand or creative people in EU offices
- **3** — same group, but transfers between operating companies are political or rare
- **1** — EU offices exist but no plausible path from a UAE creative role
- **0** — no EU presence at all

## Why #1 is #1

**Serviceplan is the best-shaped employer found for her, and it is not close.**

- **Brussels, Paris, Madrid, Milan and Vienna offices** — that is one preferred country
  (Belgium) and three acceptable ones, inside a single group.
- **Brussels specifically.** `pipeline-jobs.md`: *"Brussels institutional sector is the one EU
  market where native Arabic is a scarce asset"*, English-workable, and a €44,441 highly-skilled
  threshold — the second-lowest of any preferred country.
- **Munich-headquartered and German-speaking throughout.** Her German study stops being a
  hobby and becomes an internal advantage. `pipeline-jobs.md` already concluded German study
  should be aimed at Germany, and that at B1–B2 that whole market opens.
- **Owner-managed and partner-managed**, not a listed holding company. Fewer layers between a
  regional creative and someone in Munich who can say yes.
- **Dubai Design District**, which is the creative cluster rather than a back office — and
  **they have an Abu Dhabi office**, so a first move need not require relocating within the UAE.

**Second: Accenture Song.** Structurally the easiest transfer on the list, because consultancies
move people internationally as a matter of routine while agencies almost never move creatives.
Consulting pay is the highest here, and the client base — governments and institutions — is
precisely her 22 years. Song has offices in Dublin, Amsterdam, Madrid and Milan.

**Third, and the highest-leverage single item on this page: PepsiCo.**

`pipeline-jobs.md` already carries **Creative Excellence Senior Director, International
Beverages, Dublin 2** as a live lead, rated *"strongest Irish lead"*. The Dublin hub that role
sits in is the global beverages marketing and design hub — and **PepsiCo AMESA is headquartered
in Dubai.**

> **She could be applying to the exact Dublin role she already wants, as an internal
> candidate, from a Dubai office of the same company.**

That is a materially shorter path than an external application competing with the EU field, and
it is the only place on this list where the UAE job and the EU job are the same employer and the
same already-identified role. **If PepsiCo Dubai has anything at her level, it outranks a higher
offer anywhere else on this page.**

| **ADNOC · Mubadala · e& · G42 · First Abu Dhabi Bank** | Among the best-paying employers in the country | **0** |
| **Emirates · Etihad** | Large in-house creative teams, strong brand work | **0** — European stations, no European brand function |

These are legitimate targets **for money only**. If the honest answer to `move-or-stay.md` is
that she needs a large pay step and nothing else, this is where the largest steps are. But a
job here is ten months of higher savings and **zero** contribution to getting to Europe — and
several of them are Abu Dhabi employers where the social overlap with WeDo is highest, which is
the confidentiality problem in `stealth-search-uae.md`.

**One specific caution on the government-adjacent entities:** secondment structures, long notice
periods and multi-year commitments are common. Check the contract shape before the salary.

## Tier 3 — the trap

**Other Abu Dhabi agencies.** Lateral pay, no European route, maximum exposure risk — Abu Dhabi's
creative community is small enough that a direct approach can reach her boss socially before it
reaches an offer. **Worst option on every axis.** Do not spend search effort here.

## How to approach the tier-1 list — order matters

1. **PepsiCo first**, because the destination role is already identified and internal beats
   external. Check where the brand function sits post-Riyadh move.
2. **Serviceplan second.** They have an Abu Dhabi office; a first conversation need not even be a
   relocation within the UAE. `pipeline-jobs.md` records that Douglas, Mutabor and Moss all
   surfaced German-market employers hiring for exactly her AI positioning — Serviceplan is the
   same market, reachable from where she already lives.
3. **Accenture Song third.** Government and institutional experience is the entry argument.
4. **Radisson fourth** — smaller pay step, but Brussels HQ running global brand strategy is
   disproportionate value for a preferred country.

All four via the private channels in `stealth-search-uae.md`. **None via LinkedIn.**
