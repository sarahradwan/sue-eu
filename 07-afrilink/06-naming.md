# 6. The name

**Straight answer: "She Afrika" contradicts your own decision — yes, unambiguously. And
"Afrilink" is crowded enough that it is not a safe default either.**

Neither name clears. That is a less convenient answer than picking one, but naming into a
known conflict is how brands get renamed at the point they finally start working.

---

## 6.1 Does "She Afrika" contradict the branding decision? Yes.

The decision, from the brief:

> **Do not market as "women only".** Focus will naturally be women, but broader positioning
> leaves room to add men or mixed programmes later **without a rebrand**.

"She Afrika" places a gendered pronoun in the first word of the name. It is the most prominent,
least changeable element of the brand.

There is no reading under which this is compatible. The stated purpose of the decision was to
avoid a rebrand when mixed programmes are added; "She Afrika" *guarantees* that rebrand. It
does the precise thing the decision existed to prevent.

**This one is not a judgement call, and it does not need debating with Hadeer.** The founders
made a strategic decision; one of the two candidate names violates it on its face. Either the
decision stands and the name goes, or the decision is being quietly reversed — in which case
reverse it explicitly, in the open, rather than through a name choice.

Worth adding: the decision itself is sound. Pillar 3 (corporate training) and pillar 5
(cultural events) both have obvious mixed-audience versions, and corporate L&D buyers —
`04-financial-model.md` §4.4, the only genuinely profitable stream — rarely commission
women-only training. **A gendered name would narrow the single revenue line that works.**

---

## 6.2 "Afrilink" — available in principle, crowded in practice

**Search-verified: at least four existing organisations use the name.**

| Entity | What it is | Conflict risk |
|---|---|---|
| **[AFRILINK Entrepreneurs International](https://www.idealist.org/en/nonprofit/c6d774d5765f4a8187ba6130a19a2ac8-afrilink-entrepreneurs-international-providence)** | US non-profit (Providence, RI). Mission: "enabling change in the status of **African youth** through enterprise" — operations across Africa incl. Kenya. [LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/company/afrilink-international-entrepreneurs), [Facebook](https://www.facebook.com/AfrilinkEntrepreneurs/) | **Highest.** Non-profit, African, youth, enterprise development — near-identical sector to Afrilink's pillars 2 and 4 |
| **[Afrilink Global](https://b2bhint.com/en/company/za/afrilink-global--K2013079818)** | South African company, reg. K2013079818, Cape Town, inc. 2013 | Moderate — Anglophone Africa |
| **[Afrilink Consultants](https://afrilinkconsultants.com/)** | Business consultancy, Kenya — investor support in Africa | Moderate — "business support" overlaps pillar 2 |
| **[Afrilink Select](https://www.afrilinkselect.com/aboutus.html)** | Brand management, Shenzhen — African-origin products | Low |

**The first one is the problem.** A US non-profit working on African youth enterprise
development, with an active LinkedIn and Facebook presence, is the closest thing to a direct
sector collision. It makes Afrilink hard to find, hard to distinguish, and — if it ever
registers a trademark in a market Afrilink expands into — a genuine legal obstacle.

Remember Year 3 targets Europe. **A name that collides with an established African-development
non-profit is a poor foundation for a brand intended to operate in the Netherlands, France,
Germany and Belgium**, where that organisation's footprint and any EU trademark would matter far
more than they do in Abu Dhabi.

### Also, as a brand judgement

The `-link` suffix is generic and dated — it reads telecom, logistics, B2B directory,
2005. It says *connection infrastructure*, not *community, ambition, belonging*. For an
organisation whose entire proposition is warmth and sisterhood, the name is working against the
brief. Sara will already know this; it is noted because "Afrilink" appears to be the default
purely by virtue of not being "She Afrika", and a default is not a decision.

## 6.3 Domains and handles — could not verify

**Unverified, and the tooling is the reason.** DNS resolution and outbound HTTP are both
blocked in this environment (`01-legal-uae.md`, research constraint note). No domain or handle
availability could be checked directly.

What *is* known from search results: `afrilinkconsultants.com`, `afrilinkselect.com` and
`b2bhint`'s South African record all exist, so the `.com` namespace around "afrilink" is
populated. **`afrilink.com` is very unlikely to be free** — a 9-character dictionary-style
compound registered during the 2000s almost never is. **[Assessment, not verified.]**

### The 20-minute check to run before deciding anything

| Check | Where |
|---|---|
| Domain availability — `.com`, `.ae`, `.org`, `.co` | Any registrar; check WHOIS, not just the "buy" page |
| Instagram, LinkedIn, TikTok, X handles | Try to register. Availability search lies; the signup form does not |
| **UAE trademark** | Ministry of Economy trademark search |
| **International trademark** | WIPO Global Brand Database — free, covers most registries |
| **EU trademark** | EUIPO eSearch — **do this one**, because Year 3 is Europe |
| Plain search | The name + "Africa", + "women", + "network" |

**Rule: one name, all handles, one owner.** Matching handles across platforms matters more than
the `.com` for a community brand — the audience finds you on Instagram and LinkedIn, not by
typing a URL. If the `.com` is gone but every handle is free, that is a workable trade.
The reverse is not.

## 6.4 How each reads in Arabic

**Sara is a native Arabic speaker and a brand professional — she is better placed to judge this
than I am. Offered as a first read to react to, not a finding.**

### "Afrilink" → أفريلينك

- Transliterates cleanly. "Link" is an established English loanword in Gulf business Arabic
- No negative meaning, no unfortunate homophone
- Reads as foreign/corporate — neutral. Neither warm nor offensive
- Slightly awkward: the final `-nk` cluster is not native to Arabic phonology, so it is spoken
  as a foreign word, never naturalised
- **Verdict: safe, unloved**

### "She Afrika" → شي أفريكا

Two separate problems, and the first is serious:

1. **"شي" (shay) means "thing" in Gulf and Levantine colloquial Arabic.** To a large share of
   the UAE's Arabic-speaking population, "She Afrika" reads or sounds like **"thing Africa" /
   "something Africa."** The intended English pronoun does not survive the crossing. In a
   country where the audience, the venue partners, the sponsors and the regulators are
   substantially Arabic-speaking, that is a real cost — not a subtle nuance.

2. **"Afrika" with a *kāf* is a non-standard transliteration.** Standard Arabic for Africa is
   **إفريقيا** (*Ifrīqiyā*), with a *qāf*. Rendering it أفريكا reads as a foreign misspelling —
   the way "Afrika" looks in English.

- **Verdict: actively problematic in Arabic, on top of contradicting the branding decision**

## 6.5 Recommendation

**Drop "She Afrika" outright.** It breaks the founders' own decision, narrows the only
profitable revenue stream, and reads badly in the local language.

**Do not default to "Afrilink."** It is not disqualified, but it is crowded in exactly the
sector and geography that matter, and it is a weak brand for a warmth-led community.

**Name it properly, once, before anything is public.** Renaming is cheap now — nothing is
printed, no handles are established, no members know the name. Renaming after 200 people know
you is expensive and visible.

### Constraints for the third option

No name suggestions here — that is Sara's actual profession, and a list from me would be the
least useful thing in this document. What is missing from the plan is not creative; it is the
**clearance discipline**. So:

1. **Not gender-locked** — the decision already taken, and correct
2. **Clears trademark** in UAE *and* EU, checked before adoption, not after
3. **Handles available** across Instagram, LinkedIn, TikTok
4. **Works in Arabic** — spoken and written, no colloquial collision, standard إفريقيا if Africa
   is named at all
5. **Travels to Europe** — it must work in Amsterdam and Brussels in 2029, not only Abu Dhabi
   in 2027
6. **Not `-link`, `-hub`, `-connect`, `-bridge`** — the entire category is saturated and dated
7. **Sayable once and remembered** — this brand spreads by word of mouth in WhatsApp groups.
   If it cannot be spelled correctly after being heard once, it does not spread

**Sequencing note:** naming is the most enjoyable decision here and the least urgent. Per
`01-legal-uae.md`, the contract, Hadeer's visa status and the lawyer conversation come first —
a name for a venture whose legal shape is unresolved may not fit the shape it ends up taking.
An organisation that becomes a programme inside a host body (Option D) may not need a
standalone brand at all.
