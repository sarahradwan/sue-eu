# Afrilink / She Afrika — venture brief

Captured 25 July 2026 from Sara's separate planning conversation. **Not yet evaluated.**
Nothing here is verified or costed. The evaluation session's job is to test it.

Working names under consideration: **Afrilink** or **She Afrika**. Not decided; to be
agreed with Hadeer.

## Vision

A long-term community and empowerment platform starting in the UAE, expanding to the GCC
and eventually Europe. Explicitly **not** a quick-money play: the stated goal is a
respected organisation with real impact and a sustainable model.

## Mission

Empower African women through professional networking, skills development, leadership
training, business support, marketing and branding consultation, youth development, and
cultural connection.

**Branding decision already taken:** do not market as "women only". Focus will naturally be
women, but broader positioning leaves room to add men or mixed programmes later without a
rebrand.

## Team

| Person | Role |
|---|---|
| **Sara** | Branding, marketing, strategy, community, partnerships, events, business support, media consultations |
| **Hadeer** | L&D, training programmes, workshop design, youth development, corporate training |
| **Ahmed** | Photography, video, event production |
| **Yomni** | Supports larger events |

Volunteers and freelancers as needed. **No employees planned initially.**

## The five pillars

1. **Community & networking** — monthly events, sisterhood circles
2. **Business support** — branding, marketing strategy, social, positioning, startup guidance
3. **Learning & development** — leadership, communication, EQ, workplace and career skills
4. **Youth programme** — girls ~14–22: confidence, leadership, entrepreneurship, LinkedIn, career prep
5. **Cultural events** — African culture, with business relationship-building

## Revenue model

**Initial:** event tickets cover venue, refreshments and food only. Not intended to profit.

**Later:** workshops, training, marketing and branding consultations, corporate training,
memberships, marketplace commissions, sponsorships, digital products, youth programmes.

## Zero-budget strategy

Funded by founder contributions and cost-covering ticket sales. Growth via partnerships,
volunteers, freelancers and community ambassadors. Leverage cafés, coworking spaces, small
businesses, sponsors and embassies instead of spending.

## Launch plan

- **Phase 1** — Instagram, LinkedIn, WhatsApp community. Intro content. Free coffee meetup.
- **Phase 2** — First official event. Cost-covering tickets. Collect emails, WhatsApp
  contacts, testimonials, photos, video.
- **Phase 3** — One event per month. Consistency over scale.

**Launch-phase free workshops:** LinkedIn basics, personal branding, confidence, CV
writing, interview skills, zero-budget startup, marketing basics, time management, public
speaking, emotional wellbeing.

## Growth

- **Year 1** — UAE only. Community, brand, monthly events, workshops, success stories.
- **Year 2** — GCC chapters: Saudi, Qatar, Kuwait, Bahrain.
- **Year 3+** — Europe via African diaspora: UK, Netherlands, France, Germany, Belgium.

## Risks already identified by the founders

1. **Inconsistency** — communities die when events stop
2. **Lack of value** — networking alone is insufficient
3. **Weak positioning** — trying to be everything

## Success factors already identified

One quality event monthly · consistent value · a core of 10–15 highly engaged members who
become ambassadors.

## Website

Simple landing page: hero, about, programmes, upcoming event, join form, founders, contact.
Promotion initially through LinkedIn.

# ⚠️ Questions this brief does not answer

Recorded here so the evaluation session starts from the hard parts, not the vision.

## 1. Legal standing in the UAE — potential kill-switch

- Sara holds a **UAE employment visa sponsored by WeDo**. What does UAE law and a standard
  UAE employment contract permit regarding outside commercial activity?
- Charging for workshops, training or consultations is **commercial activity requiring a
  licence**. What licence, what cost, and can it be held by someone on another employer's
  visa?
- What is actually involved in registering a community organisation or association in the
  UAE as expatriates? This is materially harder than in most jurisdictions.
- Does even cost-covering ticketing count as commercial activity?

**Nothing else matters until this is answered.** It determines whether the venture is
legal, what shape it must take, and whether Sara can be a named founder at all right now.

## 2. Collision with Sara's existing commitments

Between now and the EU application window she already carries: a full-time job, CIM Level 7,
an IELTS sitting she has never taken (Sept–Oct 2026), and a document legalisation chain of
6–10 weeks that gates both relocation routes. Plus LinkedIn twice weekly.

The evaluation must answer honestly whether a monthly-event commitment is **compatible with
that**, or whether it competes with the things that have hard deadlines.

## 3. The relocation interaction — genuinely double-edged

**Against:** Year 1 is "UAE only", and Sara intends to leave the UAE by mid-2027. Founding a
UAE-anchored community and then departing 12–18 months later is a real tension, for her and
for the members and co-founders relying on her.

**For:** founding and running an organisation is exactly the evidence her strongest
scholarship routes select on. **SI Global Professionals weighs leadership and professional
experience and has no GPA gate** — the scheme where her 2003 grade does not count against
her. Scholarship motivation letters must answer "why now, and what will you do with it";
"expand this organisation into European African-diaspora communities" is a genuine,
verifiable answer, and Year 3 of this plan already names the Netherlands, France, Germany
and Belgium — her target countries.

So this could be the thing that *makes* the scholarship application compelling rather than
a distraction from it. **But only if the timing and the handover to Hadeer are deliberate
rather than accidental.**

## 4. Profit potential — to be tested, not assumed

Early revenue is founders selling their own time (workshops, consultations). Memberships,
marketplace commissions and digital products need scale that does not exist yet. The
evaluation needs real numbers: realistic attendance, ticket prices, venue costs, workshop
pricing against the actual Abu Dhabi and Dubai market, and an honest answer on whether
year 1 breaks even.

## 5. The naming inconsistency

**"She Afrika" contradicts the branding decision already taken.** The founders agreed not
to explicitly limit the organisation to women so that men or mixed programmes could be
added without a rebrand. "She Afrika" hard-codes the limitation into the name — the exact
outcome the decision was meant to avoid. "Afrilink" does not.

Also unchecked for either name: trademark conflicts, domain and handle availability, and
whether the name works in Arabic.
