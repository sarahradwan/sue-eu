# Afrilink — evaluation verdict

**Evaluated 27 July 2026.** Full working in `01-legal-uae.md` … `06-naming.md`.

---

## The verdict in one paragraph

**This is a good idea, at the wrong time, in the wrong legal structure, at four times the
scale it should launch at.** It is not a bad idea and it should not be abandoned. But as
currently described it is illegal for Sara in three of its five pillars, it does not fit in
the calendar between now and February 2027, it would add little to a 2027 scholarship
application while damaging the preparation that application depends on, and it loses roughly
AED 16,000–28,000 in year one. Every one of those problems has a fix, and all four fixes point
at the same structure.

## The thing that matters most

Not any single finding, but the way two of them combine.

Sara's entire EU plan runs on being **employed, solvent and legally resident in Abu Dhabi**
through spring 2027. The salary funds the attestation, the IELTS fees and the applications. The
job is what makes the CV say *current senior role*. The visa is what makes her legally present.
The eligibility filter records that she has **no capital to self-fund**.

Afrilink, as described, has her publicly offering brand and marketing consultation — her
employer's core commercial activity — while employed by that employer, on that employer's visa,
in a way her LinkedIn-monitoring boss will see.

**The downside is not "the venture fails."** The downside is a contract dispute in the eight
weeks around the Swedish deadline, with the visa, the salary and the plan attached to it.

The upside for year 1 is a few thousand dirhams, a community of ~30, and a leadership credential
she demonstrably does not need. **That asymmetry is the finding**, and no amount of careful
execution changes its shape.

## What to do instead

Four independent lines of analysis — legal, calendar, relocation, financial — converged on the
same structure without being designed to. That convergence is the strongest signal in this
evaluation.

### Now → February 2027

1. **Read the WeDo contract** — outside work, conflict of interest, non-compete. 20 minutes.
   Nothing else can be decided first.
2. **Ask Hadeer her exact visa type.** One message. If she is on a spouse/family visa, the
   venture has a clean legal front person and the whole picture improves.
3. **Run one free, private, unticketed coffee meetup** — late September, after the IELTS sitting.
   Half a day. No licence needed. **This is the demand test**, and demand is currently unproven.
   Then ask attendees whether they would pay AED 100, and see who returns for a paid second
   event. Under AED 500 answers the central business question.
4. **Recruit two or three Sub-Saharan African women as co-founders or founding advisers.** Cheap,
   fast, and it answers the identity question before it becomes the criticism that stalls growth.
5. **Let Hadeer build the operational spine** — programme design, licensing, curriculum. If she
   can carry it for six months while Sara is heads-down, that is the strongest possible evidence
   the venture survives Sara's 2027 departure. Better to learn it now than when a community
   depends on the answer.
6. **Do not launch publicly. Do not announce on LinkedIn. Do not name it yet.**

### March 2027 onward

7. **Launch properly** — applications submitted, IELTS done, attestation done, real capacity back.
8. **Hadeer fronts it.** Named founder, licence holder, public face. Sara advises ≤8h/month and
   is named publicly only after leaving WeDo.
9. **One pillar: community and networking.** Business support and L&D become the paid arm at
   month 9+. Cultural events are a format, not a pillar. Youth is year 3, with an institutional
   partner.
10. **Do not pay for venues.** Run inside an already-licensed host — a coworking operator, a
    chamber, an embassy. It solves the legal problem and the financial problem in one move,
    taking year 1 from −AED 16,250 to roughly break-even.

## What this costs the plan if ignored

Stated once, plainly, because it was asked for straight:

> Launching Afrilink publicly in autumn 2026 risks the visa and the salary that the EU
> relocation runs on, in exchange for a credential Sara already has, during the only months in
> which the 2027 application can be built. If the venture must exist in 2026, it should exist
> privately, quietly, in Hadeer's name, at a fraction of this scale.

## Confidentiality — flagged as required

Per `CLAUDE.md` and `stealth-positioning`: **a public launch is visible to her employer.**

It says nothing about relocation, so it does not breach the letter of the rule. It breaches the
spirit in a way that may be worse — it makes her *interesting* to the person monitoring her, for
six months, starting now; it is a plausible contract breach in his own commercial field; and it
degrades the cover story that currently explains her attestation chasing, her IELTS leave day
and her CIM study time. Full analysis in `01-legal-uae.md` §1.6.

**There is no stealth version of founding a public community — publicity is the point.** This
venture and the confidentiality strategy are in direct conflict, and the conflict cannot be
designed away, only accepted or deferred. The only structure that resolves it is Sara not being
the public founder.
