# 4. The money, with real numbers

**Verdict up front: this is a credibility and network asset, subsidised by the founders. It is
not a profit business in year 1 or year 2.**

Cost-covering tickets do not cover costs at realistic attendance. The gap is roughly
**AED 1,000–1,500 per event**, which is the founders' money. The only revenue that is real in
year 1 is the founders selling their own time — and that revenue is attributable to *their*
reputations, not to the brand.

---

## Sourcing note

Venue and training figures below are **search-verified market rates** from published sources.
Attendance, no-show rates, café terms and per-event permit fees could **not** be verified —
government fee schedules were unreachable (see `01-legal-uae.md`). Those are marked
**[EST]** and are my estimates. They are the softest numbers here and the model is sensitive to
them, so treat every **[EST]** as a thing to replace with a real quote before committing.

quoted.

the AED 1,182 15-person boardroom. **Get three real quotes before trusting this.**

**Café and coworking partnerships — the brief's zero-budget assumption.** Actual terms could
not be verified; no published café hire terms for Abu Dhabi were reachable. What such deals
typically look like **[EST]**:

| Model | Typical shape | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| Minimum spend | AED 60–120/head, guests order directly | No venue cost to Afrilink, but attendees pay more and it is not "free" to them |
| Off-peak free hire | Sunday/Monday daytime, quiet hours | Free, but the worst slots for working professionals |
| Revenue share | Venue keeps F&B, no hire fee | Best case. Needs a venue that wants the footfall |
| Coworking community slot | Free/discounted for member-relevant events | Realistic if a founder holds a membership (AED 250/mo) |

**The coworking route is the strongest lead and the cheapest thing to test.** A AED 250/month
Witwork membership plausibly buys community-event access — worth one phone call, and it would
change the model more than any other single variable.

## 4.2 Attendance — [EST], and the model turns on it

No verifiable data on UAE diaspora community event attendance. These are my estimates,
built from the mechanics of a launch with zero brand equity and a personal-network seed list.

| | Pessimistic | **Base** | Optimistic |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Event 1** — invited | 40 | 60 | 80 |
| RSVP (~50%) | 18 | 28 | 40 |
| **Attend** (60–70% of RSVP) | **11** | **18** | **28** |
| **Event 6** — reached | 150 | 300 | 500 |
| RSVP | 25 | 45 | 70 |
| **Attend** | **16** | **30** | **48** |

Two things drive this and both are unglamorous:

- **No-show rates for free and low-priced community events are high — 30–50% [EST].** Price is
  the lever: a paid ticket, even AED 50, materially improves show rates. Free RSVPs are close
  to worthless as a forecast.
- **Growth is not automatic.** Event 6 being bigger than event 1 assumes consistent quality and
  active recruitment. Communities plateau fast without paid promotion. The brief's own risk #1
  — inconsistency — is exactly what flattens this curve.

**Base case: ~18 at the first event, ~30 by the sixth.** That is a *successful* outcome for a
volunteer-run launch, and it is nowhere near the scale any of the later revenue streams need.

## 4.3 Per-event economics — base case

Abu Dhabi, 25 attendees, mid-range assumptions:

| Line | Cost | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Venue (partner/coworking rate) | AED 1,500 | [EST] — below the 2,000–5,000 commercial range, assuming a deal |
| F&B @ AED 60/head × 25 | AED 1,500 | [EST] |
| Print, collateral, signage | AED 400 | [EST] — low, because Sara designs it |
| Speaker thank-you, misc | AED 300 | [EST] |
| Photography / video | AED 0 | In kind (Ahmed) |
| Design, marketing, admin | AED 0 | In kind (Sara) |
| **Total direct cost** | **AED 3,700** | |
| **Cost per attendee** | **AED 148** | |

Now the revenue side, at a ticket price a new community can actually charge:

| Line | AED |
|---|---|
| 25 tickets @ AED 100 | 2,500 |
| Less DCT levy, 10% of gross ([verified](https://dct.gov.ae/en/e.services/events.licensing.system.aspx)) | −250 |
| Less ticketing platform fee ~7% [EST] | −175 |
| **Net to Afrilink** | **2,075** |
| **Shortfall vs cost** | **−1,625** |

### The finding

> **To genuinely cover costs at 25 attendees, the ticket must be about AED 160 — not AED 100.**
> Once the 10% DCT levy and platform fees are included, true break-even is ~AED 178.

**"Cost-covering tickets" is not a neutral, self-financing mechanism. It is a price point that
does not cover costs.** Either the founders subsidise every event, or they charge AED 175+ —
which is premium pricing for an unknown community brand and will suppress the attendance the
whole model depends on.

**Annualised**, on 10 events (Sept–June, the cadence [IBWG Abu Dhabi](https://ibwgabudhabi.org/)
actually runs):

| Scenario | Per event | **Year 1 founder subsidy** |
|---|---|---|
| Pessimistic — 15 attend, AED 75 ticket, commercial venue | −AED 2,800 | **−AED 28,000** |
| **Base — 25 attend, AED 100 ticket, partner venue** | −AED 1,625 | **−AED 16,250** |
| Optimistic — 35 attend, AED 125 ticket, free venue | +AED 190 | **+AED 1,900** |

Add the fixed legal costs from `01-legal-uae.md` if they licence:

| Item | AED/yr |
|---|---|
| twofour54 freelance permit | 3,500–7,500 |
| Event permits × 10 | **unverified** — could not reach DCT/DET fee schedules |
| Website, domain, tools | 1,500–3,000 [EST] |
| Lawyer consultation (one-off) | 1,000–2,500 [EST] |

**Realistic year-1 cash position: between −AED 20,000 and −AED 40,000**, borne by the founders,
before anyone is paid for a single hour. Split across four people that is manageable
(~AED 5,000–10,000 each). It is not nothing, and it should be agreed explicitly *before*
launch, not discovered in month four. Unspoken cost-sharing is one of the most common ways
co-founder relationships fail.

Note the optimistic row: break-even needs **all three** of high attendance, premium pricing and
a free venue. **The venue is the swing factor.** Free venue alone moves the base case from
−AED 16,250 to roughly −AED 1,250. Which is the real strategic conclusion: *do not pay for
venues* — Option D in `01-legal-uae.md` is a financial strategy, not just a legal one.

## 4.4 What the paid work actually commands — search-verified

The good news, and it is genuinely good: **the UAE training market pays well.**

| Offer | Market rate | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Half-day corporate workshop | **AED 5,000–15,000** | [BMC](https://www.bmc.net/blog/general-blog-posts/management-and-leadership-training-cost-dubai) |
| Half-day, specialist (e.g. AI) | AED 8,000–15,000 | [sawankr](https://sawankr.com/courses/go-highlevel/ai-training-for-business-teams-in-uae-2026-what-an-ai-trainer-actually-does) |
| **Full-day corporate** | **AED 15,000–30,000** | same |
| Multi-day programme | AED 25,000–100,000 | same |
| Short leadership workshop | from AED 3,500 | [BMC](https://www.bmc.net/blog/general-blog-posts/management-and-leadership-training-cost-dubai) |
| Public soft-skills course, per seat | AED 1,000–34,900; **average AED 3,686** | [Laimoon](https://courses.laimoon.com/uae/soft-skills/fees) |

**One full-day corporate engagement at AED 20,000 outweighs an entire year of event ticketing.**
That is the single most important number in this document, and it reframes the venture: the
events are not the business. The events are marketing for the business.

**But three constraints, and they are severe:**

1. **It requires a licence** (`01-legal-uae.md` §1.2), and — if publicly advertised — plausibly
   KHDA/ADEK approval (§1.5). The closed-B2B carve-out is the route to check.
2. **Corporates do not buy training from six-month-old community brands.** They buy from known
   providers, or from individuals with credentials. Which leads to:
3. **This revenue is founder-attributable, not brand-attributable.** If Hadeer sells a corporate
   L&D day, she sells it on her L&D record. If Sara sells a branding consultation, she sells it
   on 22 years and a €500k national programme. **Neither sale requires Afrilink to exist.**

That third point deserves to be uncomfortable, because it undermines the revenue case: **the
only genuinely lucrative stream in the plan is one the founders could pursue without founding
anything.** Afrilink adds a client-acquisition channel and a credential — real, but second-order.
It does not create the earning capacity; they already have it.

**Realistic year-1 paid work: 0–2 engagements, AED 0–30,000 combined [EST]**, sold on personal
reputation, and only if the licensing question is resolved first.

## 4.5 Which streams need scale they will not have

| Stream | Needs | Have | Realistic start |
|---|---|---|---|
| Event tickets | 30+ paying attendees | 0 | Month 1 — **loss-making** |
| Workshops / consultations | A licence + buyers | Licence unresolved | Month 6+, **if** legal |
| **Corporate training** | Licence + corporate relationships | Personal credibility only | Month 9–12 — **the real revenue** |
| Sponsorship | ~100 engaged members, a media kit, an invoicing entity | 0 | Month 12–18 |
| Memberships | **150–300 paying members [EST]** | 0 | **Year 3.** At AED 300/yr × 200 = AED 60,000 — but 200 paying members is a large community |
| Digital products | Audience in the thousands | 0 | Year 2–3 |
| **Marketplace commissions** | Transaction volume **+ handling third-party money** | 0 | **Year 3+, and legally the hardest.** Taking a cut of others' transactions raises payment-handling questions well beyond a freelance permit — do not plan around this |
| Youth programmes | Safeguarding regime (`05-concept-and-market.md`) | Nothing | **Cut from year 1** |

**Four of the eight named streams need a scale of community that does not exist and will not
exist within Sara's UAE timeline.** The brief already knows this — "memberships, marketplace
commissions and digital products need scale that does not exist yet." Confirmed, and the
marketplace one is worse than assumed, because it is a regulatory problem as well as a scale
problem.

## 4.6 So what is it?

The question was: profit business, credibility and network asset, or non-profit needing
sponsorship? All three are valid; pretending it is the first when it is the second is not.

**It is the second, with a path to the third, and it is not the first.**

| | Assessment |
|---|---|
| **Profit business** | ❌ Not in year 1 or 2. Events lose AED 16,000–28,000/yr. The profitable stream — corporate training — is founder-attributable and needs a licence the founders may not be able to hold |
| **Credibility and network asset** | ✅ **This is what it is.** A platform, a body of proof, a network, a client-acquisition channel, and a leadership credential. Cost: ~AED 20,000–40,000 and ~350 founder-hours in year 1 |
| **Non-profit needing sponsorship** | ⚠️ The natural year-2–3 destination — but the UAE association route is effectively closed (`01-legal-uae.md` §1.3), so it would be a *commercially licensed* entity operating non-profit in practice. Sponsorship is the most realistic external money: diaspora-facing banks, remittance operators, telecoms and corporate DEI budgets all have relevant budget lines **[EST — no verified UAE sponsorship benchmarks found]** |

### The honest sentence

> **Afrilink is a marketing and credibility investment of roughly AED 20,000–40,000 and 350+
> founder-hours in year one, which returns network, proof and positioning — not cash.**

That is a perfectly good reason to do it. It is not a reason to call it a business, and it is
not a reason to do it *this* year — see `02-calendar-collision.md`.

**The one change that most improves these numbers:** stop paying for venues. Option D — running
inside an already-licensed host — solves the legal problem and the financial problem with the
same move. It takes the base case from −AED 16,250 to roughly break-even, and removes the event
permit burden entirely.
