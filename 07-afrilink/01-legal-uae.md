## 1.1 What her employment visa and contract allow

**Search-verified.** UAE work authorisation is tied to a specific employer. Under Federal
Decree-Law No. 33 of 2021, work permits are issued per employer, and working for anyone else
requires its own permit — most relevantly a **part-time work permit** from MOHRE, which
"allows establishments registered with the Ministry to employ employees under a part-time
employment contract" and, once granted, lets employees "work for more than one employer."
([MOHRE](https://www.mohre.gov.ae/en/our-services/part-time-work-permit-2022.aspx),
[dubai.ae](https://www.dubai.ae/living/jobs-employment/part-time-employment))

The condition that matters: reporting on the eligibility rules states that all residents
other than a "highly talented" category "must have written consent from their primary sponsor
and a valid MOHRE-approved permit" — and that applicants must already hold a full-time job
with a valid work permit. ([uaevisaonline](https://uaevisaonline.com/articles/uae-part-time-work-permit),
[MOHRE service page](https://www.mohre.gov.ae/en/services/part-time-work-permit-2022))
*Single-source on the exact NOC wording; the underlying requirement for employer consent is
search-verified across results.*

**Separately, her contract.** UAE employment contracts routinely restrict outside work, and
Article 10 of the Labour Law permits non-compete clauses limited by time, place and type of
work where the employee has had access to clients or business secrets.
([BSA](https://bsalaw.com/insight/enforceability-and-limits-of-non-compete-clauses-under-the-new-uae-labour-law/),
[Mayer Brown](https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2024/07/restrictive-covenants-uae))

This is the sharpest point in the whole legal analysis, so it gets stated bluntly:

> **Sara is a Creative Director at an advertising agency. Afrilink's pillar 2 is "branding,
> marketing strategy, social, positioning." That is her employer's core commercial activity.**

A non-compete does not need to be exotic to bite here. Selling brand and marketing
consultation while employed by a brand and marketing agency is the textbook case such clauses
exist for. Even if she never touches a WeDo client, the *activity* competes. **She needs to
read her actual contract before anything else** — specifically the outside-work, conflict-of-
interest and non-compete clauses. That is a 20-minute task and it is the single highest-value
action in this document.

### What breach actually costs her

Not a fine. The exposure is structural, because the visa and the job are the same object.

| Consequence | Basis |
|---|---|
| Termination for breach of contract | Contractual, not statutory |
| Work permit and residence visa cancelled on termination | MOHRE cancels the permit first, then the residence visa ([MOHRE](https://www.mohre.gov.ae/en/services/cancellation-of-work-permits-and-employment-contracts-2022)) |
| Grace period to leave or re-sponsor | Reported as 30–180 days under the ICP framework by skill level, then AED 50/day overstay ([dubaiupdater](https://www.dubaiupdater.com/uae-visa-cancellation-rules/)) — *single-source* |
| Fines / possible detention / deportation for unlicensed work | Working without the correct permit is reported to carry worker-side fines up to AED 50,000, plus deportation and re-entry ban ([connectfreelance](https://connectfreelance.ae/risk-of-working-without-work-permit-uae/), [Ayshams Law](https://ayshamslaw.com/working-on-a-visit-visa-in-uae-legal-risks-and-consequences/)) — *single-source on the AED 50,000 figure* |
| Employer-side fine | Art. 60, as amended by Federal Decree-Law No. 9 of 2024: reported at AED 100,000–1,000,000 for employing without a permit ([qureos](https://www.qureos.com/gcc/uae/penalties)) — relevant because *Afrilink* would be the offending employer if it ever engages anyone |

**Read the second and third rows together.** Losing the job means losing the visa, on a clock
of 30–180 days. Sara's entire EU plan assumes she is sitting in Abu Dhabi, employed and
solvent, through to at least spring 2027 — running the attestation chain, sitting IELTS,
paying for it all from salary, and applying with a current senior role on the CV.

A dismissal in, say, November 2026 does not merely cost her a job. It costs her the salary
funding the applications, the current-employment status that makes her CV credible, and
possibly her legal residence — in the exact eight weeks when the Swedish deadline lands. The
eligibility filter already records that she has **no capital to self-fund**
(`01-relocation/eligibility-filter.md`). There is no buffer under this risk.

**That asymmetry is the finding.** The upside of year-1 Afrilink is a few thousand dirhams
and a community. The downside is the relocation plan.

## 1.2 The licence question

**Search-verified: yes, charging requires a licence.** Delivering paid workshops, training or
consultations is commercial activity and needs a trade licence. There is no informal tier.

### Which licence, and what it costs

*All figures are advertised prices from commercial setup agents, not official schedules.
Treat as an order-of-magnitude range, not a quote.*

| Route | Reported cost | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Free-zone freelance permit, generic | "from AED 1,070" ([jsb.ae](https://jsb.ae/blog/uae-freelance-licenses-starting-at-just-dh1070-unlock-your-talent/)) | Lowest advertised; almost certainly permit-only, no visa, no establishment card |
| **twofour54** (Abu Dhabi media free zone) freelance permit | AED 3,538.50 inc. VAT, residency optional ([diac](https://diac.ae/blog/twofour54-abu-dhabi-media-free-zone/)); range quoted AED 7,500–15,000/yr ([uaeinform](https://uaeinform.com/visa-guide/freelance-permit)) | Covers 60+ media fields incl. marketing, PR, creative design. **The natural fit for Sara's activity** |
| twofour54 design & creative services company licence | from AED 15,000 ([sijil](https://sijil.ae/en/compare/twofour54/design-license-cost-dubai)) | Full company, not freelance |
| Full freelance setup with visa | from AED 9,999 ([emirabiz](https://emirabiz.com/uae-freelance-permit/)) | Irrelevant here — she already has residency |

**Good news:** twofour54 is in Abu Dhabi, covers exactly her activity, and residency is
optional — so she can hold a permit while remaining on WeDo's visa. Roughly **AED 3,500–7,500/
year** *(estimate from the range above)*.

### Can she hold it on another employer's visa? Technically yes. Practically, this is the trap.

**Search-verified:** UAE law permits employees to own companies and hold licences, and
free zones increasingly waive the NOC — Meydan Free Zone is explicitly reported as having
"eliminated the NOC requirement, allowing individuals on an employment visa to establish their
own company without employer permission."
([Meydan FZ](https://www.meydanfz.ae/blog/can-you-start-a-business-while-still-being-employed-in-dubai),
[Virtuzone](https://virtuzone.com/blog/noc-meaning/), [tdabeer](https://tdabeer.ae/en/trade-license-while-employed-dubai-2025/))
Mainland setups generally still require an NOC. *twofour54's specific NOC policy is
**unverified** — it must be asked directly.*

**But the free zone waiving the NOC does not waive her contract.** These are two different
obligations and the setup agents selling licences have no reason to mention the second one.
A free zone saying "we don't need your employer's permission" means only that *the free zone*
does not need it. If her contract prohibits outside work, she is in breach the moment the
licence issues — regardless of what the free zone asked for.

**And the NOC route has its own cost, which is not money.** Asking WeDo for an NOC is a
disclosure event. She would be formally telling the employer who monitors her LinkedIn that
she is building something outside the agency, in the agency's own commercial field, six months
before she needs to be maximally unremarkable. See §1.6.

## 1.3 Registering an association — effectively closed to them

**Search-verified, and this is decisive.**

Non-profit associations are governed by Federal Law No. 2 of 2008 on Public Welfare
Associations, with establishment now handled by the **Ministry of Community Empowerment**
(formerly Community Development). The founder requirements:

| Requirement | Source |
|---|---|
| Minimum **7** founding members (MoCE application route) | [MoCE](https://www.moce.gov.ae/en/w/establish-an-npo) |
| Minimum **10** founders, **at least 2 UAE nationals** (Law 2/2008) | [ICNL](https://www.icnl.org/research/library/united-arab-emirates_2_uae_fedlaw2_2008/) |
| **At least 70% of founders must be UAE nationals** — non-nationals capped at 30% | [MoCE](https://www.moce.gov.ae/en/w/establish-an-npo), [ICNL](https://www.icnl.org/wp-content/uploads/UAE-Philanthropy-Law-Report-May-2023.pdf) |
| Founders must be of legal age, good conduct, no relevant convictions | [ICNL](https://www.icnl.org/research/library/united-arab-emirates_2_uae_fedlaw2_2008/) |

Run the arithmetic against the actual team. Sara, Hadeer, Ahmed and Yomni are four
non-nationals. At a 30% cap, four expatriate founders require **at least nine or ten Emirati
co-founders** — and those Emiratis would be the legal majority of an organisation for African
women, with the founding control that implies.

**That is not a paperwork obstacle. It is a different organisation.** Recruiting nine Emirati
nationals willing to be named founders of a diaspora community group, before the group has
held a single event, is not a realistic Year-1 activity. Abu Dhabi additionally licenses NGOs
at emirate level through the Department of Community Development
([ADDCD](https://www.addcd.gov.ae/en/Licensing-of-Non-Governmental-Organization)) — *scope and
requirements unverified.*

**Conclusion: "register Afrilink as a UAE association or NPO" should be struck from the plan.**
Not deferred — struck. If the founders want a registered legal entity in year 1, the only
realistic path is a commercial licence (§1.2), which makes it a business, not an association.

*One genuine opportunity noted in passing:* MoCE launched an **AED 100 million NPO empowerment
fund** with grants up to AED 5 million, first window closing 31 May 2026 — now passed
([Zawya](https://www.zawya.com/en/press-release/government-news/the-ministry-of-community-empowerment-launches-aed100-million-npo-empowerment-fund-gn9uicr0),
[The National](https://www.thenationalnews.com/news/uae/2026/04/16/uae-launches-dh100million-fund-to-boost-non-profit-sector/)).
Irrelevant now — they could not have qualified without being a registered NPO first — but it
shows the state actively funds this sector *through the registered structure they cannot enter*.

## 1.4 Does cost-covering ticketing count? Yes. There is no threshold.

**Search-verified, and this is the finding that surprises most people.**

The Abu Dhabi Department of Culture and Tourism runs an Events Licensing System. Two rules
from it answer the question directly:

1. **Organisers must use the official ticketing system for "all types of paid or non-paid
   tickets."** The rule is written to cover free tickets explicitly.
2. **DCT charges 10% of total ticket sales.**
   ([DCT](https://dct.gov.ae/en/e.services/events.licensing.system.aspx),
   [Khaleej Times](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/life-and-living/abu-dhabi-how-to-get-an-event-licence-conditions-process-explained))

Dubai is structurally the same: DET requires event organisers selling tickets to integrate
with DET's ticketing system, tickets may only be sold through DET-integrated platforms
(Platinumlist, Virgin Tickets, BookMyShow and similar), and **you must not sell tickets or
promote the event publicly before the e-Permit is approved.**
([Dubai DET](https://www.dubaidet.gov.ae/en/licences-and-permits/events-and-activations),
[ticmint](https://ticmint.com/blog/event-permit-dubai/how-to-get-an-event-permit-uae-in-2025-step-by-step-guide/))

**So the brief's central financial assumption is wrong.** "Tickets that only cover costs, not
intended to profit" is not a lighter category. The regulator's test is not your margin — it is
whether you are publicly organising and ticketing an event. Three consequences:

- The 10% AD levy applies to gross ticket sales, not profit. On cost-recovery pricing that
  levy comes straight out of the founders' pockets.
- Tickets cannot be sold through WhatsApp, Instagram DMs or a bank transfer. They go through
  an integrated platform, which takes its own cut and creates a paper trail in a name.
- **Publicity is gated.** No promoting the event before the permit. The Phase-1/Phase-2 launch
  plan in the brief — announce on Instagram and LinkedIn, then ticket — runs in the wrong
  order.

*Per-event permit fees were **unverified** — DCT/DET fee schedules were unreachable. This is
a known gap and it materially affects the model in `04-financial-model.md`.*

**Where the line actually falls:** the regulated object is a *public, promoted, ticketed
event*. A genuinely private, invitation-only gathering where no ticket exists and no money is
collected by the organisers is a different thing. That distinction is the basis of the legal
structure in §1.7 — and it is a line worth having a lawyer draw precisely, because "private
gathering" and "unlicensed public event" are separated by how you promote it.

## 1.5 The pillar nobody costed: training is separately regulated

Pillar 3 (L&D) and pillar 4 (youth) are **more** regulated than the consulting, not less.

Dubai requires an Educational Services Permit from **KHDA** to deliver training — and
reporting is explicit that "you cannot legally advertise a course, enroll a student, or issue
a recognised certificate until KHDA has approved your institute."
([best-solution](https://www.best-solution.ae/business-setup/training-institute-license-dubai/),
[claemirates](https://www.claemirates.com/establishing-a-training-institute-in-dubai/))
Abu Dhabi's counterpart authority is **ADEK**, with ACTVET historically covering vocational
training. ([ProPartner](https://www.propartnergroup.com/blog/2023/09/khda-and-actvet-approvals-in-the-uae/))
*Emirate-specific requirements and costs for a small training provider are **unverified**.*

One reported carve-out worth chasing: **closed B2B corporate training delivered under contract
to a company for its own employees** is described as a different case from running public
courses. ([ProPartner](https://www.propartnergroup.com/blog/2023/09/khda-and-actvet-approvals-in-the-uae/))
*Single-source and load-bearing — if true it is the cleanest paid route in the entire venture,
because it needs a trade licence but plausibly not an education permit. **Verify this
specifically with the lawyer.***

Note what that implies: the free public "launch-phase workshops" listed in the brief — LinkedIn
basics, CV writing, public speaking, ten of them — are *advertised courses*. Free does not
exempt them from the advertising restriction any more than free tickets exempt an event.

## 1.6 The confidentiality trade-off — flagged as required

Per `CLAUDE.md` and the `stealth-positioning` skill, this must be stated rather than buried.

**A public launch is visible to her employer.** Nothing about Afrilink mentions relocation, so
it does not breach the letter of the confidentiality rule. It breaches the spirit in a way
that is arguably worse:

1. **It draws exactly the attention she cannot afford.** The premise of her LinkedIn strategy
   is that her boss reads her feed and sees ordinary professional pride. A founder
   announcement is not ordinary. It makes her *interesting* to the person monitoring her, for
   six months, starting now.
2. **It is a plausible contract breach in her employer's own field** (§1.1). This is not "she
   has a hobby." It is "our Creative Director is publicly offering brand and marketing
   consultation." A boss who monitors LinkedIn will see it, and it hands him a legitimate,
   documentable grievance that has nothing to do with relocation.
3. **The NOC route means telling him directly** — in writing, in advance.
4. **A licence is a public record**, searchable by name.
5. **It burns the cover story.** Her attestation chase, her IELTS leave day, her CIM study
   time — each currently has a dull, true explanation. Once she is visibly building an
   organisation, unexplained absences and distraction acquire a *second* plausible reading,
   and her boss is already looking.

There is no stealth version of founding a public community. The whole point is publicity.
**This venture and the confidentiality strategy are in direct conflict, and the conflict
cannot be designed away — only accepted or deferred.**

The one structure that resolves it: **Sara is not the public founder.** See §1.7, Option B.

## 1.7 What structure WOULD be legal

Four options, ranked by risk-adjusted sense. **Hadeer's visa status is the single most
important unknown** and changes the ranking.

### Option A — Private, unticketed, no money. *(Legal today, needs nothing)*
Invitation-only gatherings. No public ticketing, no fee collected by the organisers, attendees
pay the café directly for what they consume. A WhatsApp community and a LinkedIn presence that
promotes *ideas*, not ticketed events. No licence, no permit, no NOC, no registration.

- ✅ Legal now, zero cost, zero visa risk, tests demand honestly (see `05-concept-and-market.md`)
- ❌ No revenue at all. Not an organisation. Caps at roughly 30 people before it looks public

**This is the correct Year-1 structure and it is the recommendation.**

### Option B — Hadeer fronts it. *(Best structure if her status allows)*
Hadeer holds the licence and is the named, public founder. Sara contributes strategy, brand and
network privately, and is named publicly only once she has left WeDo.

Whether this works depends entirely on Hadeer's visa:

| Hadeer's status | Viability |
|---|---|
| **Spouse / family visa** | **Strongest case.** Reporting indicates family-visa holders can apply for a work permit directly, without an employer NOC ([uaevisaonline](https://uaevisaonline.com/articles/uae-part-time-work-permit)) — *single-source, verify.* No sponsor to ask, no contract to breach |
| Employer-sponsored, like Sara | Same problem as Sara, moved sideways. Her contract and NOC position must be checked identically |
| Golden visa / self-sponsored | Clean. Can licence and trade freely |

**Action: establish Hadeer's exact visa status before any other decision.** It determines
whether the venture has a legal front person at all. Sara cannot answer this — Hadeer must,
and it should be asked directly and soon.

### Option C — Commercial licence in Sara's name. *(Possible, and the worst option)*
twofour54 freelance permit, ~AED 3,500–7,500/yr, plus event permits per event, plus KHDA/ADEK
if training is public.

- ✅ Fully legitimate trading, invoices, corporate clients
- ❌ Requires resolving the contract question, plausibly requires an NOC (a disclosure event),
  puts her name on a public record in her employer's own field, and stakes the visa that the
  entire EU plan runs on — for revenue the model in `04-financial-model.md` says is near zero
  in year 1

**The cost-benefit does not work while she is at WeDo and pre-departure. Not recommended
before spring 2027.**

### Option D — Operate under an existing licensed host. *(Underrated, worth a week's effort)*
Run the programme as a series inside an already-licensed body: an existing women's network,
a coworking operator, a chamber of commerce, or an African embassy's cultural programme. The
host holds the licence and the permits; Afrilink supplies the programming and the brand.

- ✅ Legal cover, an existing audience, credibility by association, near-zero cost
- ❌ Shared control, and Afrilink is a programme rather than an institution at first

Embassies are the strongest lead — cultural and diaspora programming is core business for
them, and the brief already names them as a partner. *Unverified whether embassy-hosted events
are exempt from DCT/DET permits; likely handled by the embassy. Ask.*
