## 7.1 ⚠️ The confidentiality problem is now WORSE, not better

This is the headline, and under `CLAUDE.md` it is non-negotiable that it be stated rather than
buried.

**Belgian ASBL registration publishes the founders' and directors' names, professions and home
addresses in the Moniteur Belge / Belgisch Staatsblad** — a public, permanent, indexable
official gazette.
([hub.brussels](https://info.hub.brussels/en/guide/start-business-formalities/non-profit-association-npo),
[European Activism Incubator](https://activismincubator.eu/set-up-non-profit-in-belgium/))
Associations also register in the Crossroads Bank for Enterprises (KBO/BCE). The Dutch
equivalent — board members of a stichting in the KVK register — is likewise public
([KVK](https://www.kvk.nl/en/rules-and-laws/board-member-of-a-foundation-rules-and-duties/)).

Read what that actually publishes about her:

> **Name: Sara Radwan. Profession: Creative Director. Domicile: Abu Dhabi, UAE. Role: founder
> of a Belgian non-profit for women founders in the European Union.**

Compare that to the things `stealth-positioning` bans — an EU city in her location field, "open
to opportunities", any statement about working abroad. **A public EU register entry is a
stronger relocation signal than any of them.** It is not an inference from tone. It is a
government record, in her name, stating that she is building an organisation in Europe while
living in Abu Dhabi, and it cannot be deleted or made private later.

The original UAE version at least had an innocent reading — a local community group. **This
version does not.** There is no story in which founding a Belgian or Dutch entity targeting EU
women founders is unrelated to moving to Europe.

**This single fact should determine the sequencing of the whole plan.**

### The fix, and it costs nothing

**Do not register the entity while she is still at WeDo.** Three options, best first:

1. **Register after relocation.** She becomes the founder once she is in the EU — at which
   point she may also satisfy Belgium's residency requirement herself (§7.2). Nothing is lost:
   a community, a mailing list, an Instagram presence and a first gathering need **no legal
   entity at all**. Register when there is something to register.
2. **Hadeer or an EU-resident co-founder registers it**, and Sara joins the board after her
   notice is served. Same structure recommended three times over in the earlier analysis, now
   for a fourth reason.
3. **Register now with Sara named** — and accept that her employer can find it. Only if she
   judges the risk acceptable. It is her call; it should be a decision, not an accident.

**Recommendation: option 1.** Build everything now. Register nothing yet. The entity is the
last step, not the first.

## 7.2 Which country — and Belgium has a catch

Both work for a non-resident founder. They differ in cost and in one requirement that matters.

| | **Belgium (ASBL/VZW)** | **Netherlands (Stichting)** |
|---|---|---|
| Minimum founders | **2** | 1 board member |
| Notary required? | **No** — private deed | **Yes** — notarial deed |
| Non-resident founders allowed? | Yes | Yes — foreign founders and board members, subject to KYC |
| **Residency catch** | ⚠️ **"A standard ASBL must have at least two members residing in Belgium"** — *single-source, verify* | No resident-director requirement found |
| Registered address in-country | Required | Required |
| Language | FR/NL statutes | **Articles must be in Dutch** |
| Official setup cost | **€266–€340** (€111.80 register + €154.70 gazette, ex VAT) | **€575–€1,575** (notary €500–1,500 + KVK €85.15) |
| With professional help | ~€1,548 ex VAT all-in | Higher |
| Remote formation | Yes | Possible by power of attorney, notary-dependent |

Sources: [AAM-Solutions ASBL](https://www.aam-solutions.com/en/company-formation/asbl/),
[Activism Incubator](https://activismincubator.eu/set-up-non-profit-in-belgium/),
[PwC Legal Belgium](https://www.pwclegal.be/en/news/legal-structures-for-notforprofits-in-belgium--choosing-the-righ.html),
[KVK registration fee](https://www.kvk.nl/en/registration/registration-fee/),
[Netherbridge](https://netherbridge.nl/dutch-private-foundation-stichting/),
[DutchTaxGuide](https://www.dutchtaxguide.com/en/self-employment/starting-a-business/stichting).
*Search-verified; primary registry sources were not reachable (see `01-legal-uae.md` research
note).*

**The Belgian two-resident-members rule is the deciding factor and it is single-source —
verify it before choosing.** If true, Belgium is unavailable to two UAE-based founders until
they recruit two Belgium-resident members, and the Netherlands becomes the default despite
costing more.

**One option worth asking a Belgian lawyer about: the AISBL/IVZW** — the *international*
non-profit form, described as "well suited to cross-border federations and networks"
([FPA handbook](https://fpa.freecad.org/handbook/corporate/AISBL/rationale.html)). That is
precisely what this is. It is a heavier process (royal-decree approval) but may fit the
cross-border purpose better. *Unverified — worth one question, not a research project.*

**Note both require an in-country registered address.** Neither is a paper exercise done
entirely from Abu Dhabi; there is a real address to arrange. Virtual-office providers serve
this market, at ongoing cost. **[EST €300–800/yr, unverified.]**

## 7.3 🆕 GDPR — the new obligation, and it is unavoidable

"Database collection" of women in the EU is regulated processing of personal data. This is the
one genuinely new legal exposure the restructure creates, and it replaces UAE licensing as the
compliance item to design around.

**There is no non-profit exemption.** Organisations outside the EU/EEA must comply where they
process personal data of EU/EEA residents
([Mandatly](https://mandatly.com/knowledge-base/does-gdpr-apply-to-non-profit-organizations),
[DataGuard](https://www.dataguard.com/blog/gdpr-for-small-clubs-and-societies)). As an
EU-registered entity processing EU residents' data it applies twice over.

What it actually requires at this scale — manageable, but not optional:

| Obligation | Practical form |
|---|---|
| **Lawful basis** | Membership data can rest on *contract* or *legitimate interests*; **marketing generally needs consent** ([White Fuse](https://whitefuse.com/blog/gdpr-guide-for-associations-societies-and-membership-organisations/)) |
| **Privacy notice** | Plain-language, on the site, at the point of signup |
| **Data minimisation** | Collect only what the purpose needs. Resist "useful later" fields |
| **Data subject rights** | Access, correction, deletion — with someone able to action them |
| **Consent records** | Who consented, to what, when |
| **Processing from outside the EU** | Sara processing EU members' data from Abu Dhabi raises international-transfer questions — *unverified, ask* |

There is a **helpful provision for non-profits**: processing by a non-profit body relating
*solely to its members or former members*, with no disclosure to third parties without consent,
is treated more permissively ([Mandatly](https://mandatly.com/knowledge-base/does-gdpr-apply-to-non-profit-organizations)).

**But note how directly that cuts against the freelancer model.** Passing member details to
freelance consultants *is* disclosure to third parties. **The database and the freelancer
referral channel are in tension**, and the resolution is design, not paperwork: either members
opt in explicitly to being contacted about services, or freelancers are surfaced *to* members
rather than members being passed *to* freelancers. Decide that before the first record is
collected — retrofitting consent across an existing list is painful and often means starting
the list again.

**Cost: near zero if designed in from day one.** A privacy notice, a consent checkbox, a
deletion process. **The cheapest moment is before the first signup form goes live.**

## 7.4 What still stands from the UAE analysis

Three things survive the restructure. All are smaller than before.

**1. Her employment contract still applies.** Even an unpaid directorship of a foreign
non-profit can breach an outside-activities or conflict-of-interest clause. The risk is lower
than trading commercially — no UAE licence, no UAE income, no competing client work — but it is
not zero, and it is compounded by §7.1: the Moniteur Belge publishes her *profession*. **Reading
the contract remains the first action.** It is still 20 minutes.

**2. "Marketing and branding consultation" still overlaps WeDo's business** — but much less
sharply. If freelancers deliver and bill clients directly, and the association merely connects
people, Sara is not personally selling competing services. **Keep it that way while she is
employed:** she should not be a named consultant in the directory until she has left.

**3. Founding an EU non-profit gives her no residence rights.** Belgian corporate law lets
non-EU citizens direct companies with no residency restriction, but "simply founding or
directing an ASBL does not automatically grant residence rights" — the self-employed route runs
through a **company plus a professional card plus a D visa**, not a non-profit
([LawSupport](https://lawsupport.be/register-company-as-foreigner-belgium/),
[consiliojus](https://consiliojus.com/belgium-residence-permit/)).

**Flagging this because it is exactly the kind of thing that looks like a shortcut and is not.**
The entity strengthens a scholarship *application*; it is not itself an immigration route, and
it does not touch the eligibility filter's Route A or Route B. Nothing in
`01-relocation/eligibility-filter.md` changes.

## 7.5 The relocation case is genuinely stronger now

`03-relocation-interaction.md` judged the UAE version a **weak 2027 asset**. The restructure
improves that, for reasons that are structural rather than presentational:

- **The organisation is now in her destination**, not the place she is leaving. The awkward
  question — *you founded this in Abu Dhabi and you are leaving?* — disappears. She is not
  abandoning a community; she is moving toward one.
- **The handover problem largely dissolves.** No UAE members are left behind.
- **It is coherent with the application.** "I run a European network for women founders and I
  want to study in Europe" needs no explaining.
- **Online-first means it survives the move intact.** A UAE events organisation could not
  relocate. A network and a database can.
- **The unpaid arrival gathering is clean** — no permits, no ticketing, no licence.

**Two cautions, unchanged from the earlier analysis:**

1. **Do not register it *for* the application.** A brand-new entity registered weeks before a
   deadline reads as contrived; panels are calibrated for it. An organisation with a year of
   real online activity behind it and an entity registered later reads as genuine. **Activity
   first, entity second** — which is also the confidentiality answer in §7.1. They agree.
2. **She still already clears the leadership bar.** 22 years, Social Dar, the €500k programme.
   This adds narrative coherence, not qualification. Do not spend IELTS-preparation weeks on it —
   `02-calendar-collision.md`'s core finding stands.

**But the online-first version does now fit the calendar.** No monthly production deadline, no
venue sourcing, no permits. **[EST 4–8 hours/month]** — against 30–40 for the UAE version. That
is the difference between a plan that displaces the IELTS and one that does not.

**So: start it this year, as she wants. Just do not register it this year.**

## 7.6 Naming, revisited

**The Arabic objection is withdrawn.** She is right — she is not targeting an Arabic-speaking
audience, and a brand should be judged in the market it serves.

**And a correction worth making in her favour:** I criticised "Afrika" as a misspelling of the
Arabic إفريقيا. In the new target market that reverses — **"Afrika" is the correct Dutch and
German spelling of Africa.** In Amsterdam, Rotterdam, Brussels, Antwerp and Berlin it reads
native, not misspelt. For an EU-facing brand registered in Belgium or the Netherlands, that
spelling is an asset.

**What still stands:**

| Issue | Status |
|---|---|
| "She Afrika" vs the no-gender-lock decision | ⚠️ **Still a contradiction**, but weaker — the stated audience is now "women founders and business owners… we welcome everyone else". If the audience is deliberately women-led, gender-locking may now be a *choice* rather than a mistake. **Make it explicitly, or drop the "welcome everyone" clause** — the two do not sit together |
| **"Afrilink" trademark conflicts** | 🔴 **Now more serious, not less.** Four existing users, one a non-profit in African enterprise development. With the EU as the actual market, **EUIPO clearance moves from optional to essential** |
| `.org` availability | ⚠️ **Still unverified** — DNS blocked here. `afrilinkconsultants.com` and `afrilinkselect.com` exist, so the namespace is populated |

**Recommendation unchanged in substance, changed in emphasis:** "She Afrika" is now the more
*viable* of the two — better spelling fit for the EU market, no trademark collisions found —
provided the founders consciously accept the gendered positioning. "Afrilink" has a real
clearance problem in exactly the market they are entering.

**Run the EUIPO search before either name goes on anything.** It is free and takes minutes, and
it is the difference between choosing a name and inheriting a dispute.

## Revised recommendation

**Do it. Start this year. Register later.**

### Now → relocation
1. **Read the WeDo contract.** Unchanged, still first, still 20 minutes.
2. **Build the community online** — LinkedIn, Instagram, a mailing list. No entity needed.
3. **Design GDPR in from the first signup form** — privacy notice, consent checkbox, deletion
   process. Cheapest now, painful later.
4. **Run the EUIPO and domain checks** before committing to a name.
5. **Do not register the entity.** Do not put her name on a public EU register while employed
   at WeDo (§7.1).
6. **Verify the Belgian two-resident-members rule** — it decides Belgium vs Netherlands.

### On arrival in the EU
7. **Hold the free gathering.** Clean, no permits, and the momentum she wants.
8. **Register the entity** — Netherlands if the Belgian residency rule bites, Belgium if not or
   once she has two Belgian-resident members. €266–€1,575 depending on route.
9. **Add the freelancer layer** once there are members to serve, structured so freelancers bill
   clients directly.

### What has not changed
- **One pillar, not five.** Networking and the database. Everything else follows from members
  existing.
- **Demand is still unproven.** Online makes it cheaper to test, not less necessary. A mailing
  list that reaches 200 EU-based women founders in six months is the signal.
- **Youth programme stays cut** for year 1.
