# 8. Business Model Canvas

Built on the **revised model** (`07-restructure-eu-entity.md`): EU non-profit, online-first,
women founders in the EU, services delivered by freelancers. Not the original UAE brief.

Osterwalder nine-block canvas. Every entry carries the state it is actually in — **confirmed**,
**assumed**, or **blocked** — because a canvas of aspirations is a wish list, and this one is
meant to be used.

---

## What the canvas exposes

Fill in all nine blocks and one structural fact becomes hard to avoid:

> **This is a two-sided network whose only asset is the member database, and whose only revenue
> is a take-rate on other people's labour.**

Afrilink does not deliver the services. Freelancers do. Afrilink does not employ them, train
them or guarantee them. So the business — when it becomes one — is a **matchmaking margin on a
small base**. That is a real model (it is how most professional networks monetise), but it has
three properties the founders should name out loud now:

1. **It cannot earn anything until the member side has scale.** Both sides must exist before
   either is worth money. Classic cold-start problem.
2. **The take-rate is the whole business.** If freelancers and members can find each other once
   and then transact off-platform, the revenue disappears. Every network with this model faces
   it. Afrilink has no lock-in and, as a non-profit, should not want much.
3. **The asset and the liability are the same object.** The member database is the thing of
   value *and* the GDPR exposure (§7.3). It cannot be treated casually in either direction.

None of that is fatal. It does mean **the honest label is "network non-profit", not "business"**
— consistent with `04-financial-model.md`'s conclusion, now arrived at from the model's own
structure rather than from its cost lines.

## The canvas

### ◾ Customer Segments

Two-sided, plus funders. Naming them separately matters — they want different things.

| Segment | Detail | State |
|---|---|---|
| **Women founders & business owners in the EU** | Mainly African-origin, open to all. Early-stage through established. The core | **Assumed** — size unquantified |
| Aspiring founders | Employed women planning to start. Feeds the core over time | Assumed |
| **Freelance consultants & trainers** | The supply side. Branding, marketing, L&D. **They are the service capacity** | Assumed |
| Sponsors & funders | Diaspora-facing banks and remittance operators, telecoms, corporate DEI/ESG budgets, EU grant bodies | Assumed |

**Not** African women in the UAE. That was the original brief and it is superseded.

⚠️ **The single largest unknown in the whole plan: how many EU-based African-origin women
founders are actually reachable?** No verified figure. Everything downstream scales off it.

### ◾ Value Propositions

**To members**
- A peer network of women founders that does not exist in structured form — the gap identified
  in `05-concept-and-market.md`, relocated to the EU
- Founder-to-founder, not corporate mentorship. Different register, different trust
- Cultural affinity **without being limited by it** — African-rooted, open to all
- Practical business support at a price point solo founders can reach
- Visibility inside a curated directory

**To freelancers**
- Qualified lead flow from a defined niche, with no marketing spend
- Credibility by association

**To sponsors**
- Access to a genuinely hard-to-reach, underserved founder segment
- A substantive ESG/DEI story rather than a logo placement

⚠️ **All of it unproven.** No waitlist, no survey, no test event. `05-concept-and-market.md`
§5.3 sets the demand test; the EU version makes it cheaper, not less necessary.

🔴 **The LinkedIn constraint is structural, not a detail.** Her strongest channel is the one her
employer monitors. Until she leaves WeDo, Afrilink content on her personal LinkedIn is
restricted (`07-restructure-eu-entity.md` §7.1). **The venture's best distribution channel is
unavailable to its best distributor.** Plan around it: a separate Afrilink page, a co-founder's
profile, or accept slower growth until relocation.

### ◾ Customer Relationships

- **Community and co-creation** — the core mode. Members make it valuable to each other
- **High-touch, deliberately unscalable** for the first 10–15 — founders personally involved.
  This is how ambassadors are made, and it does not scale on purpose
- **Self-service** later: directory, resource library, freelancer listings
- **Curation, not brokerage** — Afrilink vouches for quality, it does not manage delivery

⚠️ **Where the GDPR tension bites.** Passing member details to freelancers is third-party
disclosure. Design the relationship so freelancers are surfaced **to** members, not members
passed **to** freelancers (§7.3). This is a customer-relationship decision before it is a
compliance one.

### ◾ Revenue Streams

| Stream | Mechanism | Realistic start |
|---|---|---|
| **Year 1** | **None. Founder-funded** | — |
| Referral / commission | Take-rate on freelancer engagements | Year 2, post-registration |
| Brokered corporate training | AED/EUR equivalent of the AED 15k–30k/day market | Year 2 |

⚠️ **Commission is economic activity.** A Belgian ASBL or Dutch stichting *may* carry out
economic activity where profits serve the purpose and are not distributed — but there are tax
and VAT consequences. **Confirm before building the model on it.** *Unverified.*

## Reading the canvas

**Strongest blocks:** Key Resources and Key Activities. The founders genuinely have the
capability, and the activities are things they can actually do.

**Weakest blocks:** Customer Segments and Value Propositions — both rest on an unquantified
audience and unproven demand. **In a canvas, weakness on the right-hand side is the serious
kind.** Infrastructure problems are solvable with effort; a value proposition nobody wants is
not.

**The block that decides the rest:** Key Partnerships. Two Belgium-resident members is a
gating requirement, not a nice-to-have, and it determines the country, the cost and the
timeline.

### Three questions the canvas asks and cannot answer

1. **How many EU-based African-origin women founders are reachable?** Everything scales off it.
2. **Will they pay — for anything?** Free community, paid services. Untested.
3. **Will freelancers accept a take-rate** from a new non-profit with no lead flow yet?

**All three are answerable cheaply, before any entity exists.** A landing page, a signup form
and 90 days of posting would answer the first two. Ten conversations would answer the third.

**That is the actual next step** — not registration, not naming, not the canvas. The canvas
is now good enough to show where the uncertainty is; the uncertainty is on the demand side, and
it is cheap to reduce.
