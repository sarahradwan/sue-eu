# 8. Business Model Canvas

Built on the **revised model** (`07-restructure-eu-entity.md`): EU non-profit, online-first,
women founders in the EU, services delivered by freelancers.

**Updated 27 July 2026** to absorb `09-membership-model.md` (free gated membership) and
`10-member-benefits-content-engine.md` (the visibility engine, Ahmed confirmed on production).

Every entry carries the state it is actually in — **confirmed**, **assumed** or **blocked** —
because a canvas of aspirations is a wish list.

---

## What the canvas exposes

> **This is a two-sided network whose only asset is the member database, and whose only revenue
> is a take-rate on other people's labour.**

Afrilink does not deliver the services. Freelancers do. So the business — when it becomes one —
is a **matchmaking margin on a small base**. Three properties follow:

1. **It cannot earn anything until the member side has scale.** Both sides must exist before
   either is worth money. Classic cold-start.
2. **The take-rate is the whole business, and there is no lock-in.** If a member and a
   freelancer meet once and transact off-platform thereafter, the revenue disappears.
3. **The asset and the liability are the same object.** The member database is the thing of
   value *and* the GDPR exposure.

**Since the last version, one thing has changed for the better.** The membership gate
(`09-membership-model.md`) converts a mailing list into a **verified directory**, which is the
difference between an asset and a spreadsheet. And the visibility engine
(`10-member-benefits-content-engine.md`) gives the network a benefit that only works because
other members exist. Both strengthen the right-hand side of the canvas, which was the weak half.

**The honest label is still "network non-profit", not business.**

### ◾ Value Propositions

**Now concrete rather than aspirational — this is the block that improved most.**

**To members**
- **Visibility** — a produced spotlight (bi-weekly), a filmed interview (monthly), assets they
  can reuse on their own channels. *The benefit founders actually want: customers and credibility*
- **Ask-the-network** — post a real problem, get answers from other founders
- **The directory** — searchable peers by sector, country, stage
- **One free consultation slot monthly**, delivered by a network freelancer
- Acceptance itself as a signal — free, but selective

**To freelancers**
- Qualified lead flow in a defined niche, no marketing spend
- The monthly free consultation as a live lead and visible credibility

**To sponsors**
- A **verified, opted-in** directory of EU women founders — materially more sellable than a
  large unverified list
- A substantive ESG story rather than a logo placement

⚠️ **Still unproven.** No applications received yet. But "apply to join" is a far better demand
test than "subscribe" — an application is intent; a subscribe is a reflex.

### ◾ Customer Relationships

- **Gated application, batched intake rounds** — monthly or quarterly, not rolling. Protects
  founder time and adds scarcity
- **Verification, not judgment** — "are you a founder?", not "is your business good enough?"
- **Visibility as the retention mechanism** — being featured is why members stay engaged
- **High-touch, deliberately unscalable** for the first 10–15 — how ambassadors are made
- **Curation, not brokerage** — Afrilink vouches; it does not manage delivery

⚠️ **Three distinct GDPR consents** now needed at signup: directory visibility, newsletter,
and **likeness** (for spotlights and interviews). One form, three opt-ins, recorded separately.

keeps Sara clear of her WeDo non-compete.

## Reading the canvas

**Strongest:** Key Resources and Key Activities — real capability, and now real production
capacity with Ahmed confirmed.

**Most improved:** Value Propositions. Concrete, differentiated, and built on benefits that only
work because other members exist. This was the weak half; it is no longer the weakest.

**Now the weakest:** Customer Segments. Everything else has firmed up around an audience whose
size is still unknown, and the gate narrows it further.

**Still decides the rest:** Key Partnerships. Two Belgium-resident members is a gating
requirement that determines country, cost and timeline.

### Three questions the canvas asks and cannot answer

1. **How many EU-based African-origin women founders are reachable?** Everything scales off it.
2. **Will they apply?** The gate makes this cheap to test and more meaningful than a subscribe.
3. **Will freelancers accept a take-rate** from a new non-profit with no lead flow yet?

**All three are answerable before any entity exists** — a landing page, an application form and
ninety days. That is the roadmap's Phase 1, and it is deliberately the only thing that happens
during the application window. See `11-roadmap.md`.
