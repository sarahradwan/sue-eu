## What a monthly event actually costs

The brief treats "one quality event monthly" as the minimum viable commitment. It is not a
small commitment. Estimated hours *(my estimates, from the work a 25–40 person event requires
— not sourced)*:

| Task | Event 1 | Steady state |
|---|---|---|
| Venue sourcing, negotiation, site visit | 6–10h | 2h |
| Permit / licensing admin (per `01-legal-uae.md`) | 4–8h | 3h |
| Brand, collateral, social assets | 8–12h | 3h |
| Programme design, speaker recruitment | 6–10h | 4h |
| Promotion, RSVPs, chasing, WhatsApp | 6–8h | 5h |
| Event day | 5h | 5h |
| Follow-up, content, photos, testimonials | 4h | 3h |
| **Total** | **~40–55h** | **~25h/month** |

Plus continuous community management — a WhatsApp group with 50 people is a daily obligation,
not a monthly one. Call it **3–5h/week ongoing**, which is not in the table above.

**Realistic load: 40–55 hours in month one, then 30–40 hours per month all-in.**

That is roughly **a full working week per month, indefinitely**, on top of a full-time senior
role, a postgraduate diploma, an English exam she has never sat, a consular chain that
requires physical chasing during business hours, and two LinkedIn posts a week.

## What it displaces

There is no spare capacity, so the question is not "does it fit" but "what gives". Ranked by
what actually gets sacrificed when a month gets tight:

**1. IELTS preparation — first to go, and the most expensive.**
Preparation is elastic; an event with 30 invited people is not. When they collide in September,
the event wins, because it has an audience and IELTS prep does not. A weak first sitting costs
a retake, and a second weak sitting eats into December. The `06-ielts` plan's entire value is
that sitting in late September buys **three** retake windows. Event preparation converts that
back into one.

**2. Application quality — the silent loss.**
Nov 2026–Jan 2027 is when motivation letters get written. A motivation letter written in a
spare hour is a rejected application, and nobody ever tells you that is why. This is the least
visible casualty and probably the largest.

**3. Attestation chasing.**
6–10 weeks of consular process requiring calls, visits and follow-ups **during business hours**
— the same scarce weekday-daytime capacity that venue visits and supplier meetings need. These
two compete for exactly the same hours, and only one has a hard deadline.

**4. CIM — already the designated casualty.**
Cannot absorb a second hit. If a Global Marketing Decisions exam sitting lands in Sept–Oct
2026, that is a fixed, unmovable date colliding with the IELTS sitting. Adding a monthly event
to that fortnight is not a scheduling problem; it is a failure.

**5. LinkedIn twice weekly.**
Ironically the one thing Afrilink would *feed* — but the content would be Afrilink content,
which `01-legal-uae.md` §1.6 explains is the content she cannot safely post.

## The two-founder objection, answered

The obvious counter: *Hadeer, Ahmed and Yomni carry the load.*

Partly true, and it is the strongest argument for proceeding. But:

- The brief assigns Sara **branding, marketing, strategy, community, partnerships, events,
  business support and media consultations** — six of the eight functional areas. She is not a
  co-founder in this plan; she is the operator.
- Founder-brand work is the least delegable part, and it is exactly her skillset. She will do
  it because she is best at it and because it is the fun part.
- Per `01-legal-uae.md`, if Hadeer is the licence holder then Hadeer is *also* carrying the
  regulatory load. The delegable half is smaller than it looks.

A version where Sara genuinely contributes 5–8 hours a month of strategy and design, and
touches nothing operational, does survive this calendar. **That version requires her not to be
the founder** — which is Option B in the legal note, and it is the same answer arriving from a
different direction.

## The verdict

| Version | Verdict |
|---|---|
| Monthly public events from Sept/Oct 2026, Sara operating | **Does not fit.** Displaces IELTS prep and application quality — the two things whose failure costs a full year |
| Monthly events from Sept 2026, Sara advising ≤8h/month, Hadeer operating | **Fits, conditionally.** Requires real discipline and Hadeer's genuine capacity and legal standing |
| Private, unticketed, quarterly gatherings through the window | **Fits comfortably.** ~8–10h per quarter. Builds the network, tests demand, no deadline pressure |
| Full launch from **March 2027** | **Fits.** Applications submitted, IELTS done, attestation done. Real capacity returns |

**Recommendation: do not launch a monthly cadence before February 2027.**

Between now and then, do the three things that cost almost nothing and preserve every option:

1. **One free, private coffee meetup** — say, late September, after the IELTS sitting. Half a
   day total. It is the demand test (`05-concept-and-market.md`), and it is the honest answer
   to whether anyone actually wants this.
2. **Build the WhatsApp community slowly.** No cadence, no promises, no deadlines.
3. **Let Hadeer build the operational spine** while Sara is heads-down — programme design, the
   training curriculum, the licensing question. If Hadeer can carry that alone for six months,
   that is the strongest possible evidence the venture survives Sara's departure in 2027. If
   she cannot, better to learn it now than in mid-2027 with a community depending on it.

Point 3 is worth dwelling on. The handover problem in `03-relocation-interaction.md` is
usually treated as an exit question. It is actually an entry test — and deferring the launch
lets them run it before anyone is relying on the answer.

## The honest framing

"It fits if you drop X" was the requested answer, so here it is without hedging:

> **It fits if you drop the September–January launch.** Nothing else in the window is
> droppable. IELTS has a deadline that costs a year. Attestation gates both routes. CIM has
> already been demoted once. The job pays for all of it.
>
> Afrilink is the only commitment on the list with **no external deadline at all** — the sole
> item whose timing is entirely the founders' choice. That is not a reason to treat it as
> unimportant. It is the reason it is the correct thing to move.
