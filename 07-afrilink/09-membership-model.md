# 9. Membership model — free but gated

Proposal (27 July 2026): **membership free, but limited to founders and business owners who
show a portfolio.** Benefit: a newsletter with updates, tips and news for women entrepreneurs
in the EU.

**Verdict: the gate is right. The newsletter is the wrong benefit.** Keep the first, replace
the second.

---

## 9.1 The gate — keep it

**Free-but-selective is a proven model, and it is the correct instinct here.** The scarcity is
qualification, not price.

[Dreamers & Doers](https://www.dreamersdoers.com/) — a curated, application-based community for
women entrepreneurs — "values quality over quantity and only admits a select number of new
members four times a year," and the curation is described as part of what makes it powerful
([Bloom](https://www.bloomlifestyleco.com/communities/dreamers-amp-doers-is-giving-women-founders-media-buzz-and-real-support),
[Hive Index](https://thehiveindex.com/communities/dreamers-and-doers/)). *Search-verified.*

Four reasons it works for Afrilink specifically:

1. **It fixes the free-membership problem.** Free usually means low commitment and low
   perceived value. Free *and hard to get into* inverts that — acceptance is itself a signal.
2. **It protects the actual value proposition.** The BMC's core promise is a **peer** network.
   Eighty verified founders is worth more than five hundred unverified subscribers, because the
   value is who else is in the room.
3. **It makes the database genuinely valuable.** `08-business-model-canvas.md` identified the
   member database as the only real asset. A *verified* list of EU-based women founders is a
   sponsorship and referral asset. An unverified mailing list is not. **Gating is what turns the
   list into the asset.**
4. **It gives the brand a defensible position** in a crowded market — see §9.2.

### But fix the word "portfolio"

**"Portfolio" is a creative-industry artifact and most business owners do not have one.** A
caterer, a logistics operator, a consultant, a shop owner, a clinic owner — none of them keeps a
portfolio. This is a Creative Director's native vocabulary leaking into a general-business
context, and it will silently filter the membership toward creative founders.

If the intended audience is founders across all sectors, ask for **proof of trading**, not a
portfolio:

- Business name and country
- Website **or** an active business social account
- One line: what the business does, and roughly how long it has been running
- Optionally: registration number, where the member is comfortable sharing it

### Verification, not judgment

**The gate should test "are you actually a founder?" — not "is your business good enough?"**

Judging quality creates bias exposure, slow decisions, awkward rejections and unbounded admin.
Verifying existence is fast, defensible and explains itself. It also keeps the promise honest:
the network is *for founders*, not *for successful founders*.

⚠️ **Cost of the gate: vetting is founder time**, the scarcest resource in the whole plan
(`02-calendar-collision.md`). At ten applications a week that is real work. Mitigate with a
structured form, a fixed weekly review slot, and — following Dreamers & Doers — **intake rounds
rather than rolling admission.** Batching is what makes it survivable alongside IELTS and the
application window.

### One thing the gate costs, and it should be a conscious trade

The BMC lists **aspiring founders** — employed women planning to start — as a segment that feeds
the core over time. **A founders-only gate removes the top of the funnel.**

Given the addressable pool is already unquantified and possibly small, that matters. Options:

- **Recommended:** one network, founders only, and let aspiring founders follow the public
  channels (LinkedIn, Instagram, the newsletter) without being members. The funnel still exists;
  it just sits outside the gate.
- Two tiers — Member (verified founder) and Subscriber (open). More surface, more admin, and
  it dilutes the signal.

## 9.2 The newsletter — the weak half

**A newsletter of "updates, tips and news for women entrepreneurs in the EU" is a commodity,
and it is the wrong shape for this venture.** Four problems:

**1. The market is well served.** [EU-Startups](https://www.eu-startups.com/) runs a weekly
newsletter on European startup news, events and funding; Sifted covers pan-European startup
trends; [Female Founders](https://www.eu-startups.com/investor/female-founders/) reaches "over
80k people across Europe"; the EU's own
[EISMEA](https://eismea.ec.europa.eu/support-women-entrepreneurs_en) publishes women-entrepreneur
support content. *Search-verified.* Afrilink would be entering a category with established
incumbents, with less time and no budget.

**2. It is a one-way broadcast, and the value proposition is a peer network.** A newsletter
creates an **audience**, not a community. Members who only receive a newsletter never meet each
other — which is the one thing they joined for.

**3. It does not improve with scale.** The 500th member gets exactly the same newsletter as the
5th. **Prefer benefits with network effects** — things that get better as more people join.
That is the whole reason to run a network rather than a publication.

**4. It is a recurring deadline.** `02-calendar-collision.md` rejected the monthly event
largely because a monthly commitment is a monthly deadline. **A weekly newsletter is a heavier
recurring obligation than a monthly event** — [EST] 4–8 hours a week to do well. That is the
single most calendar-hostile option on the table.

Note what the strongest comparator actually leads with. Dreamers & Doers' flagship benefit is
not a newsletter — it is the **"PR Hype Machine"**, connecting members to features in Forbes,
Fast Company, Marie Claire and Inc.
([Bloom](https://www.bloomlifestyleco.com/communities/dreamers-amp-doers-is-giving-women-founders-media-buzz-and-real-support)).
**Visibility, not information.**

## 9.3 What to offer instead

The test for any member benefit: **does it only work because other members exist, and does it
get better as more join?** A newsletter fails both. These pass.

### ⭐ Member visibility — the strongest option, and her unfair advantage

Founders want customers and credibility far more than they want tips. Making members *visible*
delivers both, and **Sara can produce this better than almost anyone** — 22 years of brand,
content and art direction is precisely the capability that turns a founder into a
well-presented case study.

- A monthly member spotlight: proper photography, real writing, a genuine story
- Members get assets they can reuse on their own channels
- Costs her craft time, not research time — and it is craft she already has

**This is the benefit no incumbent newsletter can copy**, because it requires knowing the
members and being able to art-direct. It is also the most portable to the scholarship narrative
and the portfolio.

### Ask-the-network

A channel where a member posts a real problem and gets answers from other founders. Highest
value-per-effort in any professional network, nearly free to run, and **it is the thing members
cannot get anywhere else.**

### The member directory

Searchable by sector, country, stage. The literal product of the gate — and the reason the
verification work pays off. Gets strictly better with every member.

### Warm introductions

Curated, deliberately unscalable, for the first 10–15. The BMC already identifies this as how
ambassadors are made.

### Then: the newsletter as a *wrapper*, not a product

Keep it — but change what it carries. Not curated external news. **Network activity:**

- Who joined this month (with links — visibility again)
- The month's member spotlight
- Open asks: "member needs a Dutch accountant", "member looking for a co-founder"
- Members' own wins and launches
- One genuinely useful resource, not ten

**Differentiated by definition, because nobody else has these members.** It also writes itself
from activity that is happening anyway, rather than requiring research.

**Cadence: monthly, not weekly.** Monthly is sustainable alongside the 2027 application window.
Weekly is not, and a lapsed weekly newsletter signals decline more loudly than a monthly one
ever could.

## 9.5 Recommendation

1. **Keep the gate.** Free, verified, founders and business owners only.
2. **Drop the word "portfolio."** Ask for proof of trading — business name, country, link, one line.
3. **Verify existence, do not judge quality.**
4. **Batch intake** — monthly or quarterly rounds, not rolling. Protects the calendar and adds
   scarcity.
5. **Lead with member visibility, not information.** It is the benefit founders actually want
   and the one Sara is uniquely equipped to deliver.
6. **Add ask-the-network and the directory.** Both get better with scale; a newsletter does not.
7. **Keep the newsletter, monthly, carrying network activity** rather than curated news.
8. **Two separate consents at signup** — directory visibility, and newsletter.

**The one-line version:** the gate is a genuinely good idea and worth keeping; the newsletter is
a media product bolted onto a network, and the benefits that actually retain founders are the
ones that only exist because other members do.
