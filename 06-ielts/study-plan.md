# Study plan

**This file is the calendar, not the syllabus.**

The dates below are fixed and can be acted on now — they follow from the course start, the
sitting window and the Swedish deadline, none of which depend on how good you are. The
*content* of each prep block is deliberately blank until D1 is marked, because that is the
whole point of running a diagnostic first.

Status: **Phase 0, day 1.** Nothing sat yet.

## Phase 0 — the next two weeks

The objective: **walk into 4 September with a complete, honest baseline across all five
sections.** Then the course is something you use selectively rather than sit through.

The whole battery is **under three hours of actual sitting time.** That is the argument for
doing it now rather than letting the course set the pace:

| | Time | Deadline |
|---|---|---|
| **D1 Writing Task 1** | 20 min | **this weekend** — everything else waits on it |
| D2 Writing Task 2 | 40 min | by 28 Aug |
| D3 Reading | 60 min | by 31 Aug |
| D4 Listening | ~40 min | by 3 Sept — *blocked on Cambridge IELTS 19 arriving* |
| D5 Speaking | ~15 min | by 3 Sept |
| | **≈ 2h 55m** | |

Running alongside, not dependent on any of it:

- [ ] **Book the test.** Academic, computer-delivered, Abu Dhabi, window 18 Sept – 1 Oct.
      Registration closes 4 days before the date; good slots go earlier. Personal email,
      personal card.
- [ ] **Order Cambridge IELTS 19** today. D4 is blocked until it lands and shipping is the
      long pole — this is the only item here with a supply-chain risk.
- [ ] Confirm the course's exact end date and how many writing scripts get marked.

### Sequencing rule

D1 first and alone. D2 and D3 only after D1 is marked — not because they depend on the
result, but because marking D1 will produce `writing-task-1.md`, and writing D2 after
reading that is a cleaner test of whether the fix transfers.

D4 and D5 are independent and can be sat in any order once the book arrives.

## Phase 1 — course weeks 1–2 (4–17 Sept)

Content set after the diagnostic battery. What is already known:

- **Attend selectively.** Six weeks pitched at candidates whose English is the obstacle
  will contain grammar and vocabulary work that is beneath C2. Skipping it is not
  arrogance, it is triage.
- **Do not skip anything involving marked writing or authentic Listening.** Those are the
  two things the course can give you that you cannot easily give yourself.
- The exam is booked and inside this phase's horizon. Everything here serves the sitting,
  not the syllabus.

---

## Phase 2 — the sitting (18 Sept – 1 Oct)

Target the **last week of September**. Result 3–5 days later on computer-delivered.

Non-negotiables on the day: passport matching the booking exactly; Academic, not General
Training; annual leave with no stated reason.

---

## Phase 3 — course weeks 5–6 (2–16 Oct)

This is the phase the whole sequence exists to create. The score report gives four section
bands — real, official, examiner-marked data, arriving with two weeks of paid tuition left
to spend against it.

Two branches:

**If 7.0+ overall with no band below 6.5** — target met. The remaining course weeks become
optional. Stand down, close the folder, and put the time into the application itself.
Attend only the mock if one falls here.

**If short** — the report says exactly which section and by how much. Two weeks of targeted
remediation, then retake in the 20–31 Oct window. This is a far better position than
finishing the course and only then discovering the gap.

## What this plan refuses to do

- **No general English study.** Not one hour. C2 with 22 years of professional use at
  director level; the gap is exam convention, and convention is a small, fixed, learnable
  thing.
- **No untimed practice.** It does not measure anything and it will not go in
  `practice-log.md`.
- **No uncalibrated material.** Cambridge official past papers or nothing. Free practice
  tests predict nothing in either direction.
- **No study plan content invented ahead of the diagnostic.** The blanks above are the
  honest state of knowledge, not an omission.
