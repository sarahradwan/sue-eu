## Why aim at 7.0, not 6.5

Sweden's floor is 6.5, and clearing a floor by one mark is fragile. Three reasons to aim higher:

1. **Erasmus Mundus consortia frequently want 7.0.** It is the hedge route; a 6.5 closes some of it.
2. **Band 6.5 with one weak section fails.** "No band below 5.5" means a single bad Listening section sinks an otherwise fine result. Headroom protects against that.
3. **It outlives Route B.** If she pivots to Route A, several skilled-migration and professional-registration routes read IELTS too.

## Profile — what to actually work on

She is **C2, native Arabic, and has worked in English professionally for 22 years** at
director level with ministerial stakeholders. **The English is not the problem. The exam
format is.** Prep should be weighted accordingly and should not waste her time on general
language study.

Predicted from that profile — to be confirmed by a diagnostic, not assumed:

| Section | Expected | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Speaking** | Strong | She presents and defends creative work to ministers. Part 3 abstract discussion should suit her. |
| **Reading** | Strong content, **time-pressured** | 40 questions in 60 minutes with no extra time. Technique, not comprehension. |
| **Listening** | **Watch this one** | Accent range and single-play audio. The most common place fluent speakers drop a band. |
| **Writing Task 1** | ⚠️ **The real risk** | Academic Task 1 is describing charts, graphs and processes to a rigid convention. Most fluent speakers have never written one. |
| **Writing Task 2** | Format-bound | Strong ideas score badly if the structure is wrong. |

**Note the irony worth exploiting on Task 1:** she does information design and data
visualisation professionally. She reads charts better than most candidates ever will. The
gap is purely the reporting convention — overview sentence, key trends, selective figures,
no interpretation. That is a fast fix, and it should be an easy 7+ rather than the weak spot.
