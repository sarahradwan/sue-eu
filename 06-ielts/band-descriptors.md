# Band descriptors — the marking reference

Everything in this folder is marked against these. They are close paraphrases of the
**public** IELTS Writing band descriptors (the versions IELTS publishes for candidates —
examiners use a more detailed internal set, but the public ones are the same criteria and
the same thresholds).

Read them once now so you know what is actually being bought. They are not what most
people assume: **"good English" is only two of the four criteria.** The other two are about
whether you did the specific job the task set. That is where a C2 speaker with no exam
training loses marks.

---

## How a Writing band is calculated

1. Each task is marked on **four criteria, 0–9, equally weighted.** Average the four.
2. `Writing band = (Task 1 band + 2 × Task 2 band) / 3` — **Task 2 is worth double.**
3. Rounded to the nearest half band.

**Overall IELTS band** = mean of Listening, Reading, Writing, Speaking, rounded to the
nearest half band (.25 rounds up to the next half, .75 rounds up to the next whole).

Sweden's requirement — 6.5 overall, no band below 5.5 — is applied to those four section
bands, not to individual criteria.

## Task 1 — Task Achievement

The criterion that separates "wrote well about the chart" from "did the Task 1 job."

| Band | What it takes |
|---|---|
| **9** | Fully satisfies all requirements; presents a fully developed response. |
| **8** | Covers all requirements sufficiently; clearly presents and highlights key features; could be extended further. |
| **7** | Covers the requirements; **clearly presents an overview of main trends, differences or stages**; presents and highlights key features but could be more fully extended. |
| **6** | Addresses the requirements; **presents an overview** with appropriately selected information; adequately highlights key features, but details may be irrelevant, inappropriate or inaccurate. |
| **5** | Generally addresses the task; format may be inappropriate in places; **recounts detail mechanically with no clear overview**; there may be no data to support the description. |
| **4** | Attempts the task but does not cover all key features; format may be inappropriate. |

**The hard rules under this criterion:**

- **No overview → capped at band 5.** Non-negotiable, and the single most common reason a
  fluent writer scores 5 on Task 1. An overview is a sentence (or two) stating the *big
  picture* with no specific figures — what the whole chart says at a glance.
- **Under 150 words → penalised.** Count with the on-screen counter.
- **Words copied verbatim from the prompt do not count** toward the 150.
- **Do not explain *why*.** Task 1 is description and comparison only. Speculating about
  causes ("this is because broadband became cheaper") is off-task and costs marks. This is
  the instinct hardest to suppress for someone who presents insight for a living.
- **Select — do not list.** Reporting every data point is a band-5 behaviour. Choosing what
  matters is a band-7 behaviour.

## Task 2 — Task Response

| Band | What it takes |
|---|---|
| **9** | Fully addresses all parts; presents a fully developed position with relevant, fully extended and well-supported ideas. |
| **8** | Sufficiently addresses all parts; presents a well-developed response with relevant, extended and supported ideas. |
| **7** | Addresses all parts, though some parts more fully than others; presents a clear position throughout; presents, extends and supports main ideas, but there may be a tendency to over-generalise and/or supporting ideas may lack focus. |
| **6** | Addresses all parts although some parts may be more fully covered than others; presents a relevant position although conclusions may become unclear or repetitive; presents relevant main ideas but some may be inadequately developed or unclear. |
| **5** | Addresses the task only partially; the format may be inappropriate in places; expresses a position but the development is not always clear; presents some main ideas but these are limited and not sufficiently developed; there may be irrelevant detail. |

**Hard rules:** under 250 words is penalised; not answering *all* parts of the question caps
this criterion; a position that is not clear throughout caps it at 6.

## Coherence & Cohesion (both tasks)

| Band | What it takes |
|---|---|
| **9** | Cohesion used so well it attracts no attention; paragraphing skilfully managed. |
| **8** | Sequences information and ideas logically; manages all aspects of cohesion well; paragraphing used sufficiently and appropriately. |
| **7** | Logically organises information; clear progression throughout; uses a range of cohesive devices appropriately, though there may be some under- or over-use. |
| **6** | Arranges information coherently with clear overall progression; uses cohesive devices effectively, but cohesion within and/or between sentences may be faulty or mechanical; referencing may not always be clear. |
| **5** | Some organisation but a lack of overall progression; inadequate, inaccurate or over-use of cohesive devices; may be repetitive for lack of referencing and substitution. |

**Note the trap at band 7→8:** over-using linkers ("Furthermore, Moreover, In addition") is
explicitly a band-7 ceiling behaviour. Mechanical signposting reads as trained, not fluent.

## Lexical Resource (both tasks)

| Band | What it takes |
|---|---|
| **9** | Wide range, very natural and sophisticated control; rare minor errors only as slips. |
| **8** | Wide range used fluently and flexibly to convey precise meaning; skilful use of uncommon items with occasional inaccuracy in word choice or collocation; rare spelling/word-formation errors. |
| **7** | Sufficient range for some flexibility and precision; uses less common items with some awareness of style and collocation; occasional errors in word choice, spelling or word formation. |
| **6** | Adequate range for the task; attempts less common vocabulary with some inaccuracy; some spelling/word-formation errors that do not impede communication. |
| **5** | Limited range, minimally adequate; noticeable spelling/word-formation errors that may cause the reader some difficulty. |

**Spelling is marked here, and computer-delivered IELTS has no spellcheck.** For a C2
writer this is realistically the only route by which Lexical Resource drops below 8 — which
is why D1 must be written with autocorrect off.

---

## Grammatical Range & Accuracy (both tasks)

| Band | What it takes |
|---|---|
| **9** | Wide range with full flexibility and accuracy; rare minor errors only as slips. |
| **8** | Wide range; majority of sentences error-free; only very occasional errors or inappropriacies. |
| **7** | A variety of complex structures; frequent error-free sentences; good control of grammar and punctuation, with a few errors. |
| **6** | A mix of simple and complex forms; some errors in grammar and punctuation that rarely reduce communication. |
| **5** | Limited range; complex sentences attempted but less accurate than simple ones; frequent errors, faulty punctuation, some difficulty for the reader. |

**Punctuation is marked here.** Comma splices are the classic C2-professional error — very
common in confident writers who set their own house style. They are counted as errors.

## Speaking — the four criteria (for D5)

Fluency & Coherence · Lexical Resource · Grammatical Range & Accuracy · **Pronunciation**.

Marking a transcript gives you the first three honestly. **Pronunciation cannot be marked
from text** and will be left blank rather than guessed.

## Listening and Reading — raw score to band

No descriptors; these are marked out of 40 and converted. Approximate Academic conversion
(the exact table shifts slightly by paper — treat as a guide, not a guarantee):

| Band | Listening (/40) | Academic Reading (/40) |
|---|---|---|
| 8.0 | 35 | 35 |
| 7.5 | 32 | 33 |
| **7.0** | **30** | **30** |
| **6.5** | **26** | **27** |
| 6.0 | 23 | 23 |
| 5.5 | 18 | 19 |

To read this correctly: a 7.0 in Reading means **getting 30 of 40 right in 60 minutes with
no extra transfer time.** Academic Reading has no separate transfer period — answers go
straight in. Listening on computer-delivered gives you **2 minutes** at the end, not the
10 minutes the paper test allows.

**Spelling counts in Listening and Reading too.** A correctly heard answer spelled wrong is
simply wrong. There are no half marks anywhere on this exam.
