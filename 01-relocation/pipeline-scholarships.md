## ⛔ CRITICAL PATH — document attestation

**This is now the binding constraint on Route B, ahead of any deadline in this file.**

She holds the transcript and the English certificate. Neither is attested. For an Egyptian
degree that is a serious, slow problem, and it is worse than the equivalent problem for
most nationalities.

**Egypt is not a party to the Hague Apostille Convention.** There is no apostille shortcut.
Egyptian documents need the **full consular legalisation chain**, country by country.
*Search-sourced 21 Aug 2026 — verify at hcch.net before acting.*

### Austria — the heaviest burden, and it gates the live lead

Austrian universities require **full diplomatic legalisation** for Egyptian documents,
because no bilateral agreement is in place. The chain:

1. **Egyptian Ministry of Education / Supreme Council of Universities** — verification
2. **Egyptian Ministry of Foreign Affairs** — authentication
3. **Austrian Embassy, Cairo** — over-legalisation, inspected for factual accuracy

> **"The required legalization must be on the documents at the time of application."**

That last line is the one that hurts. It is not a document you send afterwards.

- WU Vienna publishes an **Egypt-specific legalisation page**:
  https://www.wu.ac.at/en/programs/application-and-admission/legalize/et
- General Austrian requirement, third-country students:
  https://studieren.univie.ac.at/en/admission/info-third-country-students/legalisation/
- Translations, if needed, must be done by a **court-certified interpreter in Austria** —
  translations done elsewhere are not accepted. Her documents may already be in English,
  which would avoid this entirely. **Check.**

**Timing.** The comparable Egypt→UAE chain runs **3–5 weeks** end to end. Egypt→Austria
adds an embassy step and she is executing it from Abu Dhabi, not Cairo — so it needs a
representative in Egypt or a power of attorney. **Budget 6–10 weeks and treat that as
optimistic.**

#### ⚠️ There is no EU-wide recognition. Every country is separate.

**Asked 21 August 2026, and the answer changes how the chain should be run.**

Two different things get conflated, and both are **national, not EU-level**:

| | What it proves | Who decides |
|---|---|---|
| **Legalisation** | The document is **genuine** | The destination country's embassy |
| **Recognition / credential evaluation** | What the degree is **worth** in that system | A national body — Nuffic (NL), ZAB (DE), ENIC-NARIC centres |

**Neither is portable.** An Austrian legalisation does not work in Germany. A Nuffic
evaluation does not work in Sweden. There is no single EU recognition.

**What we have already found proves the point — four countries, four regimes:**

| Country | What it demands | Burden |
|---|---|---|
| **Sweden** | **Nothing.** Scan the originals and upload | 🟢 lightest on the list |
| **Austria** | Full consular chain, **complete at the moment of application** | 🔴 heaviest |
| **Netherlands** | **Nuffic** credential evaluation, which itself needs legalised inputs | 🟠 |
| **Germany** | **ZAB** Statement of Comparability, ~3 months, also needs legalised inputs | 🟠 |

**Why this is Egypt-specific and worse than average:** if Egypt were a Hague Apostille
country, **one apostille would serve every Hague destination** — genuinely one-fits-all.
Egypt is not a member, so the shortcut does not exist and each destination needs its own
embassy stamp.

#### The one efficiency that does exist — and the question to ask VFS

The chain is: **Supreme Council of Universities → Egyptian MoFA → [destination embassy]**.

**The first two steps are shared.** Only the final embassy step is country-specific. So the
MoFA-attested documents can, in principle, then go to several embassies.

**But:** embassies generally attest **each document individually**, stamp **originals**, and
some will not accept documents already bearing another embassy's stamp. Which raises the
question that actually matters:

> **Ask VFS and the agency: how many original sets should I prepare, and can one
> MoFA-attested set be presented to more than one embassy?**

**Get extra originals from Helwan while the request is already open.** Going back for more
later — from Abu Dhabi, through an agent — is another multi-week loop, and it would be

### Sweden — dramatically lighter, and it changes the ranking

### 🟡 AUSTRIA — clears the tuition ceiling, fails the proof-of-funds test without a scholarship

dues of roughly €25/semester. Comfortably under €2,000. This rate is **statutory and
applies across Austrian public universities** — WU, TU Wien, University of Vienna,
University of Applied Arts Vienna, TU Graz, BOKU all quote the same figure.

Confirmed by multiple independent secondary sources; **primary sources blocked.**

**Critical distinction:** this is *public universities* (Universitäten). Austrian
**Fachhochschulen** (universities of applied sciences) set their own fees and are often far
higher for non-EU students. **Check the institution type before anything else.**

## Two checks that kill most leads

**1. The non-EU fee, not the EU fee.** University pages show the EU/EEA fee first and it is
often several times lower. Sara pays the non-EU rate. Always find that number.

**2. Age and experience criteria.** Most schemes target recent graduates. Her 22 years
disqualifies her from some and makes her competitive in others — executive, professional
development and mid-career leadership schemes. **Check this explicitly every time.** It is
the most common reason a promising lead collapses late.

*Pass 1 confirmed this exactly: six of ten schemes checked died on age or degree recency.
The surviving four either ignore age (Erasmus Mundus, Ireland, Flanders) or require senior
experience as a qualifying condition (Sweden).*

**3. Public university, not private, not Fachhochschule.** Added after pass 1. Searches for
low-fee English-taught design programmes surface private institutions constantly (SRH,
BSBI, Berlin School of Design and Communication). They charge full fees. In Austria the
same trap is public *Universität* vs *Fachhochschule* — only the former has the €726.72 rate.

## Also verify

- Does the programme lead to a **residence permit**, and are there post-study work rights?
- Is the scholarship actually running this intake? Consortium funding runs in multi-year
  editions; some intakes are self-funded only.
- Is it English-taught? German A1 and French A2 are below instruction level.
- Does it accept a 2003 bachelor's plus professional experience, or require recent study?
- **Proof of funds — the test this file now applies to everything.** She has **~€6,000
  mobile**. Any route requiring more than that in provable funds needs a stipend to close
  the gap, or it is not a route. Tuition under €2,000 does not mean affordable — Austria wants ~€15,700
  proven for 12 months, Germany wants a blocked account. Budget the permit, not just the fee.
