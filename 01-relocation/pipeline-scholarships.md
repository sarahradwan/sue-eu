- Generic conversion calculators put Excellent ≈ 4.0 and Very Good ≈ 3.7–4.0, which would

## ⛔ CRITICAL PATH — document attestation

**This is now the binding constraint on Route B, ahead of any deadline in this file.**

She holds the transcript and the English certificate. Neither is attested. For an Egyptian
degree that is a serious, slow problem, and it is worse than the equivalent problem for
most nationalities.

**Egypt is not a party to the Hague Apostille Convention.** There is no apostille shortcut.
Egyptian documents need the **full consular legalisation chain**, country by country.
*Search-sourced 25 Jul 2026 — verify at hcch.net before acting.*

### Austria — the heaviest burden, and it gates the live lead

Austrian universities require **full diplomatic legalisation** for Egyptian documents,
because no bilateral agreement is in place. The chain:

1. **Egyptian Ministry of Education / Supreme Council of Universities** — verification
2. **Egyptian Ministry of Foreign Affairs** — authentication
3. **Austrian Embassy, Cairo** — over-legalisation, inspected for factual accuracy

> **"The required legalization must be on the documents at the time of application."**

That last line is the one that hurts. It is not a document you send afterwards.

- WU Vienna publishes an **Egypt-specific legalisation page**:
  https://www.wu.ac.at/en/programs/application-and-admission/legalize/et
- General Austrian requirement, third-country students:
  https://studieren.univie.ac.at/en/admission/info-third-country-students/legalisation/
- Translations, if needed, must be done by a **court-certified interpreter in Austria** —
  translations done elsewhere are not accepted. Her documents may already be in English,
  which would avoid this entirely. **Check.**

**Timing.** The comparable Egypt→UAE chain runs **3–5 weeks** end to end. Egypt→Austria
adds an embassy step and she is executing it from Abu Dhabi, not Cairo — so it needs a
representative in Egypt or a power of attorney. **Budget 6–10 weeks and treat that as
optimistic.**

**Consequence for the live lead:** WU Vienna's **round 1 closes 8 Oct 2026, 75 days out**.
Starting legalisation today, that is achievable only if nothing goes wrong. **Target the
8 January 2027 round instead**, and treat round 1 as a bonus if the papers land early.
Do not let the October date drive a rushed, rejected application.

### Sweden — dramatically lighter, and it changes the ranking

Sweden's central admissions service does **not** require consular legalisation:

- **Scan your original documents and upload them.** Certified copies are *not* accepted for
  the upload route.
- Postal route instead needs copies certified by the issuing university or a notary.
- Arabic documents need the original **plus** an official English or Swedish translation.
  Documents already issued in English need no translation.
- Name and date of birth must match across every document — ID, transcript, certificate.
- Country page: https://www.universityadmissions.se/en/apply-to-masters/provide-application-documents-masters/country-instructions/egypt/

**So the strongest scheme is also the one with the lightest paperwork.** Sweden needs a
scanner and a translator; Austria needs three ministries and an embassy. That asymmetry
should shape the order she does the work in.

## Two checks that kill most leads

**1. The non-EU fee, not the EU fee.** University pages show the EU/EEA fee first and it is
often several times lower. Sara pays the non-EU rate. Always find that number.

**2. Age and experience criteria.** Most schemes target recent graduates. Her 22 years
disqualifies her from some and makes her competitive in others — executive, professional
development and mid-career leadership schemes. **Check this explicitly every time.** It is
the most common reason a promising lead collapses late.

*Pass 1 confirmed this exactly: six of ten schemes checked died on age or degree recency.
The surviving four either ignore age (Erasmus Mundus, Ireland, Flanders) or require senior
experience as a qualifying condition (Sweden).*

**3. Public university, not private, not Fachhochschule.** Added after pass 1. Searches for
low-fee English-taught design programmes surface private institutions constantly (SRH,
BSBI, Berlin School of Design and Communication). They charge full fees. In Austria the
same trap is public *Universität* vs *Fachhochschule* — only the former has the €726.72 rate.

- **Proof of funds — the test this file now applies to everything.** She has **~€6,000
