# Review — EU Country Portfolio, 12 August 2026

Sara supplied a country portfolio (markdown) plus two market-scan CSVs. Reviewed 12 Aug 2026.

**Verdict: adopt the strategy, distrust the ratings.** The thinking is good and one insight
is better than anything the vault had. But the three documents contradict each other on two
of the three proposed core markets, and the markdown is systematically more optimistic than
its own underlying research.

---

## 1. ⚠️ The contradictions — read before acting on any rating

The markdown and the CSVs disagree, sharply, on the same countries:

| Market | Markdown verdict | CSV verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **Belgium / Brussels** | **#3, "Strong selective", core portfolio** | **"Low realism — insurmountable labour market testing hurdles"** |
| **Netherlands** | **#2, "Strong selective", core portfolio** | "Conditional" |
| **Ireland** | #9, "Conditional, role-by-role" | **"Low realism"** |

These are not shades of emphasis. "Core portfolio" and "low realism" are opposite
instructions about where to spend the next six months.

**On Belgium the markdown shows its own weak reasoning.** It promotes Brussels partly
because *"the official highly-qualified page does not state the generic labour-market-test
wording."* That is **inferring the absence of a requirement from silence on a webpage**,
which is exactly the standard the vault refuses elsewhere. The CSV, by contrast, cites the
labour market test as binding.

**Neither is verified.** One is inference from omission, the other is assertion. Belgium
needs a direct answer before it gets core-market effort.

## 2. The Netherlands claim is half right, and the missing half matters

The markdown is correct that **an employer does not need IND recognised-sponsor status for
an EU Blue Card**, only for the Highly Skilled Migrant scheme. That matches
`pipeline-jobs.md` and it genuinely widens the employer pool.

**But it never states the Blue Card salary threshold.** Checked: for 2026 the standard Dutch
Blue Card threshold is **€5,942 gross per month, the same figure as HSM for applicants aged
30+**, with a reduced €4,754 rate in limited cases.
([Exterus](https://exterus.nl/en/blogs/preliminary-salary-thresholds-for-highly-skilled-migrants-and-blue-card-holders-2026) ·
[Everaert](https://www.everaert.nl/en/new-salary-thresholds-2026/))

So the Blue Card widens **which employers can apply**, not **what they must pay**. That is
roughly **€71,300 a year** for a senior creative hire who needs sponsorship. Presenting the
Netherlands as "wider" without that number overstates the opening.

## 3. It omits Route A2 entirely, and contradicts the vault on the Spanish DNV

The portfolio has no mention of remote work for a non-EU employer converting to a Spanish
digital nomad visa. `pipeline-jobs.md` treats that as **one of only two live channels**.

Worse, the CSV states the DNV is *"precluded"* by lack of capital. The vault's finding is
that Spain's financial test can be met by the **income limb (€2,849/month)** rather than
savings, which is precisely why A2 survived the no-capital constraint.

**One of these is wrong and it is worth settling**, because A2 is the only channel that can
move while the Egyptian legalisation is still in transit.

## 4. The "no capital" premise is going stale

Several assessments lean on "no relocation capital before mid-2027" as a structural blocker.
`08-uae-income/README.md` now projects **~€30,600 banked by 31 May 2027** at AED 24,000/month,
which would clear Germany's blocked account (~€13,100) and Austria's proof of funds (€15,701).

That does not make those countries easy. It does mean a premise several ratings rest on is
being actively dismantled, and the ratings should be re-read when the money is real rather
than projected.

## 5. It does not know about the MoFA annual report

Understandable given the date, but it changes the "distinctive fit" gate. **Corporate
reporting and investor communications is now direct evidence, not adjacency**, and that is a
pan-European sector with English-led firms. Emperor alone has London, Edinburgh, Manchester
and Warwick.

## What is genuinely excellent, and should be adopted now

**1. The role-title pivot. This is the best idea in the document.**

> "Reduce dependence on generic Creative Director, Head of Creative, and Design Director
> applications. Their small number, local-market expectations, and leadership-network bias
> make them a difficult first European entry point."

It is right, and it is not obvious. Creative Director roles are few, culturally anchored,
and filled through local networks. The proposed alternatives — **Editorial Designer,
Publication Design Lead, Design Systems Lead, Brand Experience / Institutional
Communications Lead, Senior Art Director** — are where her output is rare rather than merely
strong. **Adopt this immediately.** It should reshape every search string the daily agent
runs.

**2. Sweden promoted to first.** Well argued and consistent with the scholarship research:
English-capable employers, a work-permit threshold of **SEK 34,470/month** that a senior
salary clears easily, and SI Global Professionals keeping Route B alive in the same country.

**3. Germany's age gate is a hard, specific finding.** First qualified-worker application at
**45+ requires €55,770 gross annually** or proof of pension provision. That is a real,
checkable number and it correctly demotes Germany from volume market to rare-match only.

**4. Denmark's Pay Limit route.** DKK 552,000 with a one-month Fast-Track for certified
employers. Clean, narrow, worth watchlisting.

**5. The four-gate filter.** Employer capacity, working language, distinctive fit, permit and
compensation. Better than the vault's current screen. Fold it in.

## What I would do

| Action | Why |
|---|---|
| **Adopt the role-title pivot now** | Highest-value change, costs nothing, reshapes every search |
| **Adopt Sweden as core** | Consistent across both routes and both documents |
| **Hold Belgium at "unverified", not core** | Two documents flatly disagree and the optimistic one reasons from silence |
| **Re-read Netherlands against €5,942/month** | The threshold, not the sponsor register, is the real filter |
| **Settle the Spanish DNV question** | Income limb vs savings limb decides whether Route A2 lives. `savings-target.md` already calls this the one email worth €19,000 |
| **Keep the four-gate filter** | Better than what the vault screens on today |
| **Re-run the ratings once savings are real** | Several rest on a premise that is being removed |

**The honest summary:** this is a good strategist's document with an unreliable scoreboard.
Take the strategy, the role pivot and the four gates. Treat every country rating as a
hypothesis to verify, not a conclusion, because the same research package rates Belgium both
third-best and unrealistic.
