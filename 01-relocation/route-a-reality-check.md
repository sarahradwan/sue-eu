## 2. The honest assessment — is this working?

**Route A is not failing. It is under-powered. Those are different problems with different
fixes, and conflating them leads to quitting something that is merely too small.**

**Why 0 interviews from 14 is not yet a verdict.** For a candidate who **requires
sponsorship**, has **no EU work experience**, and applies at **senior level**, realistic
application-to-interview rates in creative fields are **low single digits**. At a 5% rate, the
expected number of interviews from 14 applications is **under one.** Getting zero is exactly
what the arithmetic predicts. It is not evidence the approach is broken; it is evidence the
sample is too small to tell.

**But run the arithmetic forward, because this is the part that matters.** At ~5%
application-to-interview and ~20% interview-to-offer, **an offer needs roughly 100
applications.** At the current rate — deeply tailored CV and cover letter per role, maybe
5–10 a week at best alongside a full-time job and CIM Level 7 — **that is six months to a
year.**

**So the real finding is not "this isn't working." It is: at this volume, this route alone
takes longer than the timeline allows.**

**Three structural filters stack, and each one is multiplicative:**

1. **Sponsorship required.** Egyptian passport, no EU right to work. Removes most employers
   before anything else is read.
2. **No EU work experience.** A far heavier objection at Director and Head-of level, where
   employers are buying local market knowledge and an existing network.
3. **Senior level.** Fewer roles exist, and sponsorship willingness *drops* as seniority rises
   because employers want someone operating on day one.

None of these are about her ability. All three are about category.

## 3. What has actually been learned — the verified findings

These are real and they hold regardless of what happens next.

### Permit thresholds, 2026 (gross annual)

| Country | Floor | Note |
|---|---|---|
| 🇮🇪 **Ireland** | **€36,605** GEP · €40,904 Critical Skills | **Lowest in the set. English by default** |
| 🇪🇸 Spain | €41,356 Blue Card | |
| 🇧🇪 Brussels | €44,441 | Flanders €48,912 · Wallonia €53,220 |
| 🇩🇪 **Germany** | €50,700 Blue Card · €45,934 shortage · **€55,770 if aged 45+** | **See §18b below — this is not a hard floor** |
| 🇳🇱 **Netherlands** | **€71,304** HSM (age 30+) | **Highest by a wide margin** |

### 🔑 Germany's §18b — the salary floor is not a floor

**§18b AufenthG is Germany's default residence permit for non-EU university graduates and has
NO fixed salary minimum.** The offer must match local market rate and pass a Federal Employment
Agency review. **The Blue Card's €50,700 is the bar for the *better* permit — faster permanent
residency, easier family reunification — not the bar for eligibility.**

**Consequence: German roles paying €42,000–48,000 are viable, and this search had been
discarding that entire band.**

⚠️ **Unverified prerequisite: `anabin` degree recognition.** Her BCS Advertising & Graphic
Design, Helwan University 2003 must show as recognised. **Takes minutes to check and gates the
whole route.**

### Sponsorship is only pre-checkable in some countries

| Country | Can you verify before applying? |
|---|---|
| 🇳🇱 Netherlands | ✅ **Hard legal gate.** Employer must be on the **public IND erkend referent register**. Applying to a non-recognised employer cannot succeed |
| 🇮🇪 Ireland | ✅ **Postings often state it explicitly** — e.g. an EY Ireland listing carries *"Available for Work Visa Sponsorship: No"*. **The best verification environment of any country in the set** |
| 🇩🇪 Germany | ❌ No register needed — any employer can hire on a Blue Card or §18b. **Willingness gate only** |
| 🇧🇪 Belgium | ❌ No register. Employer files regionally. Willingness gate |

### Market intelligence — Ryan Sonder, The Chain Company (Dutch agency, IND recognised sponsor)

> *"For most of our SME clients, Dutch is the primary working language... At larger international
> or publicly listed companies, English is sometimes sufficient, but those vacancies are
> predominantly within Finance and IT rather than creative roles. **At the moment, we have no
> English-speaking opportunities within the creative sector.**"*

**The Netherlands is the hardest country in her set** — highest floor, hard legal gate, scarce
English creative roles — and it was being led with because "Netherlands first" was a stated
preference that went unchallenged into the brief. **It remains open through exactly one door:
large international employers with in-house creative teams** (Nike Hilversum, Adyen, Booking,
Philips, Heineken). Not agencies, not SMEs, not recruiters.

### Reading rejections

**A 24-hour rejection is a filter, not an opinion.** Nobody assesses a Head of Creative
portfolio overnight. Fast rejections mean the application was matched against a hard criterion —
work authorisation, location, salary, years-in-domain — and dropped.

## 7. ⭐ THE ACTUAL QUESTION FOR THE NEW CHAT

Not *"is Route A working."* The arithmetic in §2 answers that: **it is too small, not broken.**
The real questions are these three.

### Q1. Volume versus tailoring — the central trade-off

Every application so far has had a **bespoke CV variant and a written-from-scratch cover
letter.** That is high quality and it caps output at perhaps 5–10 a week. **If ~100
applications are needed, this rate cannot reach it in the time available.**

**The options are genuinely different, and this is Sara's call:**
- **Keep depth, accept 6–12 months.**
- **Split the pipeline**: deep tailoring for the ~20% best-matched roles, a fast reusable
  variant for the rest, tripling volume.
- **Change the target category** so the hit rate rises instead of the volume — see Q2.

### Q2. Is she applying for the wrong category of role?

**Evidence that she might be:** all four leadership titles that reached a decision were
rejected (Creative Director, Head of Creative, Design Director, Creative Lead). Her own
instinct — *"i am ready to settle for less senior as i am new to the market"* — is probably
correct. **And the publishing/editorial category was untested until 3 Aug**, despite being 15
of her 22 years and the place where her rarest credential (multi-script editorial systems)
actually counts.

**Adyen's rejection weakens this**, since it was a senior IC role — though it had its own known
cause. **This is a hypothesis, not a finding.** But it points somewhere specific: **senior IC
craft roles in publishing and editorial, in Ireland and Germany**, rather than leadership
titles at agencies in the Netherlands.

### Q3. 🎯 The one that actually matters — **Route A is one of two routes, and Route B has been running at a fraction of the effort**

Her brief names **two acceptable routes**: a job with sponsorship, **or a funded scholarship**.
Route A has taken nearly all the attention. **That balance deserves to be revisited, and not as
consolation — on the merits:**

- **A student visa is far easier to obtain than a work permit.** No employer has to be
  persuaded, no salary threshold applies, no sponsor register gates it.
- **It removes objection #2 permanently.** An EU degree and EU time on the ground dissolves
  "no EU work experience," which is the objection behind most of these rejections.
- **Both the Netherlands and Germany grant post-study job-search residence** — the Dutch
  *zoekjaar* / orientation year, and Germany's 18-month post-graduation permit. **A study route
  does not replace Route A; it converts Route A from hard to normal.**
- **Scholarship deadlines are the scarce resource** — rounds close months before intake, so
  delay here costs a whole year in a way that a delayed job application does not.

**The recommendation to discuss: run both, and rebalance toward Route B.** Route A continues at
higher volume and lower per-application cost, aimed at publishing/editorial and Ireland. Route B
gets real hours, because it has the higher completion probability and the harder deadlines.

⚠️ **Route B lives in `01-relocation/pipeline-scholarships.md`, owned by a separate session.
This session never touched it.** **The new chat should read it first** — the honest answer to
*"is this going anywhere"* may already be sitting in that file.

## 9. One thing worth saying plainly

**Eight rejections in five months, five of them in two days, with zero interviews, feels like a
verdict. It is not one.** It is a small sample from a hard category, applied at low volume, with
two of the eight traceable to specific fixable errors on this side rather than to her.

**What would be a real warning sign:** 50+ well-targeted applications, verified sponsors,
correct level, still no interviews. **She is at 14, most of them 36 hours old, and the two
categories where she is genuinely rare — multi-script editorial systems, and AI governance
written into brand systems — have barely been tested.**

The right response to this week is not to stop. It is to **change what is being applied for,
raise the volume, and give Route B the hours it has not been getting.**
