# 01 — Relocation

Top priority. Two routes to the EU: a job with visa sponsorship, or funded study.
See `eligibility-filter.md` for what qualifies.

## Files

| File | Purpose |
|---|---|
| `eligibility-filter.md` | The definition of "qualifies". Changes go here first. |
| `opportunity-pipeline.md` | Live tracker of every lead and its stage |
| `country-notes.md` | Per-country immigration routes, industry, language, costs |

## The weekly rhythm

**Monday check-in.** Each week:

1. **Scan** — new leads via `eu-opportunity-scout`, tested against the filter.
2. **Flag deadlines** — anything inside 60 days goes to the top of the pipeline.
3. **Progress** — what moved since last week, what stalled.
4. **One concrete action** — a single specific next step, not a list. The point is
   momentum, not a backlog.

Findings get written into the vault, not just said in chat.

## Standing research questions

Answers land in `country-notes.md` as they are found and verified.

1. Which EU countries offer routes that **don't require an employer sponsor** — job-seeker
   visas, orientation-year permits, talent/self-employment routes? These widen Route A the most.
2. Where does public tuition sit **at or under €2,000/year** for a relevant master's?
   Portugal, Spain, Germany and Austria are the likely candidates.
3. Which scholarship bodies fund **non-EU mid-career professionals** — not just recent
   graduates? Her 22 years is unusual for a scholarship pool and may be an advantage or a
   disqualifier depending on the scheme. This needs checking carefully.
4. Which EU creative markets hire in **English** at director level?
5. Does the CIM Level 7, once complete, change eligibility for any funded route?

## Framing note

Her real hurdle is not portfolio quality — 22 years, a €500k national programme, and
government-level stakeholder work stand up anywhere. It is **right to work**. So the
search optimises for *route availability first*, role or programme prestige second.
