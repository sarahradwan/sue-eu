## No capital to self-fund — added 25 Jul 2026, and it cuts across both routes

Also confirmed by Sara: **she has no capital to self-fund.** She needs someone else to pay
for the permit — an employer, or a salary that funds a residence permit she holds herself.

**Refined by the scholarships session, 25 Jul 2026 — the detail matters:** roughly **€6,000**
is *projected* from end-of-service gratuity plus selling possessions, but it **does not exist
now** and is realisable **only just before travelling** — i.e. *after* the spring 2027 permit
application that would need to show it. So it is **arrival money, not permit money, and not
preparation money.** Two consequences for both routes:

- **Preparation costs (~€1,200–2,400: legalisation, translations, IELTS, permit fees) must
  come from salary**, roughly €100–200/month from August 2026.
- ⚠️ **The gratuity may be smaller than expected.** Her WeDo tenure began **Feb 2025** — about
  2.5 years by a mid-2027 departure, which at the standard 21-days-per-year rate is on the
  order of 7–8 weeks of *basic* pay. **Worth calculating, not assuming.**

**Route A** therefore records only two kinds of route:

- **A1 — an employer sponsors the work permit.**
- **A2 — a remote job for a non-EU employer funds a digital nomad visa she holds herself.**

Every EU "come and look for work" permit is now **Ruled out** — gathered in one table at
the bottom of this file so no future session spends a pass rediscovering them.

**⚠️ This also touches Route B, and the scholarships session should know.** Several study
routes carry a **proof-of-funds or blocked-account requirement that is separate from
tuition** — Austria wants roughly **€15,700** for the student permit, Germany a blocked
account for the student visa. A route that solves tuition but not proof of funds does not
clear her constraint. Scholarships paying a **monthly stipend** (SISGP, Master Mind,
Erasmus Mundus) may satisfy it; a tuition-only saving does not. Worth confirming scheme by
scheme — it may reorder the Route B ranking.

## Verification caveat — read this before trusting a figure below

Both research passes of **25 July 2026** ran from an environment whose egress policy blocks
the official domains. Route A: `ind.nl`, `migration.gv.at`, `business.gov.nl`,
`make-it-in-germany.com`. Route B: `si.se`, `wu.ac.at`, `tuwien.at`, `bmfwf.gv.at`,
`erasmus-plus.ec.europa.eu`. All refused at the proxy. Findings below were gathered from
**search-engine extracts of those official pages**, not from opening the pages directly.

That is good enough to decide *where to aim*. It is **not** good enough to rely on a
specific euro figure, points value or deadline in an application. Every number tagged
`[verify on source]` must be re-read on the official page before it is used.

The **rules** (who is eligible, whether a sponsor is needed, whether an age cap exists) are
consistent across multiple official-page extracts and are treated as solid. The **numbers**
are not.

## Netherlands — *preferred* · **strongest A1 market**, weak Route B

### Route A — A1, employer-sponsored. Researched 25 Jul 2026.

*(The orientation year / zoekjaar is closed to her — see **Ruled out** at the foot of this
file. It recurs constantly in expat blogs as "the" Dutch route and does not apply to a
mid-career applicant.)*

**1. EU Blue Card — ✅ does NOT require a recognised sponsor. The key Dutch finding.**

> "Your employer does not need to be a recognised sponsor in order to apply for a
> European Blue Card."
> — [IND, via official-page extract](https://ind.nl/en/residence-permits/work/european-blue-card-residence-permit)

This roughly doubles the addressable Dutch employer set. A Dutch employer who has never
hired a migrant before can still hire her — no pre-existing IND status needed.

Conditions:
- **Higher education programme of at least 3 years' duration**, evidenced by an original
  credential evaluation from **Nuffic**.
- **⛔ And Nuffic cannot start until the Helwan documents are legalised** — see the
  Apostille box above. Legalisation (6–10 weeks) → Nuffic (weeks). **Sequence matters, and
  this is the longest lead time on the Dutch route.** Start legalisation now.
- Employment contract in paid employment; contracts under 6 months get treated
  differently for the income test.
- Salary threshold applies, revised **1 January and 1 July** each year `[verify on source]`.

**2. Highly skilled migrant (kennismigrant) — employer must be a recognised sponsor.**

The employer must hold IND **recognised sponsor** (*erkend referent*) status. This is the
real filter on the Dutch job search: an employer that is willing but not recognised
cannot use this route without first applying for recognition itself.

- Public register of recognised sponsors, category *Regular Labour and Highly Skilled
  Migrants*: [ind.nl/en/public-register-recognised-sponsors/public-register-regular-labour-and-highly-skilled-migrants](https://ind.nl/en/public-register-recognised-sponsors/public-register-regular-labour-and-highly-skilled-migrants)
- Updated **monthly**. Roughly 10,000–12,000 organisations `[verify on source]`.
- 2026 gross monthly salary thresholds, excl. holiday allowance `[verify on source]`:
  **€5,942** (age 30+) · **€4,357** (under 30) · **€3,122** reduced rate, which applies
  only to former orientation-year holders — so **not available to her**.

The 30+ threshold is the number that matters, and it is high: roughly **€71k base**.
That is a genuine constraint on which creative roles can carry the permit — it points at
in-house brand/design leadership and larger agency groups, not mid-size studios.

**Net effect for the Netherlands:** Route A runs on **two parallel channels** — the sponsor
register (HSM) and any willing employer (Blue Card). Search both. **Do not let the register
become the whole search.**

## Austria — *preferred* · **Route A weak, Route B the best on the list**

### Route A — A1 only

**The six-month RWR job seeker visa is out** — self-funded, *and* it fails on points. See
**Ruled out**. Her estimated score is **30–50 against a threshold of 70**: age is confirmed
at **0 of 20** (DOB 11 Aug 1980), work experience maxes at 20, language at 10, and the
40-point "special qualifications" block is written for MINT degrees, PhDs, patents and
publications. The gap is structural, not a matter of presentation.
*Source: [migration.gv.at — Very Highly Qualified Workers](https://www.migration.gv.at/en/types-of-immigration/permanent-immigration/very-highly-qualified-workers/) ·
[official points calculator](https://www.migration.gv.at/en/service-and-links/points-calculator/)*

The separate **Other Key Workers** RWR category has a lower threshold around **55 points**
and **requires a job offer**, so an employer carries it. **Worth scoring properly on the
official calculator before writing Austria off** — that is the one Austrian Route A still
open.

Minimum gross salary for RWR employment: approx. **€3,465/month** (2026) `[verify on source]`.

**Note the asymmetry:** *"studies completed in Austria"* is worth 10 points on this table,
and the Route B section below puts an Austrian degree within financial reach. **Route B
feeds Route A here.** A master's at a Vienna public university would add those 10 points,
convert the age problem into a graduate route, and open the Red-White-Red Card for
Graduates — a far lower bar than either the 70-point job seeker visa or the 55-point Other
Key Workers threshold.

### Route B — funded study — **strongest Route B country found so far**

- **Non-EU tuition (public universities): €726.72 per semester = €1,453.44/year**, plus ÖH
  student union dues ~€25/semester. **Under the €2,000 ceiling.** Statutory and uniform
  across public universities — WU, TU Wien, Uni Wien, Die Angewandte, TU Graz, BOKU all
  quote it. https://www.bmfwf.gv.at/en/science/studying/tuition-fees.html
- **⚠️ Public university ≠ Fachhochschule.** The €726.72 rate is for *Universitäten*.
  Austrian universities of applied sciences set their own fees and are frequently far
  higher for non-EU students. Check institution type first, every time.
- **Student residence permit:** *Aufenthaltsbewilligung Studierende*, available for full
  degrees over 6 months. ✅ **Route B leads to a residence permit.** Post-study: the student
  permit can be extended **12 months to seek work**, or convert straight to the
  **Red–White–Red Card for Graduates** with a job in hand. ✅ **Post-study work rights.**
  https://oead.at/en/to-austria/entry-and-residence/residence-permit-student-no-mobility-programme
- **⛔ Proof of funds — and with the no-capital constraint this is now the binding problem,
  not tuition:** age 24+ → **€1,308.39/month for 12 months ≈ €15,700**, plus €386.43/month
  if rent exceeds that. **That is capital she does not have.** Cheap tuition does not solve
  it. An Austrian study route only works with a **stipend-paying** scholarship large enough
  to satisfy the permit.
- **Scholarship bodies:** OeAD is the national agency. **Its Ernst Mach Grant is dead for
  her** — age cap 35, and it does not fund full degrees. Austria's value is the low fee, not
  its scholarships — which, given the proof-of-funds problem, is a sharper weakness than it
  first looked.
- **⛔ Document legalisation — the heaviest of any country here, and it gates admission.**
  Austrian universities require **full diplomatic legalisation** for Egyptian documents; no
  bilateral agreement is in place. Chain: Egyptian Ministry of Education / Supreme Council
  of Universities → Egyptian MoFA → **over-legalisation by the Austrian Embassy in Cairo**,
  which inspects for factual accuracy. **"The required legalization must be on the documents
  at the time of application"** — it cannot be supplied late. Translations, if needed, must
  be done by a **court-certified interpreter in Austria**; translations done elsewhere are
  rejected. WU publishes an Egypt-specific page:
  https://www.wu.ac.at/en/programs/application-and-admission/legalize/et ·
  https://studieren.univie.ac.at/en/admission/info-third-country-students/legalisation/
- **Live lead:** **WU Vienna MSc Marketing**, English-taught, €1,453/year.
  Portal opens **1 Sept 2026**; rounds close **8 Oct 2026** / 8 Jan / 8 Mar 2027.
  **Target round 2 (8 Jan 2027), not round 1** — legalisation will not realistically be
  complete by October. Open risk: WU requires prior business/economics ECTS, which a 2003
  Advertising & Graphic Design degree may not carry.
- **Next:** enumerate English-taught master's at Die Angewandte, Kunstuni Linz and the
  Academy of Fine Arts — all public, all on the same fee.

- **Creative industry:** Vienna
- **Language:** German-dominant — a real constraint at A1 for Route A. But **English-taught
  master's at public universities exist and carry the same low fee**, which is what makes

- **Status:** A1 open but low priority · **Route B strong on tuition, now weak on proof of

## Spain — **A2 lead. The nomad route that actually fits her.**

### Route A — A2, remote job + Digital Nomad Visa. Researched 25 Jul 2026.

Under the Startups Law, for third-country nationals working remotely for companies based
outside Spain. **The lowest realistic income bar of any EU nomad visa that also leads to
permanent residence.**

- **Income: €2,849/month ≈ €34,188/year** — 200% of the Spanish minimum wage (SMI), 2026
  `[verify on source]`. For a Creative Director with 22 years, a **low** bar.
- **Employer based outside Spain**, no more than 20% of income from Spanish companies. Gulf,
  UK or US employers all fine.
- **3 months minimum with that employer** before applying; employer trading **≥1 year**.
- **Bachelor's degree *or* 3+ years professional experience** — she clears both limbs, and
  the second limb means a legalisation delay on the Helwan degree need not block this route
  the way it blocks Nuffic and ZAB.
- Private health insurance, criminal record certificate, social security coverage.
- **Beckham Law:** flat **24%** income tax up to €600,000, against a standard rate reaching 47%.
- **Permanent residence after 5 years; citizenship after 10.**

**Her passport decides which application route she gets:**

- *From inside Spain as a tourist* → a **3-year** permit directly, with automatic approval if
  the authorities miss a 20-working-day deadline. **Requires visa-free entry — not available
  on an Egyptian passport.**
- *From the Spanish consulate in her country of residence* → a **1-year** visa, renewed
  inside Spain afterwards. **This is her route.**

As a UAE resident she applies at the Spanish mission in Abu Dhabi, which publishes a
dedicated page:
[exteriores.gob.es — Abu Dhabi, Digital Nomad Visa](https://www.exteriores.gob.es/Embajadas/abudhabi/en/ServiciosConsulares/Paginas/Consular/Digital-Nomada-Visa.aspx).
UAE residents explicitly eligible.

**The difficulty is not the visa, it is the remote job.** Creative Director roles are
overwhelmingly on-site — studio presence, shoot direction, client meetings. Remote CD roles
are a far thinner market than remote engineering or marketing. See `pipeline-jobs.md` for
where that search points.

## Sweden — **new section, added Route B pass 1**

Not previously on the list. Added because it holds the **single best structural fit found
so far** for a mid-career applicant.

### Route B — funded study

- **Scholarship body:** ✅ **Swedish Institute Scholarships for Global Professionals
  (SISGP)** — full tuition + **SEK 12,000/month** + insurance + **SEK 10,000–15,000**
  travel grant.
  https://si.se/en/apply/scholarships/swedish-institute-scholarships-for-global-professionals/
- **Why it fits where others do not:** eligibility **requires ≥3,000 hours** of documented
  work experience plus demonstrated leadership. **No age limit found.** Every other scheme
  on this list treats 22 years as disqualifying; this one treats it as the qualification.
- ✅ **Nationality gate CLEARED (25 Jul 2026): she holds an Egyptian passport, and Egypt is
  on the eligible-country list.** This was the blocker on the strongest Route B lead.
- ✅ **Lightest document burden of any country in this file — and it is not close.** Swedish
  central admissions wants **scans of the originals uploaded** (certified copies are *not*
  accepted for upload; the postal route wants copies certified by the issuing university or
  a notary). **No consular legalisation.** Arabic documents need an official English or
  Swedish translation alongside the original; documents already in English need none. Name
  and date of birth must match exactly across passport, transcript and certificate.
  https://www.universityadmissions.se/en/apply-to-masters/provide-application-documents-masters/country-instructions/egypt/

  **The strategic point:** the strongest scheme also has the lightest paperwork. Sweden
  needs a scanner; Austria needs three ministries and an embassy. That should decide the
  order the work gets done in.
- **Calendar, and it is unforgiving:**
  - Eligible-programme list published **mid-November**
  - Admission via universityadmissions.se closes **mid-January** — must already be admitted
  - SI scholarship portal opens for **~2 weeks in February** (2026/27 round: 9–25 Feb 2026)
  - **2027/28 dates not yet published.** Missing January makes February irrelevant.
- **Language:** ~700 **English-taught** master's are eligible. No Swedish needed.
- **Immigration:** Swedish student residence permit for full-degree study — **not verified
  this pass.** Post-study work rights unverified.

## EU-wide — not a country, but the widest door

**Erasmus Mundus Joint Masters.** ~**€1,400/month** plus tuition and travel, across a
consortium of two or more EU institutions. **No age limit and no degree-recency rule** —

Deadlines are **per-consortium**, generally **Oct 2026 – Jan 2027** for a Sept 2027 start.
https://erasmus-plus.ec.europa.eu/opportunities/individuals/students/erasmus-mundus-joint-masters
