## Chapter Two
## I'm the Success and the Sabotage

I was twenty-three when I went to my first job interview. My dad came with me.

Not to the building. Into the room.

It was a pharmaceutical company in a new neighbourhood. Pure admin work: assistant to the
marketing manager. One of my relatives had found it for me. Back then there was no LinkedIn.
Jobs lived in the newspaper and in word of mouth, and I was a fresh graduate who wanted to work
and had no luck finding any.

I had tasted my own money once already. While I was still studying, I did a job for someone
through Elance, the site that later became Upwork. They paid me a hundred dollars, and I bought
my first mobile phone. So yes, it was admin. But I was excited, and I wanted so badly to put my
talent into action.

My dad was my guardian angel. He knew I was too shy even to buy something from the baqala. So
he came with me, and when it was time, he went in with me too.

The manager greeted him with his eyebrows high in the sky. He couldn't believe it. A full-grown
woman in her twenties, bringing her dad to a job interview.

Ten years later, I got on a plane for the first time in my life.

I was thirty-three, and I am afraid of heights. A newspaper in Doha wanted me to move there, so
they flew me out to see the country and get used to it. They put me in one of the best hotels in
the city, in Al Dafna, and gave me pocket money for my stay. Six days, then they extended it by
four. They wanted me to stay longer, but it was nearly Eid al-Adha, and I went home to celebrate
with my mum.

A colleague met me at the airport, helped me check in, and took me straight to the newspaper to
meet everyone. It was awkward. I was a shy, introverted Egyptian woman doing a lot of things for
the first time.

This was the girl who, at eighteen, didn't dare book private lessons because she would have to
talk to strangers. The girl whose dad walked her into her first interview. The girl who turned
down any job more than thirty minutes from home.

That girl was out of her comfort zone, out of her city, her country, out of her old self.

The room was enormous and faced the Gulf. There was a Nespresso machine. There was an iPhone
charger built into a speaker.

That night, on that comfy bed, in a country that sleeps at nine, I found peace. I found a new me.
