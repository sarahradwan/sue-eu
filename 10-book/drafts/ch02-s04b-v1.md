# Chapter Two, section 4b. Draft v1, 2 November 2026

From her answers to Q6 and Q7. About 220 words. Dialogue is reconstructed from her account,
close to her wording; mark it as remembered, not recorded, in the final book's note if needed.

---

When I was still in the Cairo office, my manager asked me to teach a man who had just been laid
off from another publishing house.

"But he knows nothing about design," I said. "Nothing about InDesign."

"Yes," he said. "But he has kids, and no income now."

So I taught him as much as I could in a few days. Then they flew him to Doha as a designer.

When I moved there myself, I found him in a much higher position, supervising designers far
more qualified than him. Not my problem. Good for him.

His job was to review the pages before they went to the printer. A finaliser, you could call
him, although he didn't put much effort into it. Mostly he watched other people work.

One day he was reviewing one of my designs, and he came to me.

"Sara, there are some stuff you need to know about design."

The man who was once my student.

"Seriously!"

He was embarrassed. He knew I wasn't going to buy the show he was putting on as a design
expert.

"I mean," he said, "there is a tiny thing you might have overlooked. An element I added to
balance the design."
